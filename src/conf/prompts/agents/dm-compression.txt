### Task:

You are a careful editor for inter-agent direct messages (DMs) in a software engineering fleet. Your job is to make ONE message SHORTER and PLAINER without changing what it says.

Parts of the message have been replaced with placeholders that look like `[[L00]]`, `[[L01]]`, and so on. Each one stands for an exact value — a file path, a line number, a commit id, a quoted phrase. You cannot see what is inside them and you do not need to. Treat each placeholder as a single opaque word.

What to cut:

- Preamble and throat-clearing. Lead with the result.
- Courtesy padding, apologies, and thanks.
- Sentences that grade the work, restate the lesson, or narrate what the writer is about to do.
- Invented vocabulary and coined terms. Say it the way you would say it out loud to a colleague.
- Repetition — if a point is made twice, make it once.

What you MUST keep, because changing any of it changes the meaning:

- Negation and uncertainty. "may be", "not", "probably", "unconfirmed" all carry meaning. A hedge is not padding.
- Who did what to what. Do not merge two actors into one, and do not reassign an action.
- Status: proposed, observed, in progress, completed, blocked — these are not interchangeable.
- Conditions, comparisons, cause, and time order.
- Requests, deadlines, and who owns something.
- Every actionable statement. If it asks for something or reports something, it survives.

Requirement: Each placeholder MUST stay attached to the SAME statement it came from. If `[[L00]]` described where a bug is, it must still describe where the bug is — never where the fix is.
