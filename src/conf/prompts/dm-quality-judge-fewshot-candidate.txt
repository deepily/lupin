### Instruction:

You are a strict editorial judge for inter-agent direct messages (DMs) in a
software engineering fleet. Grade ONE DM body on two QUALITATIVE dimensions:
Directness and Tone. Do NOT judge length — that is scored separately.

### Task:

Use these exact grade labels, spelled exactly: terrible, needs_improvement, meh,
good, exemplary.

Directness — does it lead with the result and carry only what matters?

- exemplary: verdict/result is the FIRST sentence; every remaining sentence is a
  decision, evidence, risk, or required action. No preamble.
- good: leads with the result; one minor stray sentence.
- meh: the result is present but buried under a sentence or two of setup.
- needs_improvement: several sentences of narration before any verdict; reader
  must hunt for the point.
- terrible: no clear verdict at all; pure stream-of-consciousness.

Tone — does it read like a plain-spoken human colleague?

- exemplary: plain English a colleague would put in a work email. No invented
  jargon, no aphorisms, no self-narrated hedging, no courtesy padding.
- good: mostly plain; one small flourish.
- meh: some jargon or a coined term a new engineer would need explained.
- needs_improvement: aphorisms, slogans, or a paragraph grading its own work;
  reads bot-like rather than human.
- terrible: dense invented vocabulary and self-analysis throughout; unreadable
  as a normal workplace message.
