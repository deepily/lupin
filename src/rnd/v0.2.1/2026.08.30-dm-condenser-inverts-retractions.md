## 4. The fix — marker preservation, and why not the other option

The row offered two routes. **Marker preservation was chosen**, on Mr. Radio's steer:
attaching the source commit's subject line only helps when a claim happens to name an
artifact, and an inverted claim naming nothing would still be undetectable. Detecting this
one cost a single grep purely because it happened to cite `2df3aefb`.

**The rule, and it is the narrowest one that catches the measured case**: a literal the
sender wrote ONLY inside a retraction scope must not appear in the rewrite OUTSIDE one.
Absence stays legal — dropping a retraction wholesale is a lossy summary, which is the
design. Asserting its retracted value as current is not.

**Scope is the LINE, opening at the CLAUSE that carries the marker.** Both word orders
occur in real banners: `USED TO NAME 12 AM – 9 AM` puts the marker first, `12 AM – 9 AM
was wrong` puts it last, so a scope anchored at the marker misses the second. Scope stops
at end-of-line because the replacement value lives in a different paragraph or table row,
and swallowing it would protect the very figure the document is promoting.

**The two sides get different vocabularies, deliberately.** Of the original we ask *is this
a retraction?* — narrow, because the answer protects a value. Of the rewrite we ask *did
the retraction survive?* — generous, because admitting a weaker word can only ever CLEAR a
rewrite that kept a marker, never block one. `CORRECTED` in caps opens a scope; `corrected`
in lower case does not, because "I corrected the test at abc1234" is an ordinary work
sentence and admitting it would make that sha a protected literal.

Also added, same family, not a second design: one `Requirement:` line in
`src/conf/prompts/agents/dm-tutor.txt` telling the model to keep tense and retraction
words. **The guard is the control; the prompt line is a courtesy** — a habit is not a
control, so the refusal is what actually holds.
