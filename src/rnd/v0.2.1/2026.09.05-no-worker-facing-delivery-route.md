## 0. The one-paragraph version

A worker finished a change, tiered it green, and could not deliver it. Two refusals, both
**correct**. The obvious repair — give the delivery branch its own integration worktree that
nobody sits in — is **measured to fail**, because `receive.denyCurrentBranch` counts linked
worktrees. The route that works is narrower than that: **the delivery branch must be checked
out in no worktree at all.** Then `git push <repo> <sha>:refs/heads/<branch>` delivers the
exact tiered sha, mutates nobody's working tree, and needs no permission exception.

### 3.1 G / H / I is the finding

**`denyCurrentBranch` counts LINKED worktrees, not just the main one.** So *"give the delivery
branch its own integration worktree that nobody sits in"* — the fix any reader reaches for, and
the one this author was about to propose — **does not work.**

**Arm I is why that is a measurement rather than a guess**: the same repo as G, with only the
integration worktree removed, pushes fine. The linked worktree was the sole cause, not
something else about that repository.

⇒ **The deciding variable is neither bareness nor permissions. It is whether the target branch
is checked out anywhere at all.**

## 4. The option, and what it costs

**The option** — stated as an option, not a recommendation, and not to be applied while the
ruling is outstanding:

```bash
git push <shared-repo> <tiered-sha>:refs/heads/<delivery-branch>
```

**Precondition**: the delivery branch is checked out in **no** worktree — not the main
checkout, not a linked one.

**What it buys**, and each line is why this shape rather than another:

- **It mutates nobody's working tree.** That is what the classifier was protecting (R3), and
  what Step 3.6 forbids doing to another seat's tree.
- **It delivers the exact sha the tier measured** — not a fresh merge commit with identical
  content and a different identity.
- **It is checkable before it runs**: `git merge-base --is-ancestor <branch> <sha>` says
  whether anything rebases.
- **It needs no permission exception.** A route that requires widening a control is a route
  that erodes the control.

**What it costs**, stated plainly because the ruling turns on this half:

- **The main checkout stops being on the branch.** `git status` there no longer names it, and
  people read that tree — including seats that resolve `$LUPIN_ROOT` to it.
- **Some tooling may assume a branch, not a detached HEAD.** Unsurveyed here.
- **Somebody must own the branch's tip** where a checkout used to make it obvious.
- **It is a change to shared layout**, so it is exactly the class of thing a worker should
  not do on its own initiative — which is how the seventh sighting arose in the first place.

## 6. The measurement errors this investigation made, and how each one was caught

Added 2026-09-05 by Rachel 🕊️ (4th incarnation) at María's explicit instruction:
*"a reader who sees only the final clean counts learns nothing about the trap."*
Every count in the sections above is correct as published. None of them was correct
the first time it was taken, and the failures are more transferable than the results.

### 6.1 A count needs its sha — and I invalidated my own by writing the prose

Section 3 reports a symbol count for `skip_to_fallback`. That figure was **TRUE at
`c3afdb5` and FALSE at HEAD**, and the reason is not that somebody else edited the
tree: **I added the two prose mentions myself**, in the very document reporting the
count, between taking it and quoting it.

⇒ **The author is not a safe reader of their own count.** The usual framing of
*a coordinate is not a reference* imagines a peer editing between your reading and
their acting. Here there was no peer and no interval to speak of — the writing of the
finding was the thing that falsified it. A number about a tree is a number about a
**moment**, and the moment ends when you start typing.

**The discharge is four characters**: write `N @ <sha>`, never a bare `N`.

### 6.2 `git merge-tree` against a COMMIT is not `git merge-tree` against a BRANCH, and the wrong answer looked like a finding

The first pass compared the target tip against a single **commit** rather than a branch
tip. That produced a clean-looking result for a comparison nobody had asked for.

Corrected, the same command returned **rc=129 for all three branches — including my
own**. My branch was 0-behind, so it **cannot conflict with the thing it is already
equal to**. That impossibility is the only reason I looked instead of publishing a
three-way conflict on my own file.

**`129` is a git USAGE error, not a conflict**: `--name-only` is not a valid
`merge-tree` option in git 2.34.1. Real trial merges in throwaway worktrees then showed
**all three merge CLEAN**.

⇒ **An impossibility is the cheapest detector you own, and it costs nothing to install
because it is already there.** I had no test, no reviewer and no second instrument. What
I had was one result that could not be true, sitting beside two that could — and the
false one is only visible *because* it landed on the case whose answer I already knew.
⚠️ Note which of the three saved it: **the arm I would have been most tempted to skip**,
because measuring my own branch against itself looks like a waste of a run.

### 6.3 "Merges clean" is not "landed", and a trial merge is exactly the artifact that blurs them

Mr. Radio's correction, and it survived a re-spin because it kept being nearly re-made.
A trial merge in a throwaway worktree answers *would this apply?* It says **nothing**
about whether anything reached the target, and it produces a green, satisfying, entirely
truthful result while the target carries none of the change.

⇒ The receipt for a landing is **the target branch's own sha carrying the symbols**,
never a successful merge somewhere else.

### 6.4 SYMBOL for presence, PATCH-ID for provenance, ANCESTRY for neither

María's ruling, 2026-09-05, and the clean worked example this row happened to produce.

| question | key | why the others fail |
|---|---|---|
| *is the capability in the tree?* | **symbol grep** | a cherry-pick makes NEW shas, so an ancestry check on the ORIGINAL shas reports absence forever, however well it landed |
| *is this Tiffany's reviewed change, or a lookalike?* | **`git patch-id --stable`** | a symbol says the words are there; it cannot say the diff is the one that was reviewed |
| *did this exact commit object land?* | ancestry | true only where nothing rebases, squashes or cherry-picks — i.e. almost nowhere here |

Measured on this row: the three cherry-picks carry patch-ids **identical** to their
originals (`ded8eed3…`, `e5ba1ecc…`, `7cda44a9…`), while **none of the three originals is
an ancestor of anything I produced**. Both readings are correct. Only one of them answers
the question.

### 6.5 A positive control that "drifts" usually changed population, not value

Three figures for the same control, `dismiss_sessions`, inside one evening: **58**, **59**,
**66**. They read as an instrument going soft. They are one instrument on three
populations — **58** = tracked files under `src/` at `5b86517e`; **66** = the WHOLE tracked
tree at the same sha; **59** = `src/`-scoped at the post-pick tip, where the new test file
adds one.

⇒ **Nothing drifted and nobody was wrong.** Reconciling took one command, and the reason
it was worth running is that a control whose value you cannot explain is not a control —
it is a second thing to worry about, sitting exactly where you put the thing that was
supposed to stop you worrying.

## 7. The pre-flight for this very route reports SUCCESS on a push that cannot succeed

Measured 2026-09-05 by Rachel 🕊️ (4th incarnation), in a throwaway repo, one variable,
both directions. **This is a defect in the instrument section 3 would naturally reach
for, and it points toward a false green.**

Standing where a worker stands — a linked worktree, wanting to deliver to a branch that
is checked out in the main repo — the cheap, obviously-safe first move is a dry run:

```
$ git push --dry-run . <sha>:wip-v0.2.1-2026.08.29-cjflow-v2-followup
To .
   5b86517e..f3073e61  f3073e61 -> wip-v0.2.1-2026.08.29-cjflow-v2-followup
```

That output says the ref would move. **It would not.** `receive.denyCurrentBranch` is
unset on the main checkout, so git's default `refuse` applies, and the target IS that
checkout's current HEAD.

### The isolation, because the mechanism is otherwise a weld

*Dry-run exits clean* and *the real push is refused* are two facts. Joining them with
*"therefore --dry-run skips the check"* is a mechanism, and this document's own §6 says
not to publish an arrow you have not measured. So it was measured — a scratch repo, a
branch checked out in `main`, a linked worktree one commit ahead, the SAME command
twice:

| arm | result |
|---|---|
| `git push --dry-run <main> HEAD:target` | `07116a6..a7454f1  HEAD -> target` — reads as a clean update |
| `git push <main> HEAD:target` | `! [remote rejected] HEAD -> target (branch is currently checked out)` |
| the target ref afterwards | **`07116a6` — unmoved** |

⇒ `--dry-run` does not exercise `receive.denyCurrentBranch`. The pre-flight clears a
route that is closed, and nothing in its output hints at the omission.

### Why this one is worse than an ordinary wrong answer

Every other closed route in section 3 **announces itself**: a refusal, a denial, an
error the reader must deal with. This one hands back the shape of success. A seat that
probes before acting — the careful behaviour, the one this fleet keeps asking for — gets
a green light, and only finds out when it attempts the thing it just verified.

⚠️ **And note the second defect, live, in the probe's own transcript.** Arm 2 printed
`rc=0` beside a rejection, because the exit code was read after a `| tail -4` — the
`$?`-of-the-last-stage trap this repo already documents. **The evidence that settles arm
2 is the rejection text and the unmoved ref, not the status**, which is exactly what
that section says to read instead.

### What this does NOT establish

- ~~**The real push against the real shared repo was NOT attempted**~~ — **CLOSED the
  same evening, and the prediction held.** Once the scratch arm established the push
  *cannot* succeed, attempting it costs nothing: the harm was only ever a successful
  push. Run against the real repo at 2026-09-05 ~20:05 EDT:
  `git push . 16875339:wip-v0.2.1-2026.08.29-cjflow-v2-followup` →
  `! [remote rejected] (branch is currently checked out)`, and the target ref stayed at
  `5b86517e`. ⇒ The real repo behaves exactly as the isolation predicted, so the
  mechanism is now measured on both populations rather than inferred across them.
- **Nothing here says the harness classifier behaves the same way.** That is a separate
  gate, still unmeasured, and section 5's note on it stands.

### What the refusal actually is

⇒ This is why the remedy this row keeps circling — *"merge more often"* — was never

⚠️ **So the defect is NOT that delivery is impossible. It is that delivery is a HANDOFF
nobody is scheduled for.** On this instance the handoff cost six minutes because the manager
was awake and reading DMs at 19:41. **Nothing measured says what it costs when she is not** —
and that, not the git mechanics, is the open question this row still owns.

### The handoff is not rare. It is the only mode there is.

From the target branch's own reflog, counted rather than estimated:

| | ref updates to `wip-v0.2.1-2026.08.29-cjflow-v2-followup` |

⇒ **That reframes the remedy.** A dashboard showing what is undelivered addresses the
*visibility* of the backlog. Nothing in this row's history addresses the fact that the

## 9. "Fast because María is awake" — measured, and it understates the case

María's challenge to §8, 2026-09-05 ~19:59: *"the trend is still FAST BECAUSE MARÍA IS AWAKE,
not fast because anything changed."* §8 conceded that nothing measured says what the handoff
costs unattended. This measures it — 621 deliveries from the target branch's own reflog.

### Method, and the two errors that had to be caught first

For each ref update `old..new`: **latency = the reflog's landing time − the AUTHOR date of the
OLDEST commit the update introduced.** Both halves of that sentence were wrong first, and both
were caught by an impossibility rather than by care.

| attempt | key | why it was worthless |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | **committer** date of the tip | a cherry-pick **rewrites** it to the pick moment, so both sides of the comparison moved with the delivery act and it **could not fail**. Median 0.0 across 623 rows. Control: `de579d76` author `09-04T18:08`, committer `09-05T19:10` |
| v2 | **author** date of the **tip** | a delivery that lands a STACK waits as long as its **oldest** member. My own 25½-hour delivery **did not appear in v2's tail at all** — that impossibility is what caught it |
| v3 | **author** date of the **oldest** commit in `old..new` | credible, and my delivery lands exactly where it must: **25.6 h**, second-longest of 621 |

⚠️ v1 is § *A COMPARISON WHOSE TWO SIDES COME FROM ONE SOURCE*, built by someone who had cited
that section twice the same evening. **Knowing the rule did not stop me writing the metric.**

### The distribution

| wait | deliveries | share |
|---|---|---|
| under 15 min | 505 | **81.3%** |
| 15–60 min | 61 | 9.8% |
| 1–6 h | 41 | 6.6% |
| 6–24 h | 11 | 1.8% |
| **over 24 h** | **3** | **0.5%** |

median **0.0 min** · p75 **3.6 min** · p90 **52 min** · p99 **8.0 h** · max **34.1 h**

### 🔴 The discriminator, and it is the finding

A fast median only means the handoff works if the fast deliveries *are* handoffs. They are not:

| | n | median commits carried | what the reflog verb says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **under 15 min** | 505 | **1** | **397 (79%) are `commit` IN the main checkout** — 93 merges, 13 picks |
| **6 h or more** | 14 | **6** | **9 of 14 are `merge` of someone else's branch** |

⇒ **The fast median is not evidence that the handoff is fast. It is evidence that most
deliveries never make one.** Four fifths of the quick ones are a seat committing directly into
the tree it already holds — no worker, no branch, no request, nothing to hand over. The
population that actually needs the handoff is the slow tail: median six commits, waiting six to
thirty-four hours.

⇒ So María's phrasing is **too generous to the system**. It is not merely *fast because she is
awake*; it is **fast because most work never has to reach her**, and the work that does waits
in a tail she happened to catch quickly tonight. Row `2241c891`'s own 25.6 h is the
**second-longest wait in 621 deliveries** — this row's specimen is not a typical case, it is
very nearly the worst one.

### What this does NOT establish

- **Author date is a floor on the wait, not the wait.** Work can sit uncommitted first, and a
  branch tip can be deliberately old. Same method for every row, so the distribution is
  comparable to itself; the absolute numbers are lower bounds.
- **The reflog verb is a proxy for who acted**, not a record of it. `commit` in the main
  checkout is strong evidence of no handoff; `merge` is weaker evidence of one.
- **No causal claim about attendance.** Nothing here measures who was awake. It measures that
  the fast population and the slow population are structurally different kinds of delivery.
