## 1. The proposal in one sentence

> **Every measurement carries the coordinates that make it falsifiable — and which coordinates those
> are depends on what kind of thing you measured.**

This is **not** *"add a timestamp"*. A timestamp is one coordinate among several, and naming only
that one would repeat the mistake the proposal exists to fix: solving the instance in front of you
and calling it the rule.

---

## 2. What the repo already says, and the exact shape of the gap

CLAUDE.md already carries this discipline **three times — each time for one kind of measurement:**

| Existing rule | Applies to |
|---|---|
| *"Always state the root count and the sha"* | a file population |
| *"A coverage list without the sha it was taken at is not a measurement, it is a rumour with a timestamp"* | git-tracked coverage |
| *"Verify the config in the tree you are about to RUN IN"* | the measuring frame |

**Three correct rules, arrived at three separate times, each after a real incident.** None
generalises, so every new kind of measurement has had to be learned by someone being wrong about it
first.

⚠️ **And all three are about the git tree.** Every one names a sha or a config. **Nothing in the repo
says what to record when the thing you measured is not in git** — a directory, a slot, a pointer, a
lock, a process table, a live fleet.

---

## 3. The receipt — the gap fired on 2026-08-30 and named two people

A census read four memento slots at **~15:36** and found two pointing at stale sessions. It was
published as a finding, amended onto task-store row `8068c65e`, relayed to a second seat, and quoted
for about an hour.

**Both had already been repaired** — one at **15:13:53**, the other at **15:40:12**, four minutes
after the read. Confirmed afterwards to the sha (`13cf9035`, record == mirror).

> **The reading was TRUE when taken and FALSE when circulated, and nothing in it carried the clock a
> reader would have needed to tell the difference.**

⇒ **A filesystem census with no clock reads a repaired state as a broken one.** It did not merely go
stale; it accused two named seats of a condition neither was in.

**The part that argues for a rule rather than more care**: the author was me, and in the same
document I had insisted — correctly, repeatedly — on stating the sha for every git figure. **The
discipline was known, written down, and applied. In the one dimension that had a rule.**

## 4. The proposed rule

| You measured | Record | Why that coordinate |
|---|---|---|
| the git tree | the **sha** | already doctrine |
| coverage | the sha **and the frame** (`source` list, never a `--cov` scope) | a scoped run narrows what was *measured*, invisibly |
| a file count / population | the **root set** and its size | 2,318 and 1,217 were both correct — eight roots against six |
| **mutable local state** — a directory, a slot, a pointer, a lock, a process table | the **wall-clock time of the read** | it can change between the read and the sentence about it |
| a running fleet | the time **and the count** | it is a **distribution**, not a value: *"9 of 11"* is a measurement, *"the fleet is stale"* is not |
| a claim about another seat's state | the time **and** how you read it | it may already have fixed itself — twice on 08-30, it had |

**The operative sentence:**

> **Without its coordinates a measurement is not wrong — it is UNFALSIFIABLE, and that is worse. A
> reader cannot tell whether disagreeing with it means they are mistaken, or merely later.**

A wrong number gets corrected by the next person who measures. **An uncoordinated number cannot be
corrected**, because a second reading that disagrees is not evidence against it — it might be a
different moment, a different root set, a different frame. **The two readers then argue instead of
reconciling.**

---

## 5. Why a rule, and not "be careful"

**It has already been learned three times and it did not transfer.** The coverage rule, the
root-count rule and the frame rule are three instances of one principle, written down separately —
and none of them stopped the 08-30 filesystem census, by an author who had cited two of the three
that same afternoon.

⇒ **A principle stated only as instances must be re-learned per instance.** That is the whole
argument. The three specific rules are correct and none is being replaced.

**Cost**: a few words per measurement.
**What it buys**: CLAUDE.md already sets the goal — *"two counts that differ should be RECONCILED,
not adjudicated"* — without saying what makes reconciliation possible. **This is what makes it
possible.** Reconciliation requires that both readings state what they were readings *of*. Without
coordinates there is nothing to reconcile *against*, and the disagreement can only be settled by
authority.

## 7. What this proposal does NOT claim

- **It does not replace the three existing rules.** They are more specific and remain the operative
  text for their own cases. This sits above them.
- **It is not a claim that the 08-30 census was careless.** It was taken with a command, and its git
  figures were verified in the same document. **That is the point** — care did not transfer across
  the *kind* of thing being measured.
- **It has ONE incident as a receipt, not a pattern**, and the corpus bar is to accumulate a pattern
  before graduating doctrine. What is offered instead: **three rules already in CLAUDE.md are
  instances of it.** The pattern is in the file, unnamed. If that reads as insufficient, the honest
  outcome is to leave this as a research doc and wait — the next incident will arrive in whichever
  dimension nobody has written down yet, which is exactly how the first three arrived.
