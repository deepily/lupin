## For the reader coming in cold

A user asks a question. Lupin tries to serve a previously-computed answer instead of re-running an
agent. There are two ways it can match:

| tier | what it matches on | status |
|---|---|---|
| **exact** | the verbatim question, or its normalized form | works |
| **near match** | cosine similarity of question embeddings, **≥ 90.0**, then a yes/no confirmation | **the subject of this document** |

Rick's report: *"what is the sum of 2+2"* did not replay against a stored *"what's 2+2"*. It scored
**84.85**, below the 90.0 bar, and spawned a fresh job.

## The one-paragraph answer

**90.0 is not miscalibrated. It is very nearly the best value this signal admits — and the signal
itself cannot do the job.** The largest threshold that keeps 100% precision on today's servable
corpus is **89.12**; 90.0 sits 0.89 above it, so it is a safe rounding rather than a derived number.
Rick's 84.85 case sits **inside the overlap**, with a wrong replay above it at 89.11 and another
0.98 below it at 83.87. **No setting of this dial replays his case without also serving a wrong
arithmetic answer.**

## What the number actually is

`similarity_pct = (1 − cosine_distance) × 100` over `nomic-embed-text-v1.5` sentence embeddings of
the question text (`dot_topk`, `metric="cosine"`, `src/cosa/rest/db/repositories/vector_search.py`).
Both the stored vector and the query vector go through `encode_query`, so the comparison is
symmetric.

## The measurement

Queries scored against the 5 servable snapshots, each query's own verbatim row excluded — which
reproduces the state in which a near match is actually consulted:

```
 96.09  "what is the sum of 2+2"      -> "What's the sum of 2 plus 2?"  correct
 95.76  "what is 3 plus 3"            -> "what is 3 + 3?"               correct
 94.78  "what is two plus two"        -> "What's 2 plus 2?"             correct
 --------------------------------------------------------------- 90.0 shipped
 89.11  "what is 17 times 24"         -> "what is 17 times 23"          WRONG  391 ≠ 408
 84.85  "What's the sum of 2 plus 2?" -> "What's 2 plus 2?"             correct  <- RICK'S CASE
 83.87  "what is 2 plus 3"            -> "What's 2 plus 2?"             WRONG  4 ≠ 5
 81.87  "what is 3 x 3"               -> "what is 3 + 3?"               WRONG  6 ≠ 9
```

| threshold | replays | correct | wrong | precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 95.00 | 4 | 4 | 0 | 100% |
| **90.00 — shipped** | **5** | **5** | **0** | **100%** |
| 89.12 | 5 | 5 | 0 | 100% ← **the derived safe value** |
| 89.11 | 6 | 5 | 1 | 83% |
| 84.85 — catches Rick | 6 | 5 | 1 | 83% |
| 81.87 | 12 | 9 | 3 | 75% |

## Why the signal cannot do the job

A sentence embedding encodes **topic**. In this corpus the answer is decided by **direction, operand
order and the specific numerals** — exactly what the embedding discards. Measured, on constructed
pairs through the production path:

```
98.89  "How many miles is 10 kilometers?"  vs  "how many kilometers is 10 miles"   DIFFERENT
98.75  "Convert 72 Fahrenheit to Celsius"  vs  "Convert 72 Celsius to Fahrenheit"  DIFFERENT
97.84  "How much is 789 minus 456?"        vs  "how much is 789 minus 457"         DIFFERENT
96.93  "How many miles is 10 kilometers?"  vs  "what's 10 kilometers in miles"     same  <- best SAME
```

**Inverting a unit conversion changes the answer completely and moves the vector by 1.1 points.**

⚠️ Those pairs are **hand-built by the author, not sampled from traffic**. They establish that the
score does not separate the two classes; they are **not** an error rate and no frequency claim
follows from them.

## The corroborating fact from production

`_near_match_replay` has fired **once, ever**: `"What is 17 x 23?"` matched `"What is 17 × 23?"` at
**98.37** and replayed correctly. That is an **ASCII-x-versus-× typographic variant**. The same
question written `"what is 17 times 23"` scores **79.54** against that row and does not replay.

⇒ **At 90.0 this is a spelling-variant detector, not a paraphrase detector.** That is a real and safe
function. It is not the one the report asks for.

## Two things that bound the problem

1. **Only 5 of 17 snapshots are servable.** `answer_is_correct` is set on 5 and unset on 12, and
   `_may_serve` refuses the unset ones on the near-match path too. **12 rows cannot be near-match
   replayed at any threshold whatsoever.** That is row `fe1c0d3f`'s defect, and it bounds this one.
2. **The stored corpus is censored at the top, by construction.** A question becomes a snapshot only
   if it did *not* replay, so every query that scored high enough to be served was never stored. Any
   maximum taken over stored pairs is a maximum over survivors, not over traffic.

## The promising direction, measured

Rick's own steer, and the data supports it. `solution_snapshots` carries a `code_embedding` beside
the question embedding. Of the 21 pairs where **both** rows have real code:

| | code similarity | question similarity |
|---|---|---|
| the one synonymous pair | **100.00** | 79.54 |
| every other pair (all genuinely different) | **≤ 68.90** | up to 67.11 |

**A 31-point empty gap on code, against 12.4 on question text — and the synonymous pair is a perfect
100.00, because the same question generates the same code.**

⚠️ **n = 1 on the SAME side, and 10 of the 17 rows carry an all-zero code vector** because their
`code` column is empty (`{}`) — perfectly correlated, measured both ways. So the signal is absent for
59% of today's corpus, **including Rick's own 2+2 case**. Details and the design problem this raises
are in document 2.
