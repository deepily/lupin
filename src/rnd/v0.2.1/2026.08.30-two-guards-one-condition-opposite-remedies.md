# Two guards, one condition, opposite remedies

**Date**: 2026-08-30 · **Author**: Tiberius 👑 (reviewer seat) · **Requested by**: Mr Radio 🦉
**Found by**: Tiberius (layer 1), Rachel 🕊️ (layer 2), Maya 🌻 (layer 3)
**Fixed on**: `wt-tiberius-counts-hold` — `c8524ed0`, `90f6c896`, `4ee6d8a4`

## The one-sentence version

Two test guards, each correct and each independently reviewed, landed on the same branch
minutes apart, fired on the **same condition**, and told the reader to do **opposite things** —
and nothing in either change's own review, either merge, or the test tier could have shown it,
because the defect exists only in the pair.

## What each guard says

| guard | landed | on that condition it says |
|---|---|---|
| `ROTATION_HELD_NOTICE` (Maya) | `5f288b18`, 18:45:05 | **do NOT clear this by recording a scan** — the re-scan and triage are done on `wt-maya-4f0ced13` at `034e44ac`, held until the postgres credential is rotated (Rick's call) |
| `test_the_recorded_counts_are_derived_from_the_same_scan` (Tiberius) | `3a96ad03`, 18:46:28 | *"Re-record them from the SAME scan, and re-do the TRIAGE"* |

The second is the refused action, spelled out as an instruction, in the more actionable of the
two messages. **A reader clearing my red the way it asked would have cleared her hold** — and
the hold is the only branch-level signal that the credential work is unfinished.

## Why no per-change control could catch it

Every control we have is scoped to **one change**:

- **Git merged both cleanly.** They touch different regions of one file; there was no conflict
  to adjudicate, and a clean merge reads as evidence of compatibility.
- **Neither file mentions the other.** No grep, no import graph, no reference from either
  commit points at the other.
- **The tier was as expected.** Both reds were *expected* reds. Counting them told you nothing;
  the contradiction is in the message TEXT, which no assertion reads.
- **Both changes passed review — separately.** Each reviewer had a correct change in front of
  them. The defect is not in either diff.

⇒ **A defect that exists only in the pair is invisible to every control that examines one
change at a time.** It surfaced because somebody ran the merged branch and read the two failure
messages **side by side, in one run**.

## It had three layers, and each fix exposed the next

1. **Two TESTS disagreed** — one red carried the hold, the other refused it. Fixed at
   `c8524ed0`: both reds carry the notice.
2. **Then each red disagreed with ITSELF** (Rachel). `_how_to_clear_the_red` ends *"update
   detector_sha256, scanned_ref_sha, scan_fingerprint, the counts and real_findings"*, and the
   notice refusing exactly that sits **seven lines below it in the same message**. Fixed at
   `90f6c896` — the recipe is **gated, not deleted**: it prints when the hold is clear, and a
   short withheld-block naming where to read it when the hold is active. Rachel's framing, and
   the right one: the recipe is correct and becomes correct again the moment rotation lands.
3. **Then the refusal itself was hedged** (Maya). It opened *"IF THIS IS THE DETECTOR/RESCAN
   RED: DO NOT CLEAR IT…"*, and **a reader deciding whether the "if" matches them is a reader
   who can decide it does not**. Fixed at `4ee6d8a4`: unconditional.

**Each fix was correct and each one revealed the next layer.** A contradiction between two
texts does not resolve in one edit, because closing the outer one brings the inner ones into
contact for the first time.

## The same defect then happened in the conversation about the defect

Worth recording because it is the strongest evidence the class is real and not a code smell.
Mr Radio asked for the sha "for merging purposes". I replied with a single-purpose message
reading **"MERGE THIS: wt-tiberius-counts-hold, tip 4ee6d8a4"** — the review precondition I had
stated fifteen minutes earlier was not in it. He declined, correctly: *"I am declining to be the
reviewer of convenience on a branch whose whole subject is instructions that mean the wrong
thing to whoever reads them last."*

**A qualifier dropped between two messages produced exactly the branch's own defect, in the
exchange about fixing it.** Same shape: each message correct when written, the pair wrong, and
the loss invisible to the sender.

reds were contradictory for **roughly forty minutes**. By commit timestamps the two merges are
18:45:05 and 18:46:28 and the first fix is 18:48:00 — about **ninety seconds** on the shared
branch. Both may be right about different things (his window may start when the notice was
written rather than merged), and the contradiction is in fact **still live on the shared branch**
until `4ee6d8a4` merges. Reconciled, not adjudicated.

## Remedies, cheapest first

1. **Read a branch's reds together, once, after the merge.** No tooling. The only control that
   would have caught this, and it did. Cost: one run.
2. **A test that asserts the failure messages do not contradict.** Hard to state in general;
   **easy for the specific pair** — assert that while the hold is active, no failure message in
   the file contains the refused recipe step. That is a real assertion and it would have failed
   at 18:46:28. **Recommended.**
3. **A hold should own its own suppression.** `90f6c896` puts the gate in one helper reading the
   scanner's sha, so a new red that prints the recipe inherits the gate instead of re-opening
   the hole. Generalise: **a hold is a property of the condition, not of the message that
   happened to mention it first.**
4. **Sequencing.** Mr Radio names his own merge ordering as the cause. Worth noting and worth
   discounting — ordering changes *when* the pair collides, never *whether*. A remedy that only
   reorders works until somebody merges in the other order.

## The durable line

**Two guards can be individually correct and jointly wrong when they fire on one condition and
disagree about the remedy.** The reviewable unit for that class of defect is the **branch's
observable behaviour**, not the diff.
