## 1. The question in one line

Every cross-session DM is rewritten by a condenser before the recipient reads it.
**Should messages carrying a review verdict, an approval, a retraction, or a branch/sha claim
skip that rewrite and travel verbatim?**

## 2. Why now, and not as a theory

## 3. The evidence

### 3.1 A verdict was eaten in front of us — the strongest single receipt

Krishna 🦚 reviewed Maya 🌻's commit and sent his verdict. What reached me **argued its way to an
approval and never stated one**: reasoning intact, conclusion absent.

I did not reconstruct it. I asked for the verdict in one word. His reply:

> *"That verdict was in my message — it was the first two words, `APPROVE 0cf9fdd5`, and the
> condenser ate exactly the line that mattered while keeping the reasoning around it."*

**Two things make this the important one.** The loss is **invisible by construction** — a message
that reasons toward an obvious conclusion is indistinguishable from one whose conclusion was
removed. And it was caught **only by a discipline costing a round-trip every time**. Had I merged
on the obvious inference, the merge would have gone in on an approval I invented, which happened
to be correct, and **no trace of the loss would exist**.

### 3.2 A second verdict arrived with its subject removed

Clayton 😎's approval reached me as:

> *"The with 6aca1677 has been approved."*

Two branches were in play. **I could not tell which he meant.** Asked again; he re-sent bare
(`APPROVE 6aca1677`) plus a separate clarification. Cost: two extra round-trips, on the traffic
class where guessing merges the wrong thing.

### 3.3 A review verdict arrived asserting its own opposite

Tiberius 👑's review of Clayton's window test reached me saying, in consecutive sentences, that
the mutations **survive** and that they **fail**.

Both halves were true — **of different tests** (Clayton's fixture, then Tiberius's replacement).
The condenser dropped the change of subject and welded two subjects into one. I acted on neither
reading, asked one question, and he sent the test body as an artifact.

### 3.4 The rate, over a corpus rather than anecdotes

Rio ⚡ measured today's whole DM corpus rather than the cases anyone happened to notice:

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| DMs carrying an **APPROVED** verdict | 33 |
| of those, **delivered no approval at all** | **12** |
| of those, **named no branch or sha** | **10** |

**Roughly a third of approvals arrive without the approval in them.**

### 3.5 A separate, independently real transport loss

Also Rio, from what the harness saved to disk:

- **506** truncated hook payloads, each carrying **exactly one** peer DM
- Sizes over the preview: **10,026 / 13,501 / 91,944 bytes** (min / median / max)
- **7,260,297 bytes** of peer-DM text written to files **instead of to a reader**
- The largest single message lost **98% of itself**

⚠️ **A different defect from the condenser**, flagged so the two are not conflated: the condenser
**rewrites**, the truncation **cuts a tail**. They compound.

Our own `ConfigurationManager` banner led the hook's JSON on every turn and **filled the entire
2 KB preview by itself in 391 of 505 payloads**. Found by Rio, order-dependence caught by

read as partly self-inflicted and now partly repaired.

## 4. What we already did without you

We adopted a **habit**: every verdict travels **bare** — first two words `APPROVE` or `CHANGES`
plus the sha, nothing else — on the reasoning that surrounding prose is what a condenser folds a
verdict into.

**It works, and it is not a control.** Krishna had that exact rule written in his own section and
**was not applying it to his own verdicts** until one was eaten. Clayton sent a verdict with prose
attached an hour after we adopted it. By this fleet's own doctrine — *a rule that depends on
remembering is not installed* — the habit is a mitigation, not a fix.

---

## 5. The options

### Option 1 — Bypass for review-bearing traffic ✅ RECOMMENDED

Verdicts, approvals, retractions and branch/sha claims travel verbatim.

**For**: the failures concentrate in exactly one traffic class, and it is the class where a
dropped clause **merges the wrong code** rather than costing a re-read. Small, bounded surface.

**Against**: somebody must define the class, and a message that *is* review-bearing but is not
*labelled* so still gets condensed. That residual is real and I will not pretend it away — but a
rule with a residual beats a habit with no floor.

### Option 2 — Leave it; rely on the bare-verdict habit

**For**: zero engineering; it demonstrably caught things tonight.
**Against**: §4. It failed on its first live test, twice, including by the person who wrote it.

### Option 3 — Retire the condenser for DMs entirely

**For**: no classification problem at all.
**Against**: gives up the token saving that justified the condenser, and **nobody has measured
that saving**. I will not recommend discarding a benefit whose size I do not know.

---

## 6. Recommendation

**Option 1.**

The argument is not that the condenser is bad. It is that **one traffic class has a different
failure cost from all the others**. A mangled status update costs a re-read. A mangled verdict
merges code nobody approved — and §3.1 shows the loss can be **completely invisible**, because a
well-argued message with its conclusion removed reads exactly like one that never reached a
conclusion.

**The counter-argument I would make in your chair**: we caught every one of these tonight, so the
process is working. My answer is §4 — we caught them with a round-trip-per-verdict discipline
adopted four hours ago by seats who happened to be paying attention, and it had already lapsed
twice by the time I wrote this.
