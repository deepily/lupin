# One symptom, two opposite causes: route ORDERING versus route EXISTENCE

**2026-09-02 · Tiberius 👑 (session `d203e90a`), with Mr. Radio 🦉**
**Receipts**: commit `5ca0c90a` · mutation arms `3f738fcb0c39`, `60a68ab27a6a` · population = the
sixteen guards added 2026-09-01

---

## The finding in one line

**A missing endpoint and an unreachable endpoint look identical from the browser, and until
2026-09-02 every guard we had could only see one of them.**

---

## The symptom does not discriminate

The client returns `null` on **any** non-2xx and the header simply omits its clause. So these two
render the same page:

| cause | what the server does | what the route table says |
|---|---|---|
| **ORDERING** — the route exists and cannot be reached | answers `422` from the *wrong* handler | the path IS registered |
| **EXISTENCE** — the route can be reached and is not there | answers `404` | the path is NOT registered |

They are **opposite** — one is a route too many, one a route too few — and they arrive at the
operator as the same blank space. Rick found the first by looking at the page and asking twice.
Nobody would have found the second that way at all.

⇒ **A symptom that cannot distinguish two opposite causes is not a weak signal. It is an absent
one**, and the remedy is a discriminator, never a better guard on whichever side you noticed first.

---

## Both classes are real, and the boundary is measured — not reasoned

The distinction is **segment count**, and it is worth stating precisely because both of us got it
wrong in opposite directions before checking. Verified by Mr. Radio against **starlette's own
compiled path regex**, not by inspection:

```
/api/tasks/{task_id}   vs   /api/tasks/flow-ratio    ->  MATCHES      ⇒ a real shadow
/api/tasks/{task_id}   vs   /api/tasks/abc/events    ->  NO MATCH     ⇒ not a shadow, ever
```

A one-segment parameter cannot swallow a two-segment sibling. So:

- the **original 422** (`GET /api/tasks/flow-ratio` registered below `/api/tasks/{task_id}`)
  **really was a shadow** — one segment against one segment;
- the **`/events` → `/event` survivor** **cannot be** — two segments against one.

**Both of us had collapsed these into one class.** Mr. Radio framed the second as "the other half of
the shadow defect"; the first draft of this reviewer's own test file would have said so too. The
check that separated them took one regex and thirty seconds, and neither of us had run it.

---

## Why nothing caught the second class

Three guards existed over this surface. **Every one of them asks about ORDERING.**

| guard | its question | why `/events` → `/event` walks past |
|---|---|---|
| `test_no_literal_route_is_shadowed_by_a_parameterised_sibling.py` | is a registered literal UNREACHABLE behind a parameterised one? | correctly does not fire — segment counts differ, nothing is shadowed |
| `test_task_routes_resolve_literal_paths.py` | does a LITERAL path reach its own route? | its hand-kept list holds **literals**, by its own charter |
| `test_flow_ratio_endpoint.py` | does the handler behave? | calls the handler directly, so registration never applies |

⇒ **A parameterised route carrying a suffix is in NONE of those populations.** It cannot be
shadowed by its shorter sibling, and it is not a literal. Rename it and the endpoint vanishes from
the API with three guards green.

**Measured**: the arm survived the entire sixteen-file population. Two-arm proof at sha
`60a68ab27a6a` — the two ordering guards **6 passed**; the new existence guard **2 failed**, naming
the events route.

---

## The fix, and the shape of it

`src/tests/unit/test_documented_task_routes_are_registered.py` asserts that every documented task
route — **literal AND parameterised** — is registered at the path its documentation names.

Three properties, each there for a reason this evening produced:

1. **The expected paths are LITERAL STRINGS, never derived from the router.** An expectation read
   from the source moves with the source and can never fail. That is the sibling defect fixed in the
   same commit: `assert frs.get_window_hours() == frs.FALLBACK_WINDOW_HOURS` compares a result to the
   constant that produced it, so `24 → 48` survived at **100% coverage**.
2. **A positive control** (`test_the_existence_DETECTOR_actually_fires`) — a green list of documented
   routes means nothing unless the check demonstrably fails for an absent one.
3. **The segment-count claim is a TEST, not a comment**
   (`test_the_events_route_is_not_shadowable_by_its_shorter_sibling`), so if starlette's matching
   ever changes, the reader is sent to the shadowing guard rather than this one.

**It caught its own author first.** The route list was wrong on first write — it omitted the `/api`
prefix — and the parametrized cases reddened before the file was ever committed.

---

## What generalises past routes

**Ask what population your guard actually enumerates, then ask what is in neither it nor its
neighbours.** Three guards over one surface can leave a hole not because any is weak, but because
they all ask the same *kind* of question. Ordering was guarded three ways; existence, zero.

**And where two opposite causes share a symptom, name the pair.** Anyone who has only ever seen the
422 will reach for the ordering guard when an endpoint goes quiet, find it green, and conclude the
board is simply empty.

⚠️ **The client's own error handling is what fuses the two**, and it is not a bug in the client:
returning `null` on any non-2xx is reasonable in isolation. It only becomes a diagnostic hazard
because it is the sole thing an operator sees. That is worth remembering before "improve the guards"
— **a discriminating symptom would have been cheaper than either guard.**
