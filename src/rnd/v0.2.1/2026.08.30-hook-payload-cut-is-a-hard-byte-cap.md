# The hook-payload cut is a hard byte cap at both ends — and our own banner is bigger than the window

**Rio ⚡ · 2026-08-30 · row `298af249` · asked by Mr Radio 🦉**

> *"Establish first, before any fix, whether the cut is a hard byte cap or a proportion,
> and whether anything upstream can be made to fit rather than be cut."*

## 1. Verdict

**A hard byte cap, at both ends, and neither end scales with the payload.**

| | measured |
|---|---|
| **cut threshold** | **exactly 10,000 bytes** |
| **preview kept** | **constant 2,048 bytes**, over a 100× size range |
| proportion? | **no** — a 1,019.9 KB payload keeps the same 2,048 bytes as a 9.8 KB one |

**And yes, something upstream can be made to fit — it is ours.** Our own
ConfigurationManager banner leads **507 of 507** saved payloads at a fixed
**2,209 bytes**, which is **larger than the entire 2,048-byte preview**. In the
median truncated payload the reader's whole window is banner; **no DM text is
visible at all.**

## 2. The threshold is 10,000, not 10 KiB — and the 24 rule it out

Population: every `hook-*-stdout.txt` the harness saved under
`~/.claude/projects/-mnt-DATA01-…-lupin/*/tool-results/`, 2026-07-06 → 2026-08-30.
A file exists there **iff** that payload was cut, so the set is the truncated
population exactly.

| | |
|---|---|
| saved payloads | **507** |
| smallest | **10,026 bytes** |
| below 10,000 | **0** |
| between 10,000 and 10,240 | **24 — all cut** |

⇒ The cap is **10,000 bytes**, decimal. Those 24 are the discriminating evidence:
under a 10 KiB (10,240) cap every one of them would have arrived whole. An
inference from "smallest is 10,026" alone could not tell 10,000 from 10,240.

## 3. The preview is constant — this is what rules out a proportion

Across **809** truncation notices in the transcripts the label is **always**
`Preview (first 2KB)` — not one variant. The payloads those notices describe run
**9.8 KB to 1,019.9 KB** (765 notices carrying a size; median 17.2 KB, p75 41.3 KB).

⇒ **Over a 100-fold range the kept portion never grows by one byte.** A
proportional cut would have produced a spread of labels; there is exactly one.
At the top of the range **99.8% of the message is discarded**.

## 4. What can be made to fit: our own banner is the whole window

| | |
|---|---|
| saved payloads **beginning with** the config banner | **507 of 507** |
| banner size — median **and** max | **2,209 bytes** |
| the preview it has to fit inside | **2,048 bytes** |
| banner bytes across the corpus | **884,959 (0.88 MB)** |
| payloads that fall **under 10,000** if the banner goes | **158 of 507 — 31%** |

**Two separate harms, and the first is worse than the arithmetic suggests.**

1. **The banner does not merely crowd the preview — it exceeds it.** At 2,209 > 2,048
   a reader whose payload was cut sees `Using environment variables to instantiate
   configuration manager`, the INI paths, the section list — and then the message ends.
   Not a truncated DM. **Zero DM.**
2. **31% of these were never too big in the first place.** 158 payloads are over
   10,000 only because the banner is in them; without it they arrive whole and uncut.

⇒ The answer to *"can anything upstream be made to fit"* is **yes, and it is the
cheapest possible thing** — output we emit ourselves, that carries no information for
the recipient, sitting in front of the message.

## 5. What this does **NOT** establish

- **The 507 are the CUT population only.** A payload under 10,000 is never saved, so
  this says nothing about how often the banner rides an uncut payload. "507 of 507"
  means *every cut payload led with it*, not *every payload*.
- **The banner fix is NOT yet proven in the wild.** `ad7bab2b` / `69260ea7` landed
  21:14:48; **0 truncations in the 23 minutes since**, against ~5/hour through this
  evening (16:00 → 7, 17:00 → 9, 19:00 → 5, 20:00 → 5). Expected ~2, observed 0.
  **That is a signal to re-check tomorrow, not a result** — 23 minutes cannot
  distinguish a fix from a quiet fleet, and the newest saved payload (21:10, 10,772 B)
  is four minutes before the fix, which is the kind of coincidence that flatters a
  change.
- **The remaining 349** are over the cap on their own merits. The banner is a third of
  the problem by count, not the whole of it.
- **Threshold ownership.** 10,000 and 2,048 are the harness's numbers, measured from
  its behaviour. Nothing here says they are configurable.
