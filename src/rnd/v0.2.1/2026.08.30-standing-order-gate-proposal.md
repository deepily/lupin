# PROPOSAL for Rick — a standing order must reach people at the moment they act

**Date**: 2026-08-30 · **Raised by**: Mr Radio 🦉 · **Written by**: Rachel 🕊️
**Status**: 🔴 **PROPOSAL. NOTHING BUILT.** The decision is Rick's.
**Companions**: post-game `d9e1193c` · `src/rnd/v0.2.1/2026.08.30-never-let-an-instrument-certify-itself.md` (`1d6857fe`)

---

## 1. The argument, leading

> **An order should reach people at the moment they act, not in a file they may never open.**

Rick's no-new-tickets moratorium was issued **10:12 EDT** and is real, in force, and correctly
written down. At **16:28** the fleet manager self-respun. **His memento did not carry it, and neither
did his rehydrated board.** He learned the order existed from a post-game an hour later, in his own
words:

> *"I have created no rows this session, only amended two that already existed, so I am clean — but
> that is luck, not care."*

⇒ **A manager who does not know a moratorium exists cannot comply with it.** He did not fail to read
something; **nothing in the path he actually walked would have told him.** Checked: `task_create` /
`POST /api/tasks` (`src/cosa/rest/routers/tasks.py:663`) has no concept of a moratorium, and the
string appears nowhere under `src/cosa/rest/` or `src/lupin_mcp/`. **The order would have accepted
his row without a murmur.**

**This is the fleet's own `rule-instead-of-mechanism` shape, third instance the same day** — the
other two being misplaced mementos *after* the row about them was filed, and checked-hash drift
*after* the migration. In all three the instruction was correct, present, and changed nothing.

---

## 2. The proposal

**A store-side gate: `task_create` refuses while a moratorium flag is set.**

The gate sits where the actor is present and the intent is live — the same argument that decided the
memento-slot fork the same afternoon: *a guard at write time reaches the one actor who can still do
something about it.*

---

## 3. 🔴 The boundary — a refusal that cannot identify itself is a bug report

**Mr Radio's condition, and it is the load-bearing half:**

> **The gate must say WHOSE order and WHEN it was set** — or the next manager gets a refusal with no
> way to tell a standing order from a defect.

This is not polish. **An unattributed refusal is indistinguishable from a broken store**, and the
rational response to a broken store is to work around it — which is precisely the behaviour the gate
exists to prevent. A seat that cannot tell the two apart will either file through some other door or
stop working, and **neither outcome is the one the order wanted.**

⚠️ **Same defect class as everything else in the companion doc**: an instrument whose output cannot
distinguish the two states it exists to separate. Here the two states are *"the fleet is under a
deliberate order"* and *"the task store is broken."*

**Minimum refusal payload:**

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| **who set it** | tells the reader this is a decision, not a fault |
| **when** | lets a seat judge whether it is current or forgotten |
| **the order's own words** | so a reader can tell whether their write is actually covered by it |
| **where the work should go instead** | today: the local TODO. A refusal with no alternative destination just loses the finding |
| **how to lift it** | so the flag has a visible off-switch and does not become folklore |

---

## 4. The failure mode this proposal introduces, stated rather than discovered later

**A flag nobody can turn off is a silent work-stoppage.** The gate makes the order enforceable; it
also makes *a stale order* enforceable, and a moratorium that outlives its purpose will read exactly
like one that has not. Today's order is a day long by its own terms — *"for the entire day or until I
lift the moratorium"* — so the stale case is not hypothetical.

**Two cheap mitigations, either or both:**
- **An expiry** carried with the flag, so the default is off rather than on.
- **The setter named in every refusal** (already required by §3), so the person who can lift it is
  reachable from the error itself.

⇒ **A gate with no off-switch is the same rule-instead-of-mechanism failure pointed the other way** —
it would then require someone to *remember* to lift it.

---

## 5. What this does NOT propose

- **Not a block on amendments.** Mr Radio amended two existing rows today and was right to; the
  order is about *new* items. `task_amend` and `task_transition` are out of scope.
- **Not a new place for findings.** The order already names the destination — the local TODO — and
  §3 requires the refusal to point there. This proposal changes *enforcement*, not *routing*.
- **Not automatic.** Setting and lifting the flag is Rick's act. Nothing here proposes that any
  session may declare a fleet-wide moratorium.

---

## 6. Why it is worth building rather than remembering

The alternative that costs nothing is **carry it in the memento contract** — a standing-orders
element every re-spin inherits. It should probably be done anyway, and **it is not sufficient**: it
still depends on the writer remembering to record the order, **by the seat least able to know what
it forgot.** That is the failure being fixed, one layer over.

⚠️ **And the honest weakness of this proposal**: it rests on **one near miss**, not a pattern — the
manager complied by luck and no row was wrongly created. What is offered instead of a pattern is
that **the same shape fired three times on the same day** in three unrelated subsystems (§1). If
that reads as insufficient, the honest outcome is to leave this as a research doc and wait for the
instance where somebody does *not* get lucky.
