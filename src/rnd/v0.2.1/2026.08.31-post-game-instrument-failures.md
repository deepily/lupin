## RULINGS TO CARRY

1. **Commit order ≠ liveness order** (Pocholo, doctrine-grade). In a repo whose checked-out branch
   *is* its live tree, merging in the right sequence buys nothing — the writer went live the moment
   it was committed. This is why an ordering correctly reported as "held" still produced a live
   writer against an unmerged reader.
2. **"Green AND reviewed" means the reviewer's word, not the author's report of it.** I held a merge
   through four asks for one word. The reviewer had given substance three times and a verdict zero
   times; inferring the verdict would have been the same substitution I had just refused from the
   author.
3. **Never characterize a denial by trying variations.** Two seats hit permission denials; both
   stopped. I declined to run a denied command from my own seat — a different principal executing it
   routes around the same control, and worse coming from the manager. **The exact denial text
   settled it at zero risk**: "Claude Code auto mode classifier" is the harness, not our hook, which
   exonerated `kill_guard` in one word.
4. **A tool that refuses is worth more than a caller who checks.** The memento writer refused my
   write — *"a record is IMMUTABLE"* — and named `amend` as the remedy plus why raw appending fails.
   That tool was **uninvokable as documented** four hours earlier and Pocholo repaired it mid-session.
5. **An ack is not an execution.** A worker acked "prepare for re-spin" at 50.3% and was at 53.4%
   twenty minutes later — the verb had never fired. Only re-measuring caught it.
