## VERDICT, IN THE ORDER THAT MATTERS

1. **The race is REAL** at the ASGI layer with a zero-gap answer handler. The client
   closes its socket first and the generator still reaches the post-yield statement:
   **11–16 of 30 trials**, stamp landing **+0.24…+0.79 ms after the close**.
2. **It is very likely NOT reachable in production**, and this is me shrinking my own
   headline. The real `/response` handler does a DB write in a thread *between* the
   answer arriving and the generator waking. Modelled at **≥0.5 ms → 0/30**.
3. **The finding that survives all of it, and it is the bigger one: a completed
   `yield` means "written to the transport", never "consumed".** No race required —
   **20/20** stamped in the ordinary path against a client that read nothing.

⇒ Krishna 🦚's conclusion — that only a consumer acknowledgement can distinguish
*served* from *consumed* — is correct, and #3 is the receipt for it **independently
of whether the race is ever reachable.**

## TECHNIQUE — WHY THE PRE-ARMED BYTE

To take connection setup out of the race, the whole `POST /answer` is pre-sent
**except the final byte of the header terminator**. Writing that one byte completes
the request and dispatches the handler. The race is then a single `sendall` against
a single `close`, back-to-back with nothing between them.

A tee on the ASGI `receive` channel timestamps when `http.disconnect` is actually
**delivered**. It only observes what `listen_for_disconnect` already consumes, so it
adds no second consumer.

**The window is under ~200 µs.** At a 0.2 ms gap, close_first is already 0/30.

### The marker order is the whole finding

```
ACK_YIELDED / ANSWER_HANDLER_ENTERED / DISCONNECT_MSG_DELIVERED / AWAIT_RETURNED / RESPONDED_YIELDED
```

The ASGI layer **delivered `http.disconnect` before the await returned**, and the
generator resumed, yielded and stamped anyway. `listen_for_disconnect` had the
message; the cancellation it triggers did not beat the already-scheduled resumption.

### And it is indistinguishable from a real delivery

In **every** stamped race trial: `exc_in_flight=None`, and the marker sequence is
**byte-identical to arm A's drained path**. The `yield` into the closed socket does
**not** raise — no `except Exception` branch fires anywhere.

⇒ **Nothing keyed on the exception in flight can separate a false delivery from a
real one.** A fix in the `finally` that reads `sys.exc_info()` gets this case wrong.

## WHAT ORDERS THE TWO EVENTS — AND TWO HYPOTHESES OF MINE THAT DIED

The predicate is exact: **answer-handler-first 16/30 and stamped 16/30 — the same 16.**

| technique | stamped | close→`ANSWER_HANDLER_ENTERED` | close→`DISCONNECT_MSG_DELIVERED` |
|---|---|---|---|
| pre-armed byte | **16/30** | median 0.424 ms | median **0.355 ms** |
| fresh connection | **0/30** | median 0.367 ms | median **0.081 ms** |

🔴 **HYPOTHESIS 1, FALSIFIED — "connection setup cost puts the fresh technique
outside the window."** Measured from the client's send call to handler entry:
**0.273 ms pre-armed vs 0.246 ms fresh.** Effectively equal. The answer does not
arrive sooner; **the disconnect arrives later** (0.355 vs 0.081 ms), because the
pre-armed socket's pending byte competes in the same epoll batch.

🔴 **HYPOTHESIS 2, FALSIFIED — "a busy event loop widens the window."** It does delay
the disconnect, by 7×, and the stamp still never fires:

| other readable sockets at the close | stamped | close→disconnect median |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0/25 | 0.140 ms |
| 1 | 0/25 | 0.332 ms |
| 4 | 0/25 | 0.460 ms |
| 16 | 0/25 | 0.843 ms |
| 64 | 0/25 | **1.020 ms** (max 7.228 ms) |

Load delays **both** events, so the ordering never flips. Recorded because it is the
obvious next thing to believe and it is wrong.

## 🔴 WHAT SURVIVES — AND IT NEEDS NO RACE AT ALL

At gap 2/4/10 ms the stamp fires **20/20**, before the close, on the ordinary path —
and **the client read nothing.** It closed without a single `recv` after the ack.

⇒ A completed `yield` means the frame reached `send()` — **written to the
transport.** It never means a consumer read it. The stamp is a delivery claim about
bytes that may sit in a receive buffer nobody ever drains.

**This is not the race, it is not rare, and no in-handler check can see it.** Only an
acknowledgement from the consumer can distinguish *served* from *consumed*.

## 🔴 IT CORRUPTS SILENTLY — SO "NEVER OBSERVED IN THE WILD" IS NOT AN ARGUMENT HERE

Added at Mr. Radio 🦉's instruction, and he is right that the doc needed it: **~200 µs
is narrow enough that the next reader will reach for "nobody has ever seen it" as a
reason to ignore it.** That argument is unavailable against this particular defect,
and the reason is measured rather than asserted.

**A race that corrupts silently at 200 µs is worse than one that fails loudly at
200 ms.** This is the first kind.

### What is MEASURED about the failure mode

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| does anything raise? | **no** — the `yield` into the closed socket completes; no `except Exception` branch fires in any trial |
| does the `finally` see anything? | **no** — `exc_in_flight=None`, identical to a real delivery |
| does the marker sequence differ? | **no** — **byte-identical** to the drained control, arm A |
| does the client get the frame? | **no** — it has closed its fd; the bytes are unreadable by anyone |

⇒ **The server records a successful delivery and has no signal, anywhere, that it was
not one.** The stamp means *delivered-to-consumer*; on this path the consumer is gone
and the row says otherwise.

### Why absence of observation proves nothing here

The failure produces **no error, no log line, no differing marker, and no exception**.
So *"we have never seen it"* and *"it has never happened"* are the same sentence — you
cannot distinguish them **by construction**. This repo's own rule:
§ *AN EMPTY RESULT IS TWO DIFFERENT FAILURES WEARING ONE FACE* — an absence looks the
same whether you did the work or not, and here nobody could have done the work,
because there is nothing to look for.

⇒ The honest form of the reassurance is **"the window is ~200 µs and the application's
DB hop appears to close it"**, which is the finding above. It is *not* **"it has never
been observed"**, which is a claim about an unobservable.

### What I did NOT establish, and it is the half that decides severity

**Whether anything downstream recovers the lost answer.** If a re-serve or retry hands
it over on the next poll, a false stamp costs a delay. If nothing re-serves it, the
answer is lost permanently and the asking session waits forever.
