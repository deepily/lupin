# Instrument-failure inventory — the evening of 2026-08-31

**Assembled by Tiberius 👑 at Mr. Radio 🦉's request**, because the strongest thing the evening
produced was living only in DMs.

**THE ONE SHAPE, and every case below is an instance of it:**

> **Two different states shared one representation, and the instrument reported the representation.**

The failure and the success printed the same thing. In every case the reader checked something — an
exit code, a count, a verdict, an empty result — saw what they expected, and carried forward a
conclusion the instrument had never supported.

🔴 **THE DISCRIMINATOR IS ALWAYS THE SAME MOVE, AND IT IS NOT "BE CAREFUL":**
**make the instrument return the OTHER answer.** A reading you have never seen fail is not a
measurement, it is a habit with a number attached.

**Attribution is marked per row** — `MEASURED` means I ran it; `REPORTED` means a peer measured it
and I am recording their result, not re-deriving it.

---

## 1 · A truncated log reads exactly like a clean one — `MEASURED` (mine)

I launched a unit tier and was re-spun mid-run. The log ends at **73%** with two `F` marks and **no
summary line**. Nothing in the file says it was killed; a reader scrolling the tail sees dots.

**What separated the states**: the *absence of the summary line*, not the content. A finished tier
always prints `N passed`. **The presence of output is not evidence of completion.**

⇒ I reported it as *dead, not clean*, and did not name the two failures — with no summary I cannot
say what they were.

## 2 · An empty fetch: wrong population vs empty population — `MEASURED` (mine)

The VM's `origin` is not a server, it is a **file** — a git bundle six days old holding one ref.
`git fetch <our branch>` returned `fatal: couldn't find remote ref`, exit 128.

**What separated the states**: a **positive control** — fetching the one ref the bundle *does* hold
succeeded, exit 0. Without it, "couldn't find remote ref" is equally consistent with a broken fetch
path, a dead remote, or a wrong branch name.

⇒ Same family as the two-database trap already in CLAUDE.md: *an empty answer to a narrowed question
is indistinguishable from a confident negative.*

## 3 · An empty set read as a universal set, in a permission check — `MEASURED` (mine, in review)

`_default_roots()` returns `()` when `LUPIN_ROOT` is unset, and `_in_scope` read an empty root set as
**"everything is in scope"**. A startup shim installed in every interpreter therefore owned the whole
filesystem, stdlib included.

| arm | result |
|---|---|
| `LUPIN_ROOT` set | `converted=0  mode=timestamp` |
| `LUPIN_ROOT` **unset** | `converted=2  mode=checked-hash` |
| `_in_scope('/usr/lib/python3.13/json/decoder.py', ())` | **True** |

**What separated the states**: one variable, flipped, on a package that had nothing to do with the
repo. Reading the docstring would never have found it — the docstring *documented* the empty case as
a feature.

⇒ Fixed **fail-closed**. The general rule is Rio's phrasing and better than mine: **wherever an
ambiguous value grants authority, fail closed** — *"I was given no roots"* must never resolve to
*"I may rewrite anything"*.

⚠️ **The verification needed its own control.** A fix that made *everything* out-of-scope would also
read `converted=0`. The positive control — repo `src` still in scope with `LUPIN_ROOT` set — is what
distinguishes a fix from a disablement.

## 4 · A fixture that cannot discriminate — `MEASURED` (mine)

`test_end_to_end_out_of_scope_writes_are_left_alone` asserted `converted==0` / `timestamp` with roots
pointing at `/nonexistent/elsewhere`. Correct assertions, and **blind**: the *inherited* default scope
`$LUPIN_ROOT/src` **also** excludes `tmp_path`, so a child that silently ignored its roots argument
produced a byte-identical observation.

**What separated the states**: reverting the fixture to its pre-fix form and re-running.

| test | old fixture |
|---|---|
| `…reports_what_it_actually_converted` | **FAILED** — discriminates |
| `…out_of_scope_writes_are_left_alone` | **PASSED** — blind |

⇒ The repair is a **paired arm**: a positive case whose expected result *requires* the argument to
have been honoured. Two arms, one sha: new fixture 2 passed, old fixture failed on the positive row.

⚠️ **Rio found this himself and disclosed it as "no mutation kills"** — and the cause was the
fixture, not the mutation space. **An assertion audit passes a blind fixture clean every time.**

## 5 · A positive control that proves nothing — `MEASURED` (mine, self-caught)

Asked whether my kill-guard carve-out could false-deny a bare `kill <pid>`, I probed with
`killall python` as the positive control. It **ALLOWED** — and I nearly read that as a hole.
`killall` is simply outside that guard's scope.

**What separated the states**: picking a control the instrument is actually supposed to catch —
`kill -9 $(pgrep -f pytest)`, which **DENIED**, naming the listing-consuming rule.

⇒ **A control must be a case the instrument is designed to catch.** Otherwise a null result measures
your choice of control, not the code. Same defect as #4, wearing a reviewer's hat.

## 6 · Two gates, one word "denied" — `MEASURED` (mine) + `REPORTED` (Mr. Radio)

A peer's `kill` was refused and my `lupin-vm.sh run` was refused. Two refusals, one word, and they
looked like they might join: my carve-out landing a false deny.

**What separated the states**: the refusing gate **names itself**. `kill_guard` is a PreToolUse hook
and says so; the other was the Claude Code auto-mode classifier — the harness, not our code.

⇒ Mr. Radio cleared it by reading the classifier's name; I cleared it by measurement, which is
strictly stronger because it rules out **stuck-open** as well as **false-deny**: the guard allows all
four bare single-pid shapes *and* denies the listing-consuming one.

## 7 · A comfortable explanation nobody had tested — `REPORTED` (Rio measured; I endorsed it wrongly)

A test blew a 30-second ceiling during a tier while five peer tiers were live. "Load, not contact"
explained it, and was passed up the chain three times — twice by Mr. Radio, once endorsed by me.

**What separated the states**: Rio re-ran it under `systemd-run --scope` at **CPUQuota
100 / 25 / 10 / 5%**. It passed **every time at 1.15–1.18s**. Starving it does not reproduce the
timeout ⇒ **not CPU-bound** ⇒ load cannot be the mechanism.

⇒ Recorded as **UNEXPLAINED**, ceiling left at 30 (raising it would switch off the sensor). Mr.
Radio's steer for whoever picks it up: 1.15s under a 5% quota then a 25s overrun is **blocking, not
starving** — look at an I/O wait, a lock, or a probe timeout.

⚠️ **My part**: I agreed from the comfortable side. Asking what would falsify it was the reviewer's
job and I did not do it. **A busy box and a slow test co-occurring is a coincidence until someone
shows the mechanism.**

## 8 · A count that measures the wrong thing — `MEASURED` (mine, self-corrected)

Comparing the VM's branch to ours, `rev-list --left-right --count` gave **1280 / 546**, which reads as
"the VM is 1,280 commits of work ahead".

**What separated the states**: a spot check. `stash_guard.py`, landed by a VM-side commit, is
**present on both** lines — the work re-landed across branches, so the commit counts overstate the
content divergence. The honest measure is the **file diff**: 838 files whole-tree, of which 617 are
tests and R&D docs; **95 files of deployed code**.

⇒ I had already sent the 1280 figure before checking it. **Correcting it cost one message; letting it
stand would have priced a deployment wrong.**
