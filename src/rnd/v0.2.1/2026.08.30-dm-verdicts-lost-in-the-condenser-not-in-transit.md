## 7. What I would build, and what I would not

**Not** another content guard — the words were all present in the measured case and it
still misled.

1. **A verdict is its own DM.** Approval, sha, branch, nothing else. Under the trigger by
   construction, so there is nothing to reorder. This is Tiberius's proposal and it is the
   only one of the three that cannot fail quietly.
2. **Never reorder.** Condense in place; a summary may drop a topic but must not promote a
   later one above an earlier one. The measured harm is ordering, not omission.
3. **A speech-act guard** — refuse a rewrite that emits an imperative or interrogative
   label the sender did not write. Narrowest rule that catches §2.2 **and** Maya's
   fabricated directive.

⇒ **(1) is a receipt and (2)–(3) are detectors.** Per the fleet's own doctrine, prefer the
receipt.

## 9. The half that was NOT explained — it was truncated out of the hook payload

§5 said the Krishna `88631dc1` approval arrived as item #78 of a wall and was
*delivered but unreadable*. **That was wrong, and it was too generous to the
channel.** It was never in the recipient's context at all.

**The routing, read out of the listener** (`cc_notification_listener.py:605-630`):
an inbound peer DM is routed by the recipient's liveness — **IDLE** → tmux-inject
to wake the pane; **ACTIVE** → `_buffer_message`, "drained by the next injecting
hook (PreToolUse/PostToolUse/Stop) at a clean tool boundary." Mr. Radio was
mid-turn at 17:36:20, so the approval was buffered as #78 and the whole backlog
was scheduled to arrive as **one hook output**.

**The measurement, using 78 real DM bodies from this repo's own corpus rather
than invented ones:**

| buffered DMs | hook payload |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 830 chars (0.8 KB) |
| 5 | 5,525 chars (5.4 KB) |
| 20 | 18,466 chars (18.0 KB) |
| **78** | **73,428 chars (71.7 KB)** |

**And large hook output does not arrive.** Measured in this session: a
`UserPromptSubmit` hook emitted 11.8 KB and the harness replaced it with
`Output too large (11.8KB). Full output saved to: <path>` plus `Preview (first
2KB)`. At that size a 78-deep drain keeps **the header and about three DM
blocks**; messages four through seventy-eight reach a file on disk that nothing
reads. **The crossover is at 13 buffered DMs** — the payload passes 11.8 KB at
`n=13`.

⇒ **A correctly-addressed, verbatim, uncondensed approval on line one was
genuinely not delivered.** Not condensed, not buried, not misread — cut off
somewhere past the third message of a backlog. `dispatched: true` was still
returned.

### 9.1 What this claim does NOT establish

**The last hop is inferred, and the arithmetic is not.** The 71.7 KB and the
13-message crossover are measured against this repo's real corpus. The
truncation was observed on **`UserPromptSubmit` stdout**, which is not the
`PostToolUse`/`PreToolUse` `additionalContext` the drain actually rides. Whether
that path applies the same cut at the same threshold is **not measured here**,
and the honest test is to drain a >13-message buffer into a live session and read
what arrives. Nothing in this section should be quoted as if that test had been
run.

### 9.2 The backlog header turns out to do more than label the flood

§8 recorded, correctly at the time, that the depth marker only labels the flood.
Under truncation it does something better by accident: **it is line one, so it is
the one thing inside the 2 KB preview.** A reader whose payload was cut still
sees `[Backlog]: 78 messages arrived while this session was busy` — which is the
only surviving evidence that seventy-five messages existed.

That is luck, not design. The header was written to stop a reader skimming a
wall; it happens to be positioned where a truncating harness cannot remove it.
**Worth keeping deliberately now that the reason is known** — and worth saying
out loud that the design argument for putting it first was "a header under a wall
is read too late", not this.
