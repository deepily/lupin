## The three findings worth carrying

**1. A refusal must name a RUNNABLE exit, and there were two cases, not one.**
Pre-stamping works only on a seat's FIRST root write: records are immutable, a second `write`
exits 3, and a self-respun seat keeps its session id — so its second cycle always finds its own
record. `memento_io` says so at `:1981`. A refusal naming only the pre-stamp flag strands the
ordinary repeat cycle. *And the fix for that shipped, for twenty minutes, with two BARE
`memento_io.py` tokens in the refusal string — an exit nobody could run.* The p-is-p corpus
guard caught it. Not me, not review.

**2. The missing half of a guard is the half the incident needed.**
A suite that only proves absence-⇒-timeout agrees enthusiastically with a reap that looked in
the wrong tree. `grep -l "worktree add" test_reap_memento*.py` returned nothing.

**3. When every measurement fits both hypotheses, the instrument is wrong — not the sample size.**
Two seats, one hour, FOUR corpus sweeps on whether a body timestamp was frozen or regenerated.
A corpus cannot separate "frozen" from "regenerated to a value that happens to match". **Two
greps of the generator settled it.** The row had named that read as the gap in its first scope
note; nobody did it, including me, twice, while writing the note saying it was the gap.
