# A guard's REFUSAL is tested. Its REMEDY is executable advice that nothing checks.

## The class in one line

**Every guard here has tests for what it refuses. None had a test for what it tells you to
do instead — and the remedy is the half the reader actually executes.**

Three instances in one evening, in two different guards, found independently. That is a
pattern, not a run of bad luck, which is why it is a page rather than three commit messages.

---

## Why the remedy is the most load-bearing text a guard emits

A deny message is displayed at one specific moment: **a seat has just been refused, and is
now casting about for a substitute.** It has maximum trust — the guard just demonstrated it
knows something the reader did not — and minimum scrutiny, because the reader is mid-task
and looking for the shortest way forward.

⇒ **A wrong line there is not a documentation defect. It is an instruction, delivered at the
moment of maximum compliance.**

## The three instances

| # | guard | the remedy said | why it was wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `stash_guard` | `git checkout <sha> -- <path>` … **"Touches nothing shared, races nothing."** | Flatly false. A path-level checkout overwrites the working-tree copy — a peer's uncommitted work included — and moves no HEAD, so **no reflog entry** and invisible to forensics. |
| 2 | `stash_guard`, my own replacement | *"`cp` from a backup YOU took … only your own copy is **guaranteed** to hold your own bytes"* | An overclaim that implied `cp` is the SAFE form. **The `cp` fixes only which BYTES come back; the write is identical**, so it still reverts a peer's edit made since the backup. |
| 3 | `commit_scope_guard` (Rio) | *"`git commit -F <file>` parses cleanly and gets reviewed."* | **Incomplete in a way that fails open.** Measured: `git commit -F msg.txt -- <paths> <<'EOF'` is *still* unreviewed. It is the **attachment** that defeats the parse, not where the message comes from. |

**Instance 2 is the instructive one, and it is mine.** I wrote it while FIXING instance 1,
having spent the same evening measuring exactly this hazard — and Mr. Radio caught it within
the hour. Knowing the mechanism cold did not stop me writing a remedy that overstated its own
safety. **That is why this needs a mechanical check rather than more care.**

## What makes this class hard to see

**All three remedies were legal, working commands.** Nothing was denied by another guard;
nothing errored. A check that merely asks *"does the recommended command run?"* passes all
three. The defect is that the remedy **recommends a hazard without its caveat**, or states a
safety property the command does not have.

⇒ **Same family as the fixtures elsewhere in this repo that cannot discriminate**: correct-
looking, named for the thing that broke, and blind. And the same family as an assertion that
compares a resolved value against the constant the resolver built it from — it *restates* the
code rather than checking it.

---

## The mechanical check

Extract the backticked commands from each guard's remedy text; if one is **hazard-shaped**, the
remedy must carry its **caveat** nearby.

```python
HAZ    = re.compile( r"git\s+(?:checkout\s+\S*\s*--\s|restore\b)" )
CAVEAT = re.compile( r"overwrit|reflog|last resort|backup", re.I )
```

**Measured across every guard that emits a remedy, at `92445956`:**

| guard | backticked commands | hazard-shaped | caveat present |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stash_guard` | 8 | `git checkout <sha> -- <path>` | **yes** (after the fix) |
| `kill_guard` | 11 | — | n/a |
| `merge_head_guard` | 6 | — | n/a |

**The positive control is history**: run the same check before `30ba7976` and the caveat column
reads **no** for `stash_guard`. The check finds the defect it was written for.

⚠️ **What it CANNOT do, stated so a green is not over-read.** It cannot tell whether advice is
*good* — only whether a remedy names a known hazard while omitting its known caveat. Instance 3
(the heredoc attachment) is **outside** its reach: that remedy named no hazard, it was simply
incomplete. **A guard's remedy still needs a reader; this check removes one specific way of
being wrong, not the general one.**

## What generalises

**Ask of every control you write: is the advice it gives tested, or only the refusal?** The
refusal is the part that feels like the control, so it gets the tests. The remedy is the part
that changes what a person does next.

⚠️ And note where all three were found: **not by review of the guard, but by working on
something else and reading the message the guard emitted.** Instance 1 turned up during a
census of restore commands; instance 3 during Rio's own commit. **Nobody goes looking at
remedy text, which is precisely why it drifts.**
