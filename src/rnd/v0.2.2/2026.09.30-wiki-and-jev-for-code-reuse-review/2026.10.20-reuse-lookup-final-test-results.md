## Today's runs (2026-10-21)

**The vector set moved; the sweep did not.** The daily refresh replaced vector set `4c3ef7ca` with `cbf6776d` at 11:15 EDT (80 rows re-embedded, 8,639 rows in all), and no copy of `4c3ef7ca` is left on disk. So the baseline ran on `4c3ef7ca` and every column run today runs on `cbf6776d`. The full sweep (`p5s-sample`, the reference for recall and verdict agreement) was made last night and was not re-run. The index also moved: the sample step recorded `a54b9ef5…`, and the 30 lookup receipts of `p5st-b1nb3` all carry `4b5914d5…`. Recall and verdict agreement of today's columns therefore compare a lookup on today's vectors and index with a sweep from last night's. How many of the 80 re-embedded rows are among the sweep's picks is not measured on this page.

**A cache replay was found and set aside.** The first B1 no band run, prefix `p5st-b1nb`, finished with 97 requests for 180 searches and 9,008 tokens per need; its canary matched last night's band-off canary on all 30 searches. The Jev call cache is keyed by shape, model, template hash, need text, candidate text and repeat number (`reuse_pack.py`, `replay_key` and `candidate_key`), with no prefix, policy or manifest in it, and it lives in `<data folder>/jev-cache`. A second run of the same needs and entries on the same data folder therefore replays the stored answers, and repeats cannot show wobble if they replay stored draws. That run is excluded from every column. The baseline itself was live: 180 of 180 searches made 3 requests each (Pocholo's count from the per-search counters).

**Method for the columns run today: a fresh data folder per arm.** Each arm has its own folder under `projects-data/lupin/` (`reuse-review-b1nb2` for this column) with an empty `jev-cache`, `receipts` and `call-log`; the vectors, sample, results and query cache are links into `reuse-review`, so results and the sweep stay in one place. A run uses `LUPIN_REUSE_REVIEW_DATA_SUBDIR=<folder name>` together with `--data <folder>`, and also `LUPIN_REUSE_CANDIDATE_VECTORS_DIR` set to the absolute shared `reuse-review/vectors`. The last variable is needed because the :7999 server confines `vectors_dir` under its mounted `reuse-review` root (`resident.py` `_confined`, `LUPIN_REUSE_REVIEW_DATA_ROOT` in `docker-compose.yml`), and a vectors folder under the fresh data folder is refused even as a real copy. `p5st-b1nb2` stopped on that refusal with no spend. For `p5st-b1nb3`, 180 of 180 searches made live calls (540 requests, 3 per search, n429 0, n529 0).

**What this column shows against the baseline.** With the band off, the same arm and the same needs on fresh draws give verdict-only flips of 3 of 30 against 2 of 30, shortlist-id flips of 16 against 14 (fresh-03, 17 and 26 are new, fresh-25 dropped out), and identical recall, verdict agreement, false reuse and false extend. The intervals overlap throughout, so this is the same behaviour on two sets of draws; the differences are inside the noise of 30 needs and are not tested for significance here. The flipped needs of this column were not compared against the 0.3 cut-off as the baseline's were.

## The band columns

**Like-for-like comparison.** `p5st-b1nb3`, `p5st-b1w6` and `p5st-b2w5` ran on one vector set (`cbf6776d`) and one index (`4b5914d5…`), on fresh draws in their own data folders, so they compare with each other. The baseline ran on `4c3ef7ca` and mostly on index `a54b9ef5…` (6 of its 180 searches, all of fresh-30, carry `01f85b09…`), so it does not.

**What the band did to the verdicts.** With the band on, 170 of the 180 searches of B1 and 174 of B2 ended `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE`, and every other one was `NEW`. Neither arm returned a `REUSE` or an `EXTEND` on any search, including the 15 needs that have a duplicate in the index; the no-band run returned `REUSE` on 82 searches. **Verdict agreement of 1 of 10: the band turns answers into `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE`; it does not change which entry wins** (Pocholo, read-only, from the result files, the receipts and the tree at `28ef9f387`). On the 10 sampled needs the lookup's verdict is `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE` for all ten in both band arms, against 8 agreements with the sweep for the no-band run on the same vector set. The one band agreement is fresh-15, where the sweep also says `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE`. Across all 30 lookups: no band `REUSE` 14, `NEW` 4, `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE` 10, `EXTEND` 2; B1 band `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE` 28, `NEW` 2; B2 band `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE` 29, `NEW` 1. In the 30 B1 band receipts the cause `NEAR_CUTOFF` is present in 25 (alone in 17, with `LOW_CONFIDENCE` in 8), `LOW_CONFIDENCE` alone in 3, and none in the 2 `NEW` ones.

*Mechanism.* In `verdict.py`, `decide()` adds the cause `NEAR_CUTOFF` when the policy has a band and `_near_cutoff` finds a clause inside 0.076, and any cause makes the verdict `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE` before the `REUSE` branch is reached. `_near_cutoff` looks at **every answered row**, 128 to 512 entries, not at the winner: an overlap near 0.3 or 0.9 in any one entry sets the cause, and so does a confidence near the 0.9 bar, but the confidence tests apply only to entries whose overlap is at least 0.3 (or at least 0.9). In fresh-07 the clear winners trip it themselves (`write_state` overlap 1.0 and confidence 0.91, `write_once` 0.98 and 0.90). The band was fitted as the 95th percentile of one entry's repeat-to-repeat spread, but `decide()` asks whether any of 128 to 512 entries is inside it. Running the shipped `_near_cutoff` on the rows the receipts keep (at most 14 a need) finds a row inside the band in 25 of 30 B1 band needs. The band also widens the shortlist from 128 to 512 in 25 of the 30 lookups, so the shortlists of the band and no-band runs differ; the winner is not the cause, since in all 14 no-band `REUSE` needs the top entry has an overlap of at least 0.9.

*Not measured:* the full set of answered rows (receipts keep only the top rows), and whether a band applied to the winner alone would restore the verdicts.

**Cost.** A band-on search costs about 3.7 times the tokens and 3.4 times the requests of a band-off search (106,911 against 29,182 tokens for B1 on one vector set). The band widens the lookup's shortlist from K 128 to 512 in 25 of the 30 lookups, so more entries go to the Jev check, which is why the requests per search rise from 3 to about 10.

**Flips.** Verdict-only flips stay at 2 or 3 of 30, inside the interval of the no-band run. The verdicts barely move because they are almost all `UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE`. Shortlist-id flips rose from 16 to 22 (B1) and 21 (B2), with 20 of each keeping the same verdict. The band widens the shortlist, which gives more room for ids to change. That reading is a guess, not a measurement.

**Recall.** 24 of 25 (B1) and 25 of 25 (B2), against 22 of 25 with no band, on the same 7 sampled needs with picks. The intervals overlap (0.805 to 0.993 and 0.867 to 1.000 against 0.700 to 0.958).

**Count note.** The first row of the figures counts the needs whose five repeats (the lookup left out) do not all carry the same final verdict: 3 for B1 (fresh-02, 08, 28) and 2 for B2 (fresh-02, 08), the same rule as the baseline and the no-band column. With the lookup counted as well, B1 band is 4 of 30 (fresh-24 is added) and the others do not change. Clayton's posting on the row also gives a narrower "verdict-only" figure of 1 of 30 for both band arms; it counts something narrower and is not used here.

**Not measured.** How many of the 80 rows re-embedded on 2026-10-21 are among the sweep's picks (the old vector set is gone). Whether B1 and B2 differ: the two band runs differ by a few needs and the intervals overlap throughout.

## Baseline: the flipped needs against the 0.3 cut-off

The verdict policy cuts at an overlap of 0.3; the measured band is 0.076. For each flipped need the table says whether any entry's overlap landed on both sides of 0.3 across its five repeats, and whether the entries that changed its shortlist came within 0.076 of 0.3 in any repeat.

- All 14 flipped needs have an entry on both sides of 0.3 across their repeats. Among the 16 needs that did not flip, 5 do.
- "Any entry within 0.076 of 0.3" does not separate the two groups: 14 of the 16 unflipped needs have one.
- In 8 of the 14 flipped needs the entries that changed the shortlist were within 0.076 of 0.3 (fresh-04, 05, 10, 13, 15, 20, 22, 23).
- In the other 6 (fresh-11, 12, 14, 16, 21, 25) they were not. What moved those shortlists is **not measured**: the stored rows hold overlaps and no confidence, so the 0.9 confidence bar was not checked.
- The two verdict flips are fresh-05 (REUSE and UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE) and fresh-10 (EXTEND and UNCERTAIN_READ_SOURCE).

## What the figures do not say

- Recall and verdict agreement compare the lookup with the full search. The full search is the reference, not the truth, and the sample is 10 needs, 7 of them with picks.
- The fresh file has 15 absent needs, so false reuse rests on 15 cases and its upper bound is 0.204.
- The index sha of the baseline is the one the sample step recorded. Main's index has moved since, so the later columns carry their own index sha, and only columns run on one index are compared with each other.
- The baseline and B1 no band ran with no band in the policy. The band columns carry the band commit `bcae78623`.
- The band columns were run at factor 4 of the driver's estimate for the canary and run ceilings, because the band's extra requests exceeded the default 1.5. Their canaries read 3,327,808 tokens (B1) and 3,148,144 (B2) against 876,782 for the no-band canary.
