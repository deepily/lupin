# Lupin reuse checker: design critique and prioritized fixes (21 Oct 2026)

The architecture is not sound for minimising false "write new". The biggest leak is the judge, not the shortlist. A judge that sees only a signature and one docstring line recognises 51% of known twins (225/438), so even a perfect retriever would put a NEW verdict on about half of real duplicates. Fix the verdict semantics and what the judge sees before spending anything on retrieval. At 8,661 entries, retrieval cost is not a real constraint.

## TL;DR

- **Stop emitting NEW now.** Replace it with `NO_MATCH_FOUND(judged=N, nearest=K)` plus a mandatory grep/read step. My estimate: route A's end-to-end recall on twin-like needs is ≤51%, and route B's is about 35–37% (0.69–0.73 × 0.51, assuming the two leaks are independent; the populations differ). Under your scoring rule, a NEW verdict at that recall is a liability. The original 30 Sep design (full sweep, no NEW without exhaustive judging) was right on architecture. The 14–15 Oct changes swapped the guarded error from false-NEW to false-REUSE without a recorded rationale.
- **The literature agrees with your measurements.** Prose→code retrieval has a real ceiling that grows with task realism. CodeRankEmbed scores 77.9 MRR on CodeSearchNet\[1\] but drops sharply on agentic-style repository retrieval (CORE-Bench).\[2\] LLM clone judges catch Type-1–3 clones well when they read bodies (GPT-4 recall 0.86–0.97), but reach only 0.15–0.33 recall on Type-4 even *with* bodies (Dou et al. 2023; Zhang & Saber 2024).\[3\]\[4\] A signature-only small classifier has no published basis for doing better.
- **Build an evaluation before building more checker.** Steps: (1) a paraphrase-recall probe plus a mined-twin set, about 300 needs with known matches and about 100 without; (2) an unaided Claude Code grep/read baseline on the same needs; (3) a pre-registered continue/stop rule. Vendor evidence on the baseline points both ways. Anthropic dropped RAG for grep; on Latent Space, Boris Cherny said the evidence was "mostly vibes", with "some internal benchmarks". Cursor reports +12.5% QA accuracy from semantic search *on top of* grep.\[5\] Only your own numbers decide it. Separately, ship cheap review-time structural clone detection; it needs no model and catches the Type-1–3 clones that agents produce.

## Key findings

1. **The judge leak (leak 2) is the binding constraint.** 49% of known twins score overlap < 0.3. No retrieval change touches that. The judge reads no code. The published clone-detection results that matter, all of which read bodies, put Type-1/2/3 recall at 0.86–1.0 for GPT-4-class models and Type-4 (semantic) recall at 0.15–0.33.\[4\]
2. **The thresholds were fitted to the wrong objective.** 0.3 was chosen as the most-recall cut subject to false-reuse ≤5%. Under the new rule, false-reuse is the cheap error, so the constraint and the objective are swapped.
3. **NEW is never gated on miss probability.** The shadow audit that would bound leak 1 has zero observations. It is blind to leak 2 by construction, and its miss rate is not the false-NEW rate.
4. **`fetch_similar` throws away the strongest signal.** For code→code similarity, body embeddings and token clone detectors are the established tools. Dou et al. report text-embedding-ada-002 reaching F1 ≈ 0.89 on BigCloneBench clone pairs using bodies.\[3\] `fetch_similar` uses route A with a signature-only judge.
5. **The fleet has a structural blind spot.** The index is rebuilt daily from one tree, so functions written today in sibling worktrees are invisible. RepoReuse (Sep 2026) finds that agents' reuse of *their own recent code* decays across turns.\[6\]\[7\] The fleet equivalent is code from other agents that the checker cannot see.
6. **The cost case for route B is weak.** Route A costs about $0.10 and 8 s per call. At 500 checks/day that is about $50/day, small next to one surviving duplicate's maintenance cost. The $0.001 shortlist buys about $0.099 per call and costs 27–31 points of recall.

### 1C. Judge (Jev)

| # | Issue | Why it matters | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | **Signature + one docstring line, by instruction ("judge only from…").** | The prompt forbids the evidence that decides clone status. | [PS] 51% twin recall. [PR] Zhang & Saber (2024), GPT-4 recall on BigCloneBench *with bodies*: T1 1.00, T2 0.86, VST3 0.94, ST3 0.97, MT3 0.87, WT3/T4 0.23. GPT-3.5: T2 0.56, MT3 0.23, T4 0.07.\[4\] Model capability matters a lot, and even frontier models fail Type-4. |
| J2 | **Small "System One" classifier.** | No published evidence supports small classifiers on Type-3/4 detection without code. Dou et al. (Fudan/Westlake/NTU, arXiv:2308.01191) show small open models degenerate (LLaMA2-7B: T4 "recall" 0.99 at precision 0.51, i.e. it says yes to everything). | [PR] Dou et al. 2023. |
| J3 | **8,200 independent pairwise decisions per question.** | Per-pair error rates compound. If the 14,001 negatives resemble random entries, a 4.7% per-pair false-positive rate implies about 385 "relevant" non-matches per full sweep. NEW would then be rare and the deciding candidate usually wrong. If they are hard negatives, the per-sweep rate is unknown. | J4 | **Overlap and confidence are algebraically coupled.** With confidence = max(P_r, P_e, P_u), any overlap in [0.3, 0.9) forces P_u ≤ 0.7 and P_r, P_e < 0.9, so confidence < 0.9: *every* relevant-but-not-strong candidate is "doubtful" by construction. | The "doubtful" rule is just a second overlap threshold at 0.9 dressed as a confidence check. Only P(reuse) vs P(extend) splitting can add doubt above 0.9. | [I], derivation from [PS] definitions. |
| J5 | **No calibration evidence.** | Thresholds on raw softmax-like probabilities from a small model assume calibration that has not been measured. | [I]. |

### 1D. Verdict logic and thresholds

| # | Issue | Why it matters | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | **NEW = "no judged candidate ≥ 0.3".** It does not condition on unjudged entries (route B), dropped entries (I4), unindexed entries (I3/I5), or judge recall (leak 2). | NEW asserts a universal negative from a partial, lossy search. That is the costly error by definition. | [PS]. |
| V2 | **Uncertainty band bug.** It checks every judged candidate, so with thousands of candidates some score always lands within ±0.076 of a cut and nearly every answer becomes UNCERTAIN. | Turning the band OFF (the current proposal) removes the only mechanism converting near-misses into "not sure". With the band off, a twin at overlap 0.25 with nothing else relevant yields NEW. | [PS]; [I]. The fix is to scope the band, not delete it. |
| V3 | **The deciding candidate is undefined for EXTEND vs REUSE when several are strong.** | This only affects the cheap error, so it is low priority. | [PS]. |
| V4 | **No decision-theoretic basis.** Your rule is cost-asymmetric, which is the classic reject-option setup (Chow 1970) and Neyman–Pearson. | Thresholds should come from minimising expected cost subject to false-NEW ≤ α, not from a false-reuse cap. | Conformal risk control [PR] (Angelopoulos et al., ICLR 2024) gives finite-sample control of an expected monotone loss such as FNR, "tight up to an O(1/n) factor".\[19\] That fits "P(miss | match exists) ≤ α" directly. |

### 2.2 What the evidence supports about the ~70% ceiling

1. **Prose→code at function level is not saturated, but realistic queries are much harder than CodeSearchNet [PR].** CodeRankEmbed: 77.9 MRR on CSN, 60.1 nDCG@10 on CoIR.\[1\] CORE-Bench (2026) shows the same model at 12–27 nDCG@10 on requirement-driven repository retrieval levels.\[15\] Your needs ("a function that does X, which I'm about to write") sit between docstring search and requirement search. So **~70% Recall@128 for prose needs at 8.6k entries is plausible for an implementation without body content or query rewriting. It is not a hard ceiling.** The code-like query result (90% top-10) on your own data is the strongest evidence that query form, not the corpus, is the bottleneck [PS; I].
2. **HyDE-style rewriting is not a guaranteed win.** One 2026 study found HyDE *reduced* single-target Recall@10 from 0.803 to 0.744 while raising pooled recall from 0.920 to 0.943 [PR].\[29\] For you, generate *multiple* hypothetical signatures, union the results, and measure.
3. **Better retrievers help agents, even when agents can search.** BrowseComp-Plus [PR]: swapping BM25 for Qwen3-Embedding-8B raised gpt-5 agent accuracy from 55.9% to 70.12% and cut search calls.\[30\] That is the non-code analogue of Cursor's result. Contrary data point: one secondhand report (ScholarCatalyst, via a GitHub issue) shows agentic search at 0.42 vs plain embedding 0.48 Recall@20, **unverified**.\[31\]
4. **Net [I]:** for "does X already exist?", the evidence supports grep + good semantic search + an agent that reads bodies over any of them alone. No published benchmark measures pre-write duplicate discovery specifically, except RepoReuse (below), so you have to measure it yourself.

### 2.3 Direct evidence on agents finding existing code

- **RepoReuse [PR] (arXiv 2609.35357, Sep 2026):** over 3,000 turns, agents read 83.6% of relevant repo code at turn 1 but 35.4% at turn 5.\[6\] "Even the strongest misses 24.3% of repository targets… although the requirement explicitly lists these functions". "What helps is a compact map of which functions exist and where to find them",\[6\] and full source of history "barely helps". **This is the best available estimate of the unaided baseline. A frontier agent misses at least a quarter of reusable targets even when told they exist.**
- **Böckeler, Codex runs [P]:** in 6 runs on the same task, only 2 found an existing reusable component; 4 "created a new file with a new function, which led to duplicated code".\[32\]

**What reduces duplication (evidence strength):**
1. **Compact "what exists" maps in context:** RepoReuse ablation [PR], Aider repo map [V]. Moderate.
2. **Review-time structural clone detection:** token/AST detectors are mature. SourcererCC [PR]: Type-1 recall 100%, Type-2 97–100%, VST3 93%, ST3 61% (inter-project 48%), precision 83–91% depending on evaluation,\[38\]\[39\]\[40\] and Type-4 ≈ 0. Cheap and deterministic. Blind to semantic clones.
3. **LLM review with bodies:** strong on Type-1–3, weak on Type-4 [PR].\[4\] Expensive per pair, so it should run on a shortlist.
4. **Pre-write lookup tools:** **no peer-reviewed outcome evidence found.** Your checker would be among the first measured instances.
5. **After-the-fact refactoring:** GPT-4o identifies Type-1 clones perfectly, Type-2 at 51.2% and Type-3 at 85.6% [PR, J. Syst. Softw. 2025].\[41\] Feasible as a nightly job, not a gate.

### Target architecture [I]

```
need ─► 3× hypothetical signatures (P2-1)
     ─► Jev full sweep over enriched entries incl. private + live worktrees (P2-2, P2-3)
        cut fitted for recall ≥ 1-α (P1-3)
     ─► frontier reader on top ≤30 bodies (P1-1)
     ─► REUSE X | EXTEND X | NOT_SURE{nearest K, scripted grep} (P0-1)
diff ─► token/AST clone gate + body-embedding fetch_similar (P1-4)
```

### Minimal working examples

**Paraphrase-recall probe (P0-2):**
```python
import random, re
def probe(catalogue, bodies, write_need, check_exists, n=300, seed=0):
    random.seed(seed)
    ids = random.sample(list(bodies), n)
    hits = {"found": 0, "not_sure": 0, "false_new": 0}
    for fid in ids:
        name = catalogue[fid]["name"]
        need = write_need(bodies[fid], banned=re.split(r"_|(?=[A-Z])", name))
        r = check_exists(need)                      # returns verdict, shortlist ids
        if r["verdict"] in ("REUSE", "EXTEND") and fid in r["deciding_ids"]:
            hits["found"] += 1
        elif r["verdict"] == "NEW":
            hits["false_new"] += 1
        else:
            hits["not_sure"] += 1
    ub = 3 / n if hits["false_new"] == 0 else None  # rule of three
    return hits, ub
```

**Neyman–Pearson cut for Jev (P1-3):** choose the largest cut that keeps twin misses ≤ α, with a finite-sample correction.
```python
import numpy as np
def np_cut(twin_overlap, nontwin_overlap, alpha=0.02):
    s = np.sort(np.asarray(twin_overlap))          # overlap of true matches
    n = len(s)
    k = int(np.floor(alpha * (n + 1))) - 1           # conformal-style index
    cut = s[k] if k >= 0 else 0.0                    # ≤ alpha of twins fall below
    fpr = float(np.mean(np.asarray(nontwin_overlap) >= cut))
    return {"cut": float(cut), "twin_miss": float(np.mean(s < cut)),
            "nontwin_flag_rate": fpr}
```
Then send everything ≥ cut to the body reader (P1-1). If that exceeds N=30, the verdict is NOT_SURE.

## Answers to the nine questions

1. **Architecture:** cheap recall → small signature-only judge → fixed cuts is not sound for false-NEW at 8.6k. Keep the full sweep (route A) as the recall stage, add body-reading verification on a small top set, and remove NEW. Don't start over; re-order priorities.
2. **Signature-only judge:** no. Have it read bodies. Jev is acceptable as a *recall filter* with a low NP-fitted cut. Final reuse/extend calls need a frontier model reading code (GPT-4-class: 0.86–0.97 recall on T2–MT3 with bodies; small models degenerate). "No model at all" (token clone detection) is the right tool at review time, not pre-write: there is no code yet to compare.
3. **Should NEW exist?** Not now. Allow it back only as `NEW(bound=x%)` once the P0-2 probe shows a 95% upper bound on false-NEW ≤ α (e.g. α=2% needs about 150 consecutive zero-miss cases; 1% needs about 300). A background full sweep on every NO_MATCH is cheap at $0.10. Do it anyway, with receipts.
4. **Ceiling:** about 70% is mostly an implementation property (prose query, no bodies, single short purpose line). Your own 90% code-like result and Anthropic's contextual-retrieval gains suggest substantial headroom. Realistic requirement-style retrieval is genuinely hard (CORE-Bench), so don't expect >95% from retrieval alone. That is why the sweep stays.
5. **Vs grep/read:** unknown for Lupin. RepoReuse puts unaided frontier agents at ≥24% missed targets even when told they exist.\[6\] Cursor shows semantic search adds to grep.\[5\] Anthropic's contrary claim is internal and unquantified. Run P0-3.
6. **What reduces duplication:** evidence is strongest for (i) compact existence maps in context and (ii) deterministic review-time clone detection, then (iii) LLM review with bodies. Pre-write lookup has no outcome evidence yet.
7. **Measuring false-NEW:** use the P0-2 set (paraphrase probe + mined twins + no-match controls) with an *independent* stronger labeller (frontier LLM reading bodies, 100 human-checked). n=300 with zero misses → ≤1% at 95%; n=150 → ≤2%. Compare routes A/B with McNemar on paired needs.
8. **False-NEW vs "not sure":** set c_miss = expected cost of a surviving duplicate. GitClear's 2025 report (Appendix A9) cites "An Empirical Study on Bug Propagation through Code Cloning" (2023): "a 17-18% rate of propagated bugs lurking in cloned code", and secondary summaries of that study report "57.1% of co-changed cloned code was involved in bugs" [V, secondary]. Assume 1–4 engineer-hours of later divergence and fix work. Set c_read ≈ 2–3 min of agent time plus 10–20k tokens of plan quota, about 0.05 engineer-hours equivalent. Chow's rule then says: abstain whenever P(match exists | evidence) > c_read/c_miss ≈ 1–5%. In practice, any no-match with a candidate above the NP cut should be "not sure". You can tolerate a "not sure" rate of 30–50% of no-match lookups before reads cost more than one prevented duplicate per 20–40 lookups. Measure the actual read time on the P0-2 no-match controls.
9. **Investment and stop rule:** budget about 2 engineer-weeks for P0 + P1. Pre-register on the 400-need set:
   - **Continue** if checker+scripted-grep reaches end-to-end recall ≥ 0.90 on matched needs, beats grep-only by ≥10 points absolute (McNemar p<0.05, about 300 paired needs), and the "not sure" rate on no-match controls is ≤50%.
   - **Stop and rely on Claude Code search plus a review-time clone gate** if the gain over grep-only is <5 points or end-to-end recall stays <0.75.
   - **In between:** keep only P1-4 and the existence map; shelve the pre-write judge.

## Caveats

- Several key results are vendor-reported (Cursor, Voyage, GitClear, Anthropic) on proprietary datasets. Treat them as direction, not magnitude.
- The end-to-end recall estimates (≤51%, 35–37%) multiply leaks measured on different populations. They are illustrative, not measured.
- Clone-detection results are mostly on Java (BigCloneBench). Lupin is Python/TS/JS. Dou et al. report much higher GPT-4 Type-4 recall on Python (0.72) but warn of training-data overlap.\[3\]
- RepoReuse (Sep 2026) and CORE-Bench (2026) are recent arXiv preprints, not yet peer-reviewed.
- The Cherny "vibes" and "outperformed everything" quotes are confirmed in the Latent Space transcript (7 May 2025). Still unverified: Cursor having dropped indexing, and the ScholarCatalyst figure.
- J3 depends on how the 14,001 negatives were sampled; F3 depends on how twins were mined. Both are open questions for the Lupin team.
