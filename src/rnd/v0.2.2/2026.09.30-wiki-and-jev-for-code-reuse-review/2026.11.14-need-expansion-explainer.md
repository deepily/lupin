# Need expansion: rephrasing the request before the reuse search

*Rick asked for this by voice on 14 Nov: "Tell me a little more about that November trial ... I need to understand this idea better." It is written to be read aloud, and it takes the academic view: what the idea is, where it comes from, what we measured, and what we don't yet know.*

## The short version

**Need expansion is already built. It is switched off, and the decision to switch it on is yours.**

- **Built and merged on 5 Nov:** lupin `aa3ab3827`, plus `31cc6df74` for the "protected head" refinement described below.
- **The switch:** `reuse candidate need expansion enabled = false` in `src/conf/lupin-app.ini`.
- **The open item:** lupin's `TODO.md` lists "switching on reuse need expansion" among the items waiting for Rick.

**What it does:** before the reuse search runs, one short model call rewrites the agent's need as three paraphrases and five words likely to appear in the code's names. The search then looks for all of them, not only the original sentence.

**What we measured:**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| 70 needs that were written by a model from the code | the search found 69 of 70, up from 60, with none of the previous 60 lost |
| 27 real requests from agents | all 27 kept their best answer; 4 lower-ranked entries were lost, and more were gained |

**The cost:** one extra model call and four extra embeddings per lookup. The judge still reads the same three packs of 50.

**The main caveat:** every one of those figures was measured with expansions written by Fable. The INI now points the expander at Sonnet, and Sonnet's expansions have not been measured.

## 1. The problem it solves: the search never shows the judge the right code

The reuse lookup works in two stages:

1. **The search** picks 128 candidates out of about 13,000 indexed definitions. It ranks them with three lists, merged into one: words from the code, words from the prose (docstring and purpose line), and a meaning vector.
2. **The judge, Jev,** reads those 128 and says whether any of them already does the job.

The judge almost never loses a real answer, and the search often does. On 1 Nov the lookup found 58 of 70 known answers end to end. Of the 12 it lost, **10 never reached the judge** (plan §15.3). The plan's own summary (§9): "when a need is written without the code's own words, the search misses the exact item about 28% of the time, and the judge almost never does."

Pocholo sorted the 10 misses by cause on 5 Nov (row `09b996d5`):

| Cause | Misses | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary gap | 7 | the need and the code describe the same job in different words |
| Rank cut | 1 | the vector list had it at 51, but the merged ranking pushed it past 128 |
| Twin | 1 | an older copy of the same function was already shortlisted |
| Generic constructor | 1 | a `constructor`, among eleven other constructors |

All ten had a purpose line, and all ten were in the index. These are not missing-data misses. **The search simply didn't recognise the need in the code's own vocabulary.**

## 2. Where the idea comes from

The academic background (cited for orientation; we measured none of it):

- **This is a classic of information retrieval.** Furnas and colleagues called it "the vocabulary problem" in 1987: two people describing the same thing pick the same word less than one time in five. A search that matches only the asker's words inherits that gap.
- **Query expansion is the old remedy.** Rocchio's relevance feedback (1971) and later pseudo-relevance feedback add terms to the query before searching again. Recent work does the same with a language model: Query2doc (Wang and colleagues, 2023) and HyDE (Gao and colleagues, 2022) have a model write what a good answer would look like, then search with that.
- **We already did the other half.** Expansion can be applied to the documents instead of the query; doc2query (Nogueira and colleagues, 2019) writes likely questions for each document. Our **purpose lines** are document-side expansion: one plain-English sentence per definition, so the prose list has words to match. They were worth a lot. With them, planted duplicates reached the 128 in 12 of 13 cases; bare, 5 of 13 (plan §15.6). **Need expansion is the query-side twin.**
- **The merge is reciprocal rank fusion** (Cormack, Clarke and Büttcher, 2009). Each list gives points by position, so lists on different scales can be combined. This matters below, because adding lists changes the vote.

## 3. How it works in our code

1. The agent's need arrives as one sentence.
2. **One text-only model call**, with tools off, sees the need sentence and nothing else: no code, no file, no id. The prompt asks for JSON with "three different wordings of the same description" and "five words likely to appear in the function's name, its parameters or its file path", and it says: "Do not guess a full function name." The prompt lives in `src/lupin_mcp/reuse_needset/expansion_replay.py`.
3. **That makes five queries:** the original, three paraphrases, and the identifier words. The four new texts are embedded, and the embeddings are cached.
4. Each query runs through the same three lists, so **15 lists instead of 3**. All of them are merged by the same reciprocal rank fusion and cut to the same 128.
5. **Protected head (64):** the original need's own top 64 are always kept, and the rest of the 128 are filled from the expanded merge. Section 4 explains why.
6. The expansion is saved in the lookup's receipt, so a replay never calls the model again.
7. A failed or unreadable reply falls back to the plain search and prints one line.

*An illustration, invented for this page and not from the trial.* The need is "stop a repeated reminder from nagging while the user is away". Paraphrases might be "silence the periodic poke during absence" and "mute recurring notifications when idle". The identifier words might be `mute`, `poke`, `heartbeat`, `snooze`, `idle`. The code's real name might be `heartbeat_poke_mute.read_poke_mute`, which the original sentence shares no word with.

## 4. The trials

### Trial 1: the ceiling, 1 November (plan §15.4, run C5)

This was a retrieval-only test: 124 needs, one model call each (Fable), no judge. It asked, "Does the known answer enter the top 128?"

| Group | Readable | Entered before | Entered after | Lifted | Lowered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attribution | 33 of 37 | 22 | 31 | 26 | 5 |
| Control originals | 17 of 17 | 10 | 15 | 10 | 4 |
| Yield | 69 of 70 | 48 | 64 | 42 | 14 |
| **All** | **119 of 124** | **80** | **110** | **78** | **23** |

"Lifted" and "lowered" mean the answer's rank moved up or down, not necessarily in or out of the 128. The plan's own reading: "a measurement of the retrieval ceiling, not a product change."

### Trial 2: the replay against every alternative, 5 November (row `09b996d5`, Pocholo)

This was offline, with zero model calls: saved replies and cached vectors on the 70 yield needs. The baseline reproduced the 1 Nov trace exactly, 60 of 70.

| Change | Found (of 70) | Misses recovered (of 10) | Hits lost (of 60) | Judge packs + extra calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 60 | 0 | 0 | 3 + 0 |
| Shortlist of 192 | 61 | 1 | 0 | 4 + 0 |
| Shortlist of 256 | 61 | 1 | 0 | 6 + 0 |
| Two vectors instead of one (B2) | 63 | 3 | 0 | 3 + 0 |
| Vector list weighted ×3 | 59 | 2 | 3 | 3 + 0 |
| **Need expansion, all five queries** | **69** | **9** | **0** | **3 + 1** |
| Expansion, paraphrases only | 68 | 8 | 0 | 3 + 1 |
| Expansion, identifier words only | 62 | 6 | 4 | 3 + 1 |

Most of the gain comes from the paraphrases. A wider shortlist buys one miss for one to three extra judge packs, and only expansion reaches the misses that sit deep in every list.

Krishna also tested collapsing near-twins to free up slots, and it recovered nothing, with expansion or without it.

The only miss still lost is the generic constructor, which ranks 3,834th in the vector list.

### Trial 3: real requests, 5 November (row `09b996d5`, Pocholo, Cheech's go)

The test set was 28 real `check_exists` lookups from agents (21 Oct to 5 Nov), each of which the judge had already answered with REUSE or EXTEND. There were 222 judged entries in all. The 28 expansion calls used Fable, and the baseline was rebuilt offline.

| Measure | Expansion off | Plain expansion | **Protected head 64** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Judged entries present | 186 | 195 | **200** |
| Needs that kept their best answer (of 27) | 26 | 27 | **27** |
| Entries lost against off | 0 | 9 | **4** |

The first run had one alarming case. A vague lookup about which speaking voice a session uses lost all three of its answers. **On a re-run that did not reproduce:** the best answer stayed and two secondary entries dropped. The cause was crowding, not drift. Those entries were in the plain shortlist only through the original need's word lists. Twelve expansion lists full of generic words ("session", "voice", "load", "file") outvoted the original three. That 4-to-1 vote is why the **protected head** exists: keeping the original need's own top 64 cut the entries lost from 9 to 4 and lost no best answer. It is now an INI key, defaulting to 64.

## 5. What it costs

| | Per lookup |
|---|---|
| Extra model calls | 1. Text-only and bounded; covered by the existing fixed cost on the Max plan |
| Extra embeddings | 4, cached in the query cache |
| Judge packs | unchanged at 3 |
| Added latency | one model call's worth; **not measured live** |

Compare that with widening the shortlist, where every extra 50 candidates is another judge pack per lookup, for at most one recovered miss on this set.

## 6. What we don't know yet

1. **The model changed.** Every expansion measured above came from Fable. Since 13 Nov the expander uses the drafter's model, which is Sonnet (`reuse need drafter model = claude-sonnet-5-5`; "no Fable in any key"). Nobody has measured Sonnet's paraphrases.
2. **The synthetic needs are too clean.** The 70 were written by a model looking at the answer's code, so they lean toward the code's vocabulary. The real-request check covered only 28, and its baseline was rebuilt, not the receipts' own shortlist.
3. **Expansion is not deterministic.** The same need can expand differently twice; the voice lookup showed it. Saving the expansion in the receipt makes a replay stable, but not the first lookup.
4. **Gained entries are not proven good.** The +18 or so entries gained on real requests may be weak matches the judge would reject.
5. **There has been no live end-to-end run** with the switch on and a real model call inside a real lookup.
6. **The one constructor miss** is out of reach for vocabulary. A name like `constructor` needs the class name to carry it.

## 8. Update, 15 November 2026 (Maya, row `88469dd4`): Opus against Fable

The expander got its own model key, `reuse candidate need expansion model`, set to `claude-opus-5-5`. The drafter key stays on Sonnet. Two measurements followed, both with Opus writing the expansions:

|---|---|---|---|

### How to read it

Opus is better at the synthetic needs: 68 of 70 reach the shortlist, against 64 for Fable. On the real requests it recovers a little less and loses a little more than Fable did: 197 against 200 present, and 7 against 4 lost. No best answer was lost in either case. Protected head 64 still cuts the entries lost from 12 to 7, so the refinement keeps its job on Opus. Neither difference is large on 28 lookups, and nobody has checked whether the gained entries are good.

### Three caveats

1. **Fable's 60 to 69 of 70 was not reproduced.** It came from Pocholo's offline replay, and the script is gone. The yield row of Trial 1 above is the nearest re-run.
2. **The index moved.** The corpus and the vectors are not the ones of 5 Nov. The off column reads 186 in both runs, on different indexes.
3. **Two best answers are missing in every arm.** Under the live index, 2 of the 28 best answers are not in the shortlist with expansion off, and no arm recovers them. Fable's "27 of 27" dropped one query the record does not name, so this run scored all 28.

### The ruling

Rick: "Let's flip using the protective head of 64." The flip is one INI line, `reuse candidate need expansion enabled = true`. It lands after the model-key commits, so the switch never runs on the Sonnet drafter model. Undoing it is the same line set back to `false`, which returns the search to byte-identical behaviour.

### Still not known

There has been no live end-to-end run with the switch on and a real model call inside a real lookup. The first live lookups after the flip are that run.
