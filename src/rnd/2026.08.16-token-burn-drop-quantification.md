## Verdict first

The only durable, dated, bounce-surviving instrument on disk is the **DM traffic corpus**, and it measures **one channel — direct messages between agents — not total token burn**. On that channel, mean DM length fell **~15%** from the pre-tutor week to the tutor-era week (123.2 → 104.2 words/DM), but the signal is **noisy and non-monotonic**: it bottomed at 89 words on 08-14 and had climbed back to 120 — essentially the early-window level — by 08-16 (a real shift in both mean and median, but carried by a few personas, not a fleet-wide return to verbosity — see the rebound section).

**Rick's two headline numbers cannot be reproduced from any durable source**, and this is not a gap I can close with more digging:

- The **80% → 50% → 30% "share of tokens through the MCP server"** is dominated by `notify()` spoken/TTS payloads on every closing turn. **Nothing on the notify path is logged to disk.** Only DMs are corpus-logged. The ratio has no denominator on disk and no numerator either.
- The **~7,000 tok/min at 11 agents** came from a third-party aggregator that counts every Claude Code call's full model context. That data lives in an external tool, not in this repo.

And a limitation that outranks all of the above: **the DM corpus was created 2026-08-02, one day *after* the KISS/brevity mandate landed (~08-01).** There is **no pre-KISS DM data anywhere.** The single largest intervention has **no measurable "before"** on the only instrument we have.

So the honest answer is a **well-argued "cannot yet be measured as framed"** plus one real, narrow sub-number — exactly the outcome the row said it would accept over a manufactured figure.

## The window and the denominator

**Window**: 2026-08-02 → 2026-08-16 (the full span the corpus covers).

**Denominator chosen**: **per-DM word count**, from the DM traffic corpus, and I say why explicitly:

- `est_tokens` is present on only **2,019 of 6,234 rows** and is **absent on exactly the recent low-burn days** (08-14, 08-15, 08-16) — it was populated during the arm experiment (08-04→08-13) and stopped. Unusable as a spine.
- `len_grade` **saturates at −2** (caution 5): a 251-word DM and a 1,000-word DM both score −2, so a mean of it hits a silent floor and stops answering "are DMs getting longer/shorter?"
- **`words` is present on 100% of rows** (6,234/6,234) and does not saturate. It is the only clean, complete, durable length signal. Where the row spec calls for `avg_overage`, raw `words` is strictly better — same non-saturating property, zero missing values, no target-guessing.

**What this denominator is NOT**: it is not "all tokens." It is DM-channel words. It says nothing about `notify()`, `ask_*`, spoken TTS, or the per-turn model context the aggregator counts. Rick's 80/50/30 is a fraction of *that* larger, unrecorded total — a different denominator entirely, which is why it will never line up with this one.

**Corpus paths** (durable, outside the repo, survive `:7999` bounces):
- Live: `projects-data/lupin/dm-corpus/dm_traffic.jsonl` (6,234 rows, 08-02 → 08-16)
- Retired pre-move copy: `src/tmp/dm_traffic.jsonl.retired-2026.08.13` (same 08-02 start — does **not** extend the baseline earlier)

The in-memory audits (`_dm_quality_audit`, `_dm_length_audit` in `src/cosa/rest/routers/dm.py`) stamp `since` at module import (lines 615, 240) and zero on every bounce — caution 4. **They were not used.** Every number here comes from the append-only corpus, which accumulates across boots.

## The 08-16 rebound: real, but concentrated — not a fleet-wide regression

Mr. Radio asked the right follow-up: is the climb back to 120 words on 08-16 a real change in how people write, or an artifact of the sender mix or a few long DMs dragging the mean? The corpus can settle this, and the answer is **"real but concentrated."**

**Median moves with the mean.** Daily medians: 08-14 = 87, 08-15 = 92, 08-16 = 110. The center shifted up, not just the tail — so it is **not** a handful of long DMs dragging an otherwise-flat mean. p90 also rose (134 → 214), but the median rising rules out a tail-only story.

**It is NOT a sender-mix artifact.** Splitting 08-16's DMs by whether the sender was also active 08-15: returning senders (n=1,041) median **110**; the 4 new senders (n=90) median **114**. Newcomers wrote essentially the same length as the regulars — the mix change did not inflate the day.

**But it is NOT a broad behavioral regression either.** Of the 14 personas active on both 08-15 and 08-16, **7 wrote longer, 7 wrote shorter** — a coin-flip, not a system-wide drift. The day's rise is carried by a few specific senders writing markedly longer on 08-16: an unnamed sender (134 → 274 median), Krishna (62 → 140), mr radio (72 → 138), Cheech (83 → 136).

**Supportable statement**: the 08-16 rebound is a genuine upward shift in central tendency (mean and median agree), driven by a small number of personas rather than a fleet-wide return to verbosity, and it is not explained by who was active. What it means for *token burn* still cannot be stated, because none of this is tokens — it is DM words on one channel, and the notify/spoken path (the bulk of MCP traffic) remains unlogged.

## What the data CAN and CANNOT attribute

**This is the required part, not a hedge.** The tempting write-up shows the −15% and lets the reader infer the brevity mandate or the DM tutor caused it. The data does not support that inference, for four independent reasons.

**CAN say:**
- DM-channel mean length is **~15% lower** in the tutor-era week than the early week — a real, durable, dated number on the DM channel.
- 08-13 and 08-14 were **genuine local troughs** (93.8, 89.0 words) — the lowest daily means in the window. Rick's "lowest day I've seen" is corroborated *for the DM channel, that day.*

**CANNOT say:**

1. **Cannot attribute to any single arm (caution 3).** Three interventions overlap: KISS/brevity (~08-01), the slim per-turn rider, and the DM tutor (live 08-11). All land inside the same two-week window; the corpus has no arm-isolated control period for length. The `effective_arm` label separates the *rejection experiment* (signal_only/blind/rejecting), not the *brevity* interventions.

2. **Cannot show the KISS effect at all.** The corpus begins 08-02, **after** KISS. There is no pre-KISS DM data. The largest arm's "before" was never recorded.

3. **Cannot call it a sustained decline.** By 08-16 mean length was back to 120.3 words — the early-window level. The dip is a wobble around a noisy mean (08-05 spiked to 158.9 with no intervention), not a step-change. Whatever produced the 08-13/14 trough had reverted two days later.

4. **Cannot reproduce Rick's two headline numbers.** The 80/50/30 MCP share and the 7k tok/min are `notify()`-and-fleet-level quantities. The notify path writes **no durable log**; the aggregator data is external. Neither has a source on disk to recompute from.

**The failure mode this section exists to prevent**: a measurement that is correct about the narrow thing it examined (DM length dipped ~15%) and silent about the thing that mattered (total burn, the MCP share, the causal arm). The −15% is true and it is small, one-channel, non-monotonic, and un-attributable. It is not evidence that any intervention "cut token burn."

## A second durable source: MCP call *counts* (not tokens)

`io/claude_code_hooks/logs/hook-events.jsonl` (268k events, reaching back to **2026-07-07** — a genuine pre-KISS baseline) records every notify/ask/hook event with a timestamp but **no token field**. It answers "how many MCP calls?" not "how many tokens?" Daily `notify` counts:

| period | notify/day (range) | reading |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-KISS (07-31, 08-01) | 189, 260 | — |
| Post-KISS (08-03 … 08-16) | 309, 204, 169, … 264, 242 | **no drop in call volume** |

This is the mechanism point, and it matters for attribution: **the brevity mandate shrinks tokens *per message*; it does not cut the *number* of messages.** notify volume after KISS is the same or higher (309 on 08-03, 303 on 08-13). So any burn reduction has to show up as **shorter messages** — which is exactly what the DM `words` field measures (−15%) and what a call-count log can never see. Neither channel logs tokens, so the size of the per-message shrink in *tokens* remains an estimate (chars ÷ 4 at best).

Caveat: `ask_*` counts are single-digit and noisy; `events/session` is dominated by session churn, not burn. Only the `notify` call-count trend is clean enough to cite.

## What would make Rick's numbers measurable

Named so the gap is a work item, not a shrug:

1. **A durable notify/ask token log.** The DM corpus proved the pattern (append-only JSONL under `fleet_data_root()`, survives bounces). Apply the same to the notify/spoken path and the 80/50/30 "MCP share" gets a real numerator + denominator.
2. **Capture the aggregator series.** The 7k-vs-14k-22k tok/min lives in a third-party tool; nothing periodically snapshots it to disk. A scheduled pull would make fleet burn-rate a measured series.
3. **An arm-isolation window.** To attribute length to the tutor, run one arm alone for a bounded, logged period. Everything landing at once is why one partial day couldn't separate them — and why four days still can't.
