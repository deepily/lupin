# What Lupin should learn from a qualified yes
**Research review and proposed experiment · 21 November 2026 · Prepared for Rick and the Lupin AF team**

## Decision

Rebuild the answer contract and the experiment. Keep the decision to stop the original Layer 2 work. The archive supports a better question: **given exactly what Lupin could see when it asked, does a memory of Rick’s prior decisions improve its forecast of what he authorizes, including the conditions that change the next action?** Test whether abstracting that memory into principles adds value over retrieving the original episodes.

Keep the complete human response as evidence, even when interpreting it is difficult. Distinguish its literal form, its operational meaning, and whether the evidence warrants a persistent preference. “Yes, but leave configuration alone” earns stance credit for “yes.” A proxy taking that answer as permission to change configuration has made a material error. “Show me the diff first” may reveal a prerequisite on this card. One instance gives weak evidence that Rick wants a diff before *every* change.

The re-spin example deserves a more precise diagnosis than treating every yes as a contradiction. “Ask me before re-spinning” is a rule about **who decides**. “Yes, re-spin this agent now” answers **what to do once asked**. These can both be true. Neither the single historical no nor the later yeses establish Rick’s preference for what should happen unattended. Of 23 evidence rows for the first rule on re-spin cards, the paper reports 19 yeses and 16 of those labeled support. Re-annotate their applicability by decision stage before interpreting the labels. The observed damage is clear: adding the principles changed Haiku from 123/127 correct to 82/127. The contribution of extraction, evidence updates, applicability and model interpretation remains to be isolated.

These studies give plausible mechanisms in both directions. None establishes a winning architecture for one real owner answering correlated software-workflow cards. Lupin’s unusually valuable evidence is the temporal sequence of actual asks, written reasons and outcomes. A chosen option is conditional on the options offered and their presentation. Treating it as a strict pairwise preference over every alternative would manufacture labels, particularly when the owner asked for information or approved several choices.

## One envelope, two native answer types

Retain three linked records. **Observation** stores the verbatim card, stable field and option IDs and displayed order where available, input route, raw final response, actor, event times, and forecast exposure and draft. **Meaning** stores an annotated, versioned interpretation with supporting text spans and uncertainty. **Forecast** uses the same semantic fields plus probabilities, generated gist and model abstention. Its record is immutable at ask time. An annotation of the actual answer belongs downstream of that boundary.

| Field | Yes/no card | Multiple-choice card |
|---|---|---|
| Native answer | yes, no, neither, or text without explicit stance | For each field, a set of selected or semantically endorsed option IDs, explicit empty selection where legal, and/or write-in text. Preserve which options were merely referenced. |
| Conversational act | Approve, decline, challenge premise, request information, defer, propose alternative, etc. | Same vocabulary. Allow co-occurrence and dependencies, such as requesting context before a decision. |
| Conditions and requested changes | A list of propositions with scope, polarity, time/order and modality. Mark which affect execution. | Attach propositions to option, field, or whole card. Preserve qualifiers modifying more than one field. |
| Recommendation alignment | Derive only when an identifiable proposition and the response establish its relationship. | Derive per option/field from the mapping. Preserve explicit endorsement, conditional endorsement, disagreement, indeterminate, and no recommendation. |
| Unknowns and abstentions | No explicit stance differs from neither. Ambiguous meaning can remain unresolved. | An original write-in, cancellation, absent field, unreadable payload, and an uncertain annotator are distinct. Record model abstention independently of every owner outcome. |

Forecast comment *presence* separately from a *material constraint*. A long explanation can leave the action unchanged. A two-word “after backup” can change whether action is authorized now. The representation should support giving a set of plausible acts or abstaining on the gist while still forecasting a stance. Avoid emitting a dozen speculative caveats just to improve coverage.

**Recommended scoring examples** (illustrative, not archive quotes except where noted in the brief):

| Owner answer | Forecast | Base choice | Complete actionable answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typed “Go with the staged rollout,” where that uniquely paraphrases B | B | Full B credit | Pass if no additional material term |
| “B, but only for staging” | B | Full anchor credit | Fail. The environment restriction is missing |
| “Do B after I review the diff” | “Execute B now” | B identified | Fail. Premature execution is a material contradiction |
| “Show me the diff and then I’ll choose” | B | No endorsement to credit | Fail. The immediate act is a request for information |
| “B for staging and A for production” | B everywhere | Partial diagnostic overlap by component | Fail on whole-card action |
| “Neither, rollback already finished” | “Neither, rollback is risky” | Correct stance | Incorrect reason. Score content separately and assess whether the reason changes the next action |

A pure paraphrase gets full semantic selection credit. A material modification earns anchor credit and separate condition credit. If the alteration negates a defining part of the option, annotate an alternative that references the option. Use no universal half-credit coefficient. The 40-card hand read found 11/40 recommended or modified-recommended and 17/40 reframe/context/defer. It justifies both labels. It cannot establish that most typed answers are recommended-option paraphrases. Use all 473 typed-containing cards reported across the 1,157 multiple-choice cards when reconstructing labels. The 437 are a subset of the 528 literal departures on fully recommended cards. The paper separately reports 524 typed answers at the question level in a different denominator. Keep those units in every table.

## What to score and how to trust the judge

1. **Primary forecast endpoint.** On the prespecified population of owner-answered eligible cards, use paired difference in the proportion whose forecast gives the same immediate action or conversational act *and* preserves every material restriction or prerequisite, without material invention. On a multi-field card require all action-bearing fields to pass. A forecast withholding a guess gets no success on this all-card metric. Publish coverage and the conditional pass rate alongside it. Operationalize each criterion with reference spans and blinded adjudication. Settle the rubric and test human reproducibility before freezing this as a confirmatory endpoint. If it fails validation, the honest deliverable is an annotation study and a structured-action forecasting result.
2. **Decomposition.** Report stance/field exact match, field set overlap, whole-card exact, comment presence, material-condition detection, per-act precision/recall, and a confusion matrix with counts. Atoms extracted from the human text measure recall; atoms asserted by the forecast measure precision. Flag contradicted atoms separately from omissions and unsupported additions. Report agreement on literal UI behavior only for the UI objective. Macro-average fields within card before averaging cards. Publish results for empty-atom references and forecasts so generic answers cannot hide behind an undefined denominator.
3. **Probabilistic quality.** Collect a normalized probability vector for mutually exclusive native stances, marginals for multi-label acts and set membership, and probabilities of material qualification and whole-card correctness. Use multiclass or binary Brier scores where appropriate, observed calibration by broad confidence bands, and recall/precision for neither at a frozen threshold. Marginals alone do not give a joint probability that all fields are correct. Preserve the old stance-only score as a bridge. [Gneiting and Raftery, strictly proper scoring rules](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)
4. **Proxy policy.** Compare material errors per auto-answered card at the same coverage, including false permission, missed prerequisite, and unwarranted refusal. Report counts per all eligible asks too. A shadow forecast of a clarification can be perfectly correct while the proper proxy action is to send the actual question to Rick. Freeze the confidence threshold on development data. Choose acceptable risk with Rick before authorizing an acting proxy. [Geifman and El-Yaniv, selective prediction](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a)

**Gold labels.** Build references before showing annotators any predictions. Start with a development sample spanning both UIs, multi-select, typed paraphrases, negation, questions, deferral, missing context and cancellation. Two independent readers map fields, cite supporting spans, identify action-critical atoms and record ambiguity. Use Rick for disputed intent and a random sample of agreements, ideally by short voice batches. Preserve original disagreements, adjudication and schema version. Consult the original card and ask-time context. Later recollection can clarify interpretation but should not retroactively make private facts foreseeable. Report the unresolved fraction and best/worst-case bounds on the paired result. Freeze labels for an analysis version with a correction log.

**Judge.** Use an LLM for candidate mappings and proposition comparisons. Start from atomic scoring, then add bidirectional coverage and contradiction checks. For validation, hold out a stratified probability sample of forecast–answer pairs from all arms, including apparently easy cases and forecasts with invented constraints on plain answers. Blind arm, principle, and model identity. Score predictions separately against the reference; randomize display order in any head-to-head audit. Challenge the rubric with minimal pairs changing “before” to “after,” staging to production, “must” to “may,” or negating permission. Compare judge-versus-human and human-versus-human, publishing sensitivity to material errors, specificity, class counts, intervals and arm-specific bias. A single overall agreement can be dominated by plain yeses. [FActScore](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.741/) · [Zheng et al., judge bias](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/91f18a1287b398d378ef22505bf41832-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html)

Use separate calibration and sealed validation sets. For the primary arm difference, use fully human adjudication where affordable, or estimate the archive mean of the paired judge difference **plus** the probability-weighted human-minus-judge paired residual from the audit. Carry sampling and temporal uncertainty into the interval. Stratify on card type, predicted materiality and arm disagreement, with a nonzero inclusion probability everywhere. Revalidate after changing rubric or judge. If bias uncertainty could reverse the deployment decision, collect more human labels or report it as unresolved. [Fisch et al., stratified prediction-powered inference](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c9fcd02e6445c7dfbad6986abee53d0d-Abstract-Conference.html) · [Chen et al., ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/chen26cq.html)

## Experiment that can identify the value of principles

**Use the known archive as development and diagnostic data.** Its outcomes have already guided the new ontology and the re-spin fix. A blinded relabeling makes a fair retrospective comparison possible; it cannot restore an untouched confirmatory sample. Freeze the revised learner, baselines, labels, judge, thresholds, minimum worthwhile gain and stop rule before a new contiguous shadow window. Keep new forecasts hidden for the unaided target. Case-mix drift, UI revisions, reports from the owner, and outcomes from a displayed forecast belong in the ledger.

| Arm | What it isolates |
|---|---|
| R0. Original fixed rules, plus the strongest learned model-free baseline chosen on development | Always-yes/recommended-first and an online smoothed prior by native type, question family and recommendation. Back off when sparse. Explicitly model no-comment and qualification rates. Prevent test-set selection among rules. |
| A. Plain Haiku with the full output space | Value of card plus identical short session context. Same schema, valid actions, parser and retry budget as the memory arms. |
| B. Haiku with retrieved earlier complete episodes | Value of seeing historical evidence directly. Give it the same accessible history horizon and a matched evidence-token budget. This is the essential missing comparator. |
| C. Haiku with learned principles | Total effect of abstraction plus its retrieval pipeline. Compare C versus A for incremental personalized memory, C versus B for whether compression earns its keep. |
| Diagnostic arms | On development only: supply principles and episodes from the same source IDs to distinguish selection from compression; remove only historical qualifiers to test their incremental signal; supply an adjudicated applicable principle to distinguish retrieval failure from downstream misuse. Measure cost and latency for each. |

Choose **one** confirmatory contrast for the claim being made. For “principles add value over directly retrieving history,” use C minus B on complete-action accuracy. C minus A replicates the earlier product question; R0 is an adoption comparator. If C beats A but ties B, history helped and abstraction has not demonstrated superior accuracy. If C ties B at materially lower token cost, a separately frozen noninferiority margin and cost target could still support compression. Freeze whether the margin is absolute percentage points and the population to which it applies.

**Temporal protocol.** For each ask, freeze its rendered card, session context, available model and store snapshot. Commit all arms’ predictions before releasing the outcome. Admit an earlier card’s feedback only after actual response **and** extraction availability. Overlapping asks must not learn each other’s pending answers. Preserve past wording and eligibility and give each arm comparable treatment of timeouts, malformed outputs, cancellations, blank fields and failures. Keep owner-authored, proxy-generated, revealed, unknown-origin and timeout rows distinguishable. Use hidden human answers for the primary target. Report missingness and sensitivity for the earlier archive, where exposure is incomplete. Evaluate both natural-frequency performance and family-balanced performance, returning families, new families, and learning over time.

**Memory update.** Use three levels: immutable episode, candidate scoped hypothesis, and evidence for future applicability. Store source spans, card/field IDs, decision stage, conditions observable at ask time, predicted consequence, exceptions, timestamps, and distinct supporting/contradicting sessions. Update with every relevant answer, including bare yes. Permit “uninformative” where the rule’s antecedent was absent or unobservable. Deduplicate evidence from one source before counting it as independent support. An explicit standing instruction has different evidentiary force from one inferred refusal. Retain one-off instructions immediately for their own scope; test the reach of inferred rules on later episodes. At retrieval, rank candidates by similarity, then check antecedent, stage and scope, and allow zero applicable principles. Log which rule changed which output. Track a subsequent-answer loss for each exposed rule with a shadow prediction omitting it when feasible. This is a research ablation, not proof that every forecast error has a unique cause.

**Decision rule.** Estimate the paired risk difference on all eligible cards and block-resample intact sessions or contiguous time blocks, keeping all arms paired. Show sensitivity to block length and to removing each high-volume family. If dependence crosses sessions, time blocks and family leave-outs address different threats. Record effective counts of blocks and discordances. Freeze a fixed horizon or use an explicit sequential design. A floor of ten discordances is an operational floor and gives no generic power guarantee. Treat secondary tests as estimation or prespecify a multiplicity strategy. Report one-owner conclusions for the specified stream and time period.

## What these counts can support

I computed the smallest absolute gain detectable with approximately 80% power for a two-sided exact McNemar test at 5%, under **independent pairs and fixed population discordance rate** d. For each n I integrated over M distributed Binomial(n,d); conditional challenger wins follow Binomial(M,(1 + delta/d)/2). The two scenarios below are planning assumptions for the new endpoint. The old endpoint’s observed discordance was 182/1,926 = 9.45%. All entries are percentage-point gains; “unattainable” means even winning every discordance does not reach 80% power at that d.

| Eligible pairs | d = 15% | d = 30% |
|---|---:|---:|
| 36 no answers | Unattainable | 25.3 |
| 108 neither answers | 10.6 | 15.3 |
| 248 qualified yes/no answers | 7.1 | 10.0 |
| 473 typed-containing multiple-choice cards | 5.1 | 7.2 |
| 769 yes/no cards | 4.0 | 5.6 |
| 1,926 cards | 2.5 | 3.5 |

These are paired binary endpoint calculations. On actual neither cards they describe power for a *recall difference*, and say nothing about false positives among the other cards. Content strata overlap, notably the 106 qualified neither answers. With 108 true neither cases, recall near 50% has an independent-case 95% margin around 9 percentage points; at 248 it is about 6 points. Six-way subdivision of those 248, family transfer among the 36 noes, changes of a few points on neither, and severity-specific proxy reliability should be descriptive. For the prospective trial simulate paired outcomes from development sessions, preserving observed burst lengths, arrival rates, discordance, human audit fraction and missingness. Use the actual number of independent blocks to set the calendar horizon. To illustrate proxy requirements, even zero material errors in 100 independent auto-answers leaves a one-sided 95% upper risk near 3%; about 299 error-free independent actions are needed to put it below 1%. Clustering weakens both assurances.

## Conclusions to keep, narrow, and remeasure

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Frozen comparison and stop | Keep. The archived counts give (67 − 115)/1,926 = −2.49 points and an independent-pair exact p = 0.000462. They justify the recorded decision under its original rule. Session and template dependence affects population inference. The store limit also changed mid-run. The four-model sample shared cards and was added after the stop. |
| Re-spin damage | Keep as a post hoc diagnostic. It accounts for 41 of 48 net Haiku losses. Outside it the effect was −7/1,799 = −0.39 points. That arithmetic describes concentration. It does not estimate the performance of a repaired retriever. |
| “Fixed rules are a ceiling” | Replace with a benchmark. They scored 1,186/1,926 versus 1,176/1,926 for plain Opus. The reported paired interval for Opus minus rule spans zero. On fully recommended multiple-choice cards the paper’s 612/1,059 = 57.8% is a representational upper bound for a listed-option picker under the old labels. The rule’s actual score on that subset was 531/1,059 = 50.1%. Those quantities answer different questions. |
| Yes/no headroom | Always-yes scored 621/769 = 80.8%. Even an oracle restricted to yes/no could cover only 657/769 = 85.4% on the original labels, including the four stance-free answers as misses. | Principles help where the old scorer was blind | Open in both directions. Both arms missed the same outcome categories; this preserves the old score difference but cannot reveal the sign of the new one. A rough text match gave 210 yes recommendations versus 11 no recommendations on no-default cards with identifiable written direction. Preserve a conservative timeout policy appropriate to each action. Add explicit `recommendation`, `timeout_action`, displayed highlight/order, and `answer_source` fields. Missing or ambiguous historical advice remains unknown. |
| Recommendation caused agreement | Unidentified in replay. A paired model-only ablation can remove both the badge and position cue, retaining option identity, to measure model reliance; text may still carry substantive advice. To test owner anchoring, separately randomize badge visibility and option order on consenting, eligible low-stakes cards while holding advice content and timeout action constant. **Sequence for the team.** First produce an attrition table from raw outcome through meaningful field, writer input, stored evidence and scorer input. Re-score existing forecasts for literal versus semantic choice and adjudicate the re-spin decision-stage example. Then freeze the new annotation and output contract and test no-memory, raw-episode, and principle arms in a prospective shadow window. Resume investment in a reranker only if diagnosis shows retrieval is the bottleneck and the richer task demonstrates enough attainable gain to justify it.
