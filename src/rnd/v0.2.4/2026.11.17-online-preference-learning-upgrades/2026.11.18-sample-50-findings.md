## Findings that change the work order

1. **Bring write-in handling into the first label repair.** On card `7cb2120f`, Rick requested an approval link and supporting details on one field while choosing listed recommendations on the other two. Treating the whole card as invalid loses two exact labels; forcing the written response onto the nearest option loses the instruction. On `71783724`, a written answer accepts the recommendation while adding a requirement. Record “offered option,” “offered set” or “unlisted text” per field. Score option correctness, detection of a write-in, and fidelity to its condition separately. A typed write-in is different from a model abstention. A display-only forecast can say “likely to ask for more information” when wording is uncertain.
2. **Make the asker’s recommended option a named baseline.** A unique “(Recommended)” label appeared on 31 of 32 single-select fields. It was first on all 31 and matched Rick’s submitted label exactly on 20. First option matched on 21/32 including the one unmarked field. On the 17 single-select fields of the 13 sampled post-cutover abstained cards, first option would have matched 8. These are descriptive comparisons on selected rows. They neither establish the best policy nor separate a recommendation effect from a position effect. In replay, compare the two rules on cards where they differ, measure recommendations separately from timeout defaults, and keep written answers in the denominator.
3. **Treat “cold start” as an inadequate diagnosis.** All 17 sampled MC abstentions record five similar cases, including all 13 post-cutover abstentions. There was retrieved material. Whether it had compatible fields, legal choices or sufficient evidence remains unknown. All five pre-cutover MC forecasts in the sample had zero header overlap with their target card. The single post-cutover forecast matched the Script Review card. Persist per-field reasons and candidate IDs after retrieval, remapping and option validation. Prioritize this ahead of increasing the number of neighbors.
4. **Keep qualifiers in the primary evaluation.** Lupin predicted yes on all 21 sampled yes/no cards. Its 17 correct stances exactly tie always-yes in this sample. It attached a qualifier on 10 cards. Only four of those had a qualifier in Rick’s answer; all four texts differ verbatim. It omitted qualifiers on four other qualified answers. Wording differences alone cannot establish a semantic error. Have a reviewer check whether a qualifier preserves intent, invents a condition or imports one from another situation. The three “neither” answers include coordination, resend and clarification requests. Those are useful examples of a question needing a different response.
5. **Use free text and batch for different tests.** All three free-text forecasts scored false under the logged cosine threshold of 0.75, with logged confidence values of 0.90, 0.95 and 1.00 (actual client display is unknown). The sample is small enough to read every pair. For batch, decoding the old nested answer maps permits an exact-string diagnostic: 8/10 fields and 1/3 whole cards match. Stored scores were null on all three. These fixture results verify recoverability and should never be pooled into personal accuracy.
