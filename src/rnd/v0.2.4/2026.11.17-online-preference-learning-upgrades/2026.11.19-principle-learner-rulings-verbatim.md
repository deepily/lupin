- 2026-11-19 — **The eight walkthrough questions** (Rick, about 14:24, pasted on the command line; verbatim, numbered as Cheech numbered them):
  > Answers below, numbered as the manager numbered them. I read the committed remediation plan and the history-recovery code on the remote branch at 60e085ad5; your local checkout is 364 commits behind it.
  >
  > 2. Trained ranker: the learner owns it. The committed phases 8 and 11 contain no trained model. Phase 8 is smoothed class frequencies plus weighted neighbors; phase 11 is candidate recall and category as a ranking signal. Rule that it stays that way, and that the expert's "lightweight trained classifier" and "small contextual ranker" clauses point to layer 2.
  >
  > 3. C0: phase 6 builds it, and B1 is the same job plus one prompt block. Phase 6's Haiku background job, forecasting from the card and earlier questions, is C0. B1 should be that job with applicable principles added, behind its own switch, which fits decision 1's one-switch rule. So the learner builds no forecaster of its own. The extractor and offline replay don't depend on phase 6 and can start now. One conflict needs a ruling. Phase 2 puts a unique constraint on notification_id in prediction_log, so two arms can't each store a forecast for one card. Either the key becomes notification plus arm, or shadow arms get a sibling table.
  >
  > 4. Models: Haiku for both C0 and B1; a stronger model for the extractor. C0 and B1 must share the model and the prompt apart from the principle block, or the comparison is confounded. The extractor has no latency budget and the harder job, so I'd start it on Sonnet through the same model_transport.complete door, with its own INI key and a longer timeout. Its model id goes into extractor_version, since changing the model changes the principles.
  >
  > 5. Draft at reveal: phase 5's client sends it, phase 2's seen route stores it. The fold-open call already exists and is first-write-wins. It gains one optional field: the current state of the answer controls. This is not a blocker. Without it, "fold opened before the answer" counts as exposed and "never opened" as unexposed, which the ledger already records.
  >
  > 6. Adapter: build on rebuild_context. The 50-target export stays as a test fixture. rebuild_context already does what my adapter needed: as-of joins, link tiers and a confidence per tier. Two cautions: Usable volume is small. The plan's step 15 reports the context export starts 4 November: of 7,862 asks, 528 have reconstructed context and 349 are usable for training or test. The learner can only extract principles where context exists. A wider context export is the most valuable data step for it; 1,618 recipient answers predate the window. I couldn't find the 3,608 figure in the committed tree, so I can't say how much of that cohort has rebuilt context. The "session" tier rests on a guessed two-hour window, so evidence joined that way should be marked inferred.
  >
  > 7. Contested principles: a capped, batched multiple-choice card to Rick. It uses the existing ask path: the two conflicting answers, with options for one, the other, both under a condition, or retract. Those cards get their own answer source and no forecast, so the learner never learns from its own review queue. Cap it at about three a day. A contested principle is already excluded from retrieval, so nothing is urgent.
  >
  > 8. Ask-form principles: record only, for now. Showing them to the asking sessions changes the questions you receive, which breaks comparison against the frozen baseline and erodes the evidence behind the principle itself. It is probably their most valuable eventual use, since it cuts "neither" and write-in answers at the source. That should be a later switch of its own, limited to principles you have confirmed, and measured by the neither and write-in rate.
