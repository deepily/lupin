# Open-Weight LLMs for a Single RTX 4090 (24 GB, sm_89): Faithful, Human-Sounding Summarization of Jargon-Dense Agent Messages — State of the Art as of July 30, 2026

## TL;DR
- **Top pick: Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 at AWQ 4-bit (W4A16)** (`cpatonn/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-AWQ-4bit`), non-thinking, on vLLM with `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3` and prefix caching. It fits ~16–17 GB of weights in 24 GB, leaves room for 32k+ context, decodes at 3B-active MoE speed (fast TTFT), scores IFEval 84.7 with near-zero quant loss, and its 2507 instruct tune is explicitly built for "markedly better alignment with user preferences in subjective and open-ended tasks." For maximum faithfulness/naturalness on a dense-but-short input, a **dense 24B — Mistral Small 3.2 24B (W4A16)** — is the strongest alternative and arguably the best "human voice"; **Gemma 3 27B** is the KV-cache-efficiency and prose-quality wildcard.
- **The 4-bit-30B vs FP8-14B question:** for THIS task (short input, faithfulness + natural register, hard format constraints), the evidence favors a **well-quantized 24–32B at W4A16/FP8 over a 12–14B at BF16.** 4-bit weight-only is near-lossless on instruction-following (IFEval recovery ≈99.6–100%), and the extra parameters buy faithfulness and register that a 14B cannot match. Avoid 3-bit and avoid reasoning/thinking modes for latency and slop-control reasons.
- **Serving reality on Ada/sm_89:** vLLM V1 + AWQ/GPTQ (Marlin), compressed-tensors W4A16, and FP8 W8A8 (Marlin emulation) all work on the 4090; **FP8 KV cache works and is essentially free in quality; MXFP4 (gpt-oss) runs but Ada has no native FP4 tensor cores** so it dequantizes to bf16 for compute (works via Triton/Marlin, slower than on Hopper). NVFP4 is **not** natively accelerated on sm_89. Use `--enable-prefix-caching` (your DM template is reused) and `xgrammar` guided decoding to hard-enforce "verdict first, ≤3 lines."

## Key Findings

1. **Best overall fit = a 30B-class sparse MoE or a 24–27B dense, quantized to 4-bit or FP8.** The 24 GB card comfortably holds 24–32B weights at 4-bit (~13–18 GB) with headroom for a 16k–32k KV cache. Dense 70B does **not** fit at any usable quant without CPU offload (disqualifying for your sub-10s latency target).
2. **Quantization is near-free at the right settings.** Red Hat AI / Neural Magic report **99.5–100%+ OpenLLM recovery** for FP8 and W4A16 checkpoints of exactly your candidate models, and critically **IFEval recovery of 99.6–100%** — instruction-following, your hardest constraint, survives 4-bit. The academic literature agrees: **W8A8 and W4A16 are "near-lossless"; 3-bit and below are risky**; and **AWQ-int4 is the best-preserving 4-bit method**.
3. **Reasoning/thinking modes HURT this task.** CodingFleet's "AI Model Hallucination Rates 2026" analysis (May 29, 2026), citing the Vectara HHEM Leaderboard, finds "the Reasoning Tax is real: thinking/reasoning modes amplify hallucination 2–3× across all model families" — concrete pairs include DeepSeek V3.2→R1 (5.3%→11.3%, 2.1×) and Grok 3→Grok 4 Fast (5.8%→20.2%, 3.5×). Thinking also adds latency and verbose slop. Use non-thinking instruct variants (Qwen3 `-Instruct-2507`, or `/no_think`; gpt-oss `Reasoning: low`).
4. **"AI slop" is measurable and model-dependent.** EQ-Bench's Slop Score / slop-forensics quantify GPT-isms; this is directly relevant to your "excel at mimicking human speech" requirement. Mistral Small and Gemma 3 are widely regarded as more natural/less sloppy than gpt-oss, which is clean and literal but robotic and refusal-prone.
5. **Faithfulness on numbers/identifiers is a prompt+decoding problem as much as a model problem.** No public benchmark measures verbatim preservation of git hashes, counts, and negations. You must build deterministic pre-checks (regex extraction + set comparison) around whichever model you pick — covered in the harness section.

### 2. Fit analysis / memory math (24 GB budget)

Rule of thumb: usable VRAM ≈ 22–23 GB after CUDA context. Weight bytes ≈ params × bytes/param. KV cache per token = `2 × n_layers × n_kv_heads × head_dim × dtype_bytes`.

| Model | Quant | Weight VRAM | KV/1k tok (FP16) | KV/1k tok (FP8) | Realistic max ctx @24GB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-30B-A3B (2507) | W4A16 (AWQ) | ~16.5 GB | ~0.11 GB (GQA, 48L) | ~0.055 GB | ~48–64k (FP8 KV); ~32k (FP16 KV) |
| Qwen3-30B-A3B (2507) | FP8 W8A8 | ~31 GB | — | — | **does not fit** (weights alone > 24 GB) |
| Qwen3-32B dense | W4A16 (AWQ/GPTQ) | ~18 GB | ~0.13 GB | ~0.065 GB | ~24–32k (FP8 KV) |
| Mistral Small 3.2 24B | W4A16 | ~13.5 GB | ~0.10 GB | ~0.05 GB | ~64k+ (FP8 KV) |
| Mistral Small 3.2 24B | FP8 W8A8 | ~24 GB | ~0.10 GB | ~0.05 GB | **tight/no-fit** — the official Mistral card states it "requires ~55 GB of GPU RAM in bf16 or fp16"; FP8 ~24 GB leaves almost nothing → prefer W4A16 on one card |
| Gemma 3 27B-it | W4A16 | ~15–16 GB | **~0.02–0.03 GB** (5:1 local/global) | ~0.01–0.015 GB | **very long — 96k+** thanks to interleaved attention |
| gpt-oss-20b | MXFP4 | ~14 GB | ~0.07 GB (GQA+SWA 1:1, head_dim 64) | ~0.035 GB | ~32k comfortable; 128k = speed cliff |
| Qwen3-14B dense | W4A16 | ~8.5 GB | ~0.10 GB | ~0.05 GB | 100k+ |

**KV-cache winners (disproportionately cheap long context on 24 GB):** **Gemma 3 27B** (5 local sliding-window layers of 1024 tokens per 1 global layer → per the Gemma 3 technical report, KV-cache overhead drops from ~60% of the long-context baseline under global-only attention to below 15%), **gpt-oss-20b** (1:1 full/sliding-window-128, head_dim 64 = half standard), and hybrid-Mamba **Granite 4.0 / Nemotron-H** (SSM layers carry no growing KV; IBM claims >70% memory reduction vs comparable transformers). For your 16k–32k budget all shortlisted models fit; Gemma's advantage matters if you later grow thread history to 100k+.

**FP8 KV cache** (`--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3`): halves KV memory at negligible quality cost for this task; the reasoning-quant literature classifies 8-bit (incl. KV) as near-lossless. Use it.

**Why NOT CPU offload:** offloading experts/layers to system RAM over PCIe (no NVLink on 4090) collapses decode to single-digit tok/s and destroys TTFT — unacceptable for interactive sub-10s responses. Everything must be resident in VRAM.

- **Mistral-Small-3.1-24B W4A16 (INT4/GPTQ):** OpenLLM v1 75.03→74.63 (**99.5%**), HumanEval pass@1 99.9%, MMLU-Pro 99.0%. (`RedHatAI/Mistral-Small-3.1-24B-Instruct-2503-quantized.w4a16`)
- **Gemma-3-27b-it W4A16:** v1 78.26→78.02 (**99.70%**), vision 100.9%. (`RedHatAI/gemma-3-27b-it-quantized.w4a16`)

*(Note: some IFEval recovery rows above are for FP8/NVFP4 checkpoints; these are the quantization-noise-level deltas — instruction-following does not collapse under 4-bit/FP8 for any of your candidates. Recovery >100% reflects quant noise, not real gains.)*

**Academic evidence:**
- *"Quantization Hurts Reasoning?"* (arXiv 2504.04823, COLM 2025): "lossless quantization can be achieved with W8A8 or W4A16 … lower bit-widths introduce significant accuracy risks." **8-bit W8A8 preserves accuracy across sizes; 4-bit weight-only and 4-bit KV are near-lossless (≤1% avg)**; harder tasks (AIME) suffer up to **4× more degradation** than easy ones (GSM8K). AWQ preferred over GPTQ for weight-only; RL-trained models more fragile than distilled.
- *"Does quantization affect long-context tasks?"* (arXiv 2505.20276, EMNLP 2025): on average **8-bit (FP8/GPTQ-int8) is near-lossless (~0.2–0.8% drop); 4-bit methods lose more, with AWQ-int4 the best 4-bit method and BNB-nf4 the worst (−6.9% avg, up to −59% single-case at 128k).** Constraint-satisfaction (instruction-following-like) degrades more as the number of constraints grows — relevant to your multi-constraint format, and an argument for FP8/AWQ over nf4 and against very long contexts at low bit-width.

**The 4-bit-30B vs FP8-14B verdict for THIS task:** Choose the **larger, well-quantized model.** 4-bit weight-only barely dents IFEval (≈99.6–100% recovery) and general quality (≈99.5%), so a 24–32B at W4A16 retains its faithfulness/register edge over a 14B at BF16. The one caveat: avoid pushing a 4-bit model to very long contexts where the long-context literature shows 4-bit retrieval degradation (−16 to −23%). At your 16k–32k budget you're safe. **Do not use 3-bit** — it crosses into the "risky" regime, and number-drift on hashes/counts is exactly the failure mode low-bit quant induces.

### 5. Serving specifics on RTX 4090 (sm_89), mid-2026

- **vLLM V1 engine** is default. On Ada it supports: AWQ (Marlin), GPTQ (Marlin), compressed-tensors **W4A16**, **FP8 W8A8** (Marlin-style emulation — Ada has no native FP8 tensor cores, so throughput gain is modest vs. the memory saving), bitsandbytes NF4 (works, slower, weaker than AWQ). **FP8 KV cache** (`fp8_e4m3`/`fp8_e5m2`) supported and recommended.
- **MXFP4 (gpt-oss):** runs on Ada via Triton/Marlin fallback, but **native MXFP4 tensor cores start at Blackwell**; on Hopper it uses the OpenAI Triton `matmul_ogs` kernel; on Ada it's the Marlin fallback and dequantizes MoE weights for compute. Known gotcha: several stacks (transformers, some vLLM/NIM builds) error with "MXFP4 quantized models is only supported on GPUs with compute capability ≥ 9.0" or silently dequantize to bf16 (blowing past 16 GB). Confirmed-working paths on 4090: Ollama, llama.cpp GGUF, and current vLLM with the right Triton/kernels installed. Budget setup time.
- **NVFP4:** not natively accelerated on sm_89 (the community `AdaLLM` project shows an FP4-weight/FP8-KV custom-Triton path exists, but it is experimental, not vLLM/SGLang mainline). Prefer AWQ/GPTQ W4A16 or FP8 KV on the 4090.
- **Attention backend:** FlashAttention (default) or FlashInfer; gpt-oss historically wanted FlashAttention-3 (Hopper) — on Ada use the Triton attention backend. 
- **Prefix caching** (`--enable-prefix-caching`): **high-value here** — your DM system-prompt/template is reused across every call, so the shared prefix's KV is computed once. SGLang's RadixAttention reuses shared prefixes even more aggressively at low concurrency.
- **Guided decoding / structured output:** vLLM and SGLang both default to **xgrammar** (2026), with Outlines/llguidance selectable. Use it to hard-enforce "verdict-first, ≤3 lines" via a JSON schema or grammar (e.g., a schema with `verdict` string + `lines` array max-length 3). vLLM V1 made grammar init non-blocking; SGLang's grammar-cache gives near-zero per-request overhead on repeated schemas after warmup.
- **Speculative decoding / EAGLE / n-gram:** available in vLLM; at single-user low concurrency EAGLE or n-gram spec-decode can cut decode latency meaningfully for the largely-extractive output you're producing (much of the output echoes input tokens). Worth trying but validate acceptance rate.
- **Throughput on a 4090 (measured, indicative):** gpt-oss-20b MXFP4 has been reported around 200+ tok/s decode on a 4090 (one guide cites 225 tok/s; treat as vendor/community figure); dense 32B at Q4 ~30–35 tok/s; Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE decodes far faster than a dense 30B (~3B active) — community figures cite well over 100 tok/s. For a 500-word input and ≤3-line output, prefill dominates and end-to-end is comfortably sub-10s for all shortlisted models with prefix caching.
- **vLLM vs SGLang at low concurrency:** roughly comparable on unique prompts (within ~7% on MoE); SGLang tends to win on shared-prefix reuse and grammar-cache warmup — both favorable to your reused-template pattern.

**Minimal Python / OpenAI-SDK call pattern (FastAPI-friendly):**
```python
from openai import OpenAI
client = OpenAI(base_url="http://localhost:8000/v1", api_key="none")

SYSTEM = ("Rewrite the peer message into direct, forthright, conversational English. "
          "Verdict first. 3 lines or fewer. Detail→abstract. Preserve every number, "
          "hash, filename, and negation EXACTLY. Do not add facts.")

def rewrite(msg: str) -> str:
    r = client.chat.completions.create(
        model="cpatonn/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507-AWQ-4bit",
        messages=[{"role":"system","content":SYSTEM},
                  {"role":"user","content":msg}],
        temperature=0.2, max_tokens=256,
        extra_body={"guided_json": {  # xgrammar-enforced shape
            "type":"object",
            "properties":{"verdict":{"type":"string"},
                          "lines":{"type":"array","items":{"type":"string"},"maxItems":3}},
            "required":["verdict","lines"]}})
    return r.choices[0].message.content
```
(Keep temperature low — 0.15–0.3 — for faithfulness; the reused `SYSTEM` prefix is prefix-cached.)

### 7. Evaluation harness (pitched at judge-panel/Krippendorff level)

No public benchmark covers your exact task, so build a **two-stage harness: deterministic gates → LLM-judge panel.**

**Stage A — deterministic pre-checks (disqualify before judging):**
- **Verbatim entity preservation.** Regex-extract from the *source* the sets of: short hashes `\b[0-9a-f]{7,40}\b`, UUIDs, filenames `\b[\w-]+\.\w+\b`, integers/percentages/`n=\d+`, and negation spans ("NOT", "did not", "am not claiming"). Require every source identifier that the summary *references* to match exactly (set-membership / string-equality). Any hash or count that appears altered = **hard fail** (number-drift is your disqualifying mode).
- **Negation preservation.** Check that each source negation's polarity survives (e.g., flag if "did NOT fire a blocking ask" becomes "fired a blocking ask"). Use dependency/polarity heuristics + an LLM verifier as backstop.
- **Format compliance.** Line count ≤3 (post-verdict), verdict-first ordering, no preamble. Trivial to script.

**Stage B — LLM-judge panel (only on entries passing Stage A):**
- **Rubric, pointwise + pairwise.** Score (1) faithfulness/groundedness, (2) completeness of technical detail, (3) naturalness/low-slop register, (4) directness (verdict-first, concision). Run **both** pointwise (absolute rubric) and pairwise (A/B vs a reference model) — pairwise is more discriminative for register.
- **Panel design.** ≥3 heterogeneous judges (e.g., a frontier API model + two different open models) to avoid single-judge self-bias. Compute **Krippendorff's α** across judges per dimension; you already have this infra. Target α ≥ 0.67 before trusting rankings; below that, tighten the rubric or add anchor examples.
- **Position-bias mitigation.** For pairwise, run both A/B and B/A orders and average (as EQ-Bench does); randomize; truncate to equal length to kill length bias.
- **Slop instrumentation.** Run the slop-forensics / EQ-Bench Slop list over outputs as an *objective* naturalness proxy independent of the judges (guards against judges being fooled by vocab-flexing).
- **Sample & power.** ~100–200 real DMs stratified by density (tables vs. prose vs. hash-heavy) gives enough power to separate the top 2–3 models; report per-stratum results (some models fail specifically on ASCII tables).

### Appendix — What a second 4090 (TP=2, 48 GB) unlocks
With TP=2 (`--tensor-parallel-size 2`) you get ~48 GB aggregate, enabling: **FP8/BF16 24–32B dense** (Mistral Small 3.2 FP8 comfortably, Qwen3-32B FP8), **Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B at 4-bit AWQ** with real context, or a 30B at higher precision + 100k+ context. **Caveat:** consumer 4090s have **no NVLink**; TP all-reduces traverse PCIe (Gen4 ~32 GB/s bidirectional), so TP=2 adds per-token communication latency and scales sub-linearly at low concurrency — you may find a single-card AWQ model has *better* TTFT than a PCIe-bottlenecked TP=2 setup. TP helps capacity/context more than single-stream latency here. Pipeline-parallel or simply running two independent single-card replicas (one per GPU) behind your FastAPI load balancer is often the better use of the second card for a low-concurrency interactive service.

## Recommendations
1. **Deploy Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 AWQ-4bit on vLLM first** (config above), non-thinking, temp 0.2, FP8 KV, prefix caching, xgrammar-enforced verdict/≤3-line schema. This is your speed+faithfulness+naturalness sweet spot.
2. **Run the §7 harness on 100–200 of your real DMs** comparing it head-to-head against **Mistral Small 3.2 24B** (best human voice) and **Gemma 3 27B** (best if context grows). Decide on measured faithfulness + slop + Krippendorff-validated judge scores, not vibes.
3. **Thresholds that change the recommendation:** if deterministic hash/number-drift fails >~2% even after prompt tuning → move up to **Qwen3-32B dense 4-bit** (or FP8 on TP=2) for the faithfulness ceiling. If naturalness/slop is the losing axis → **Mistral Small 3.2** wins. If you need >64k context (long thread history) → **Gemma 3 27B**. If TTFT must be minimal and register can be terse → **gpt-oss-20b** at `Reasoning: low`.
4. **Never** use 3-bit, thinking modes, or CPU offload for this task.
5. **If you add the second 4090,** prefer either Mistral Small 3.2 **FP8** (TP=2) for max quality, or two single-card replicas for throughput — not TP=2 chasing latency.

## Caveats
- Mid-2026 SEO/aggregator pages hallucinate model names and tok/s figures freely; I trusted only primary sources and flagged the rest. Several throughput numbers (e.g., gpt-oss "225 tok/s on 4090") are community/vendor claims, not independently verified here.
- Red Hat/Neural Magic recovery numbers are vendor-published (though reproducible); >100% recovery rows reflect quantization noise, not real gains.
- The long-context quant paper (2505.20276) has an internal figure-vs-text labeling inconsistency; I report its **text** conclusion (8-bit near-lossless; AWQ-int4 best 4-bit; BNB-nf4 worst).
- No public benchmark measures verbatim hash/number/negation preservation, ASCII-table faithfulness, or your exact "verdict-first ≤3 lines" register — hence the custom harness. Treat all benchmark scores as proxies.
- Model landscape moves monthly; verify the newest `-2507`/successor tags and quant repos at deploy time. Qwen3 "3.5/3.6/4," Gemma 4, GLM-5.x, and similar names circulating on SEO sites could not be confirmed from primary sources as of this writing and are treated as unverified.
