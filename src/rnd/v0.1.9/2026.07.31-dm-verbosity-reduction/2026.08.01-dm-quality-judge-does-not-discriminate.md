# The DM Quality Judge does not discriminate — and three green signals said it did

**Row**: `ca7a2cbf` (P1, bug) · **Found by**: Mr. Radio 🦉 (`9056b85b`), 2026-08-01
**Found while working**: `ad560979` — Rick's *"that prompt file looks a little junkie"*
**Model under test**: `ConfidentialMind/Mistral-Small-3.2-24B-Instruct-2506-GPTQ-AutoRound-TextOnly`, live on `:3001`
**Status**: diagnosed, **not fixed**. The remedy is a design decision, and it is Rick's.

---

## The one-paragraph version

The judge grades every DM the same way regardless of what the DM says. Asked to grade a
message that leads with its verdict in plain English, and a message that buries its point
under narration and coined vocabulary, it returns **identical grades** — and, on the
current configuration, returns them wearing the example's own note text. This was found by
a question about section headings. Nothing in the test suite, the unit coverage, or the
prior live A/B pointed at it, and all three were green.

## 2. The measurement

Two fixed DM bodies. One deliberately good on both qualitative dimensions, one deliberately
bad on both. Deliberately kept **under** the 150-word qualitative ceiling, because past that
ceiling the judge skips the model entirely and returns an honest 🤷/0 — a probe that
tripped the ceiling would pass while measuring nothing.

**GOOD** (45 words) — leads with the verdict, every following sentence is evidence or a
required action:

> Phase 1 is done and green. 89 unit tests pass, 100% lines and branches on the changed
> file. Not committed — I am holding your gate. One risk: the retry path is still untested
> against a real timeout, so I would not merge before Thursday.

**BAD** (78 words) — buries the verdict under narration, and does it in coined vocabulary:

> So I wanted to circle back on the thing we were discussing earlier, and I have to say it
> has been quite a journey getting here. After some digging around in the queue layer I
> started to form a picture. The owed oracle's count-only path has no aperture disclosure,
> which admits re-park by induction and is therefore provenance-idempotent. Anyway I think
> it is probably fine now. That is the sharpest part of the whole exercise, and worth naming.

Three runs each, against the live model:

| run | body | directness | tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GOOD | 👍 `good` (1) | 👎 `needs_improvement` (−1) |
| 1 | BAD | 👍 `good` (1) | 👎 `needs_improvement` (−1) |
| 2 | GOOD | 👍 `good` (1) | 👎 `needs_improvement` (−1) |
| 2 | BAD | 👍 `good` (1) | 👎 `needs_improvement` (−1) |
| 3 | GOOD | 👍 `good` (1) | 👎 `needs_improvement` (−1) |
| 3 | BAD | 👍 `good` (1) | 👎 `needs_improvement` (−1) |

Six for six identical.

### The detail that turns a suspicion into a finding

Those two values are exactly what sits in `get_example_for_template()`
(`src/cosa/agents/dm_quality_judge/xml_models.py:159`). Identical grades could still be
coincidence — two messages could genuinely earn the same score. The **note text** cannot:

```
< directness > good < / directness >
< directness note > Leads with the verdict in the first sentence. < / directness note >
< tone > needs_improvem...
```

That is the example's own justification string, returned **byte-for-byte for the BAD DM,
which does not lead with a verdict in its first sentence or anywhere else.** A grade can
coincide. A justification that describes a different message cannot.

Reproduced identically for the 45-word GOOD body, the 78-word BAD body, and the single
word **`yes`**.

A fourth input — `"um so like anyway whatever i guess maybe it happened somehow you know"` —
*did* move, to `meh`. Its note was `"The result is present but buried under a sentence or
two of setup."` That is lifted verbatim from the **rubric's** `meh` anchor at
`dm-quality-judge.txt:21`, and it does not describe that input either. So it copies prompt
text on that path too — just a different span of it.

## 3. Three explanations, all killed by measurement

Each of these was plausible enough to write a fix against. None survived being checked.

### 3.1 "The template isn't assembling — the DM never reaches the model"

Dumped the final prompt string with a marker in place of the body. `{dm_body}` substitutes,
`{{PYDANTIC_XML_EXAMPLE}}` injects, and the DM sits directly above the response block.
**Ruled out.** This is the step that would normally be skipped as obviously fine, which is
exactly why it was run first once the theorising started.

### 3.2 "`Copy this structure exactly` is being obeyed literally"

Line 51 of the prompt reads *"Copy this structure exactly, replacing only the grade labels
and note text:"* immediately before the example. That is a compelling story: the model is
told to copy, and it copies.

Rewrote that line **alone** to say the example is shape-only and its content belongs to a
different message, then re-probed 3×3. Output **unchanged** at `(1, −1)` / `(1, −1)`.

**Ruled out. The edit was reverted, so the file is unmodified.** Worth stating plainly: the
most satisfying explanation on offer was wrong, and only the re-probe said so.

### 3.3 "It's the long-input parroting bug, `2a41e141`, already contained"

`2a41e141` was closed by gating the qualitative pass at `QUALITATIVE_WORD_LIMIT = 150` —
past the ceiling the LLM is skipped and an honest 🤷/0 returned. The probe bodies are **45
and 78 words**, far under it, so the model was genuinely called on every run and the gate
never fired.

**Ruled out — and this one matters beyond this bug.** The containment shipped against the
symptom that had been *observed* (long inputs), and the observation was mistaken for the
boundary. The behaviour does not respect that boundary. A gate placed at the edge of the
evidence rather than the edge of the mechanism reads as a fix for exactly as long as nobody
samples outside the original sample.

## 4. Swapping the example — and a claim I withdrew

If the model is copying the example, changing the example should change the output. It does.
It does not fix anything.

The example was swapped at **runtime** via `get_example_for_template`, never on disk — the
tree is shared with other sessions, and a temporarily-edited prompt file is a collision
waiting to happen.

**First attempt, one run per body:** variant B produced GOOD `(0, +1)` and BAD `(−1, −1)`.
Different. I reported that the model *can* grade when the example stops being copyable.

**That claim was wrong, and it was wrong in a specific way worth recording.** It was
**n = 1**. Re-run at n = 3:

| Example variant | GOOD ×3 | BAD ×3 | Separated? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** — current (`good` / `needs_improvement`) | `(1, −1)` | `(1, −1)` | ❌ pinned to the example |
| **B** — implausible pair (`terrible` / `exemplary`) | `(0, +1)` | `(0, +1)` | ❌ a *different* constant, still constant |
| **C** — self-identifying notes (`"EXAMPLE ONLY — describes a different message"`) | `(0, +1)` | `(0, 0)` | ❌ tone separates, directness does not |

A single-run difference between two conditions is indistinguishable from sampling noise. It
read as decisive because **it confirmed the hypothesis I already held** — the cheapest
possible evidence, arriving in the shape of the answer I wanted, and I forwarded it before
replicating.

⇒ **The defect is worse than parroting, and "parroting" is the wrong single word for it.**
Changing the example moves *where* the output lands. It stays near-**constant** across
inputs in every variant tested. The example is not the disease; under configuration A it is
merely where the constant happens to sit, and the copied note text is what makes that
particular resting place provable.

## 5. Why three green signals missed it

This is the part worth keeping.

**The unit tier tests the parser, not the judgement.** `test_dm_quality_judge.py` feeds
hand-written clean XML into `from_xml()` and asserts the labels map to the right weights.
Every one of those assertions is correct and none of them can fail when the model returns a
constant — the constant is well-formed XML with valid labels. 95 of 96 green throughout.
**A parser test and a discrimination test look identical in their output and answer
different questions.**

**Coverage measured the wrong axis.** The judge package carried 100% lines and branches
across three review rounds. Full coverage of the code that *handles* a grade says nothing
about whether the grade means anything. The number was real and it was answering a narrower
question than the one anybody was reading it as.

**The prior live A/B did run against the real model.** On 2026-07-31 discrimination was
checked and reported holding 3/3 after the BUG-1 fix. That probe is not this probe and its
inputs are not these inputs, and its driver lived in a reaped session's scratchpad. **This
document does not claim that result was wrong** — it has not been re-derived, and saying
more than that would be manufacturing a second finding out of an absence. What can be said:
whatever it measured, it does not cover the two bodies used here.

**And the bug that was already filed had been closed as contained.** `2a41e141` named this
behaviour. The containment gate was real, tested, and ratified — and sits entirely above a
threshold the behaviour ignores.

⇒ Four independent green signals, each honest about the question it actually asked, and the
union of them still reads as "the judge works."

## 6. What the probe is, and what it cannot yet vouch for

`judge_discrimination_probe.py` (session scratchpad, `9056b85b`):

- Two fixed bodies, N runs each.
- Passes **only** if every GOOD grade outranks every BAD grade on **both** dimensions — a
  margin, not an average, so a single overlapping run fails instead of being averaged away.
- Asserts both bodies sit under `QUALITATIVE_WORD_LIMIT` at start-up, so it cannot silently
  measure the short-circuit path instead of the model.
- Explicitly guards the all-zero fallback: if every GOOD run returns 0, a dead judge would
  otherwise satisfy "no overlap" by collapsing both sides together.

⚠️ **It has no control proving it can report `SEPARATED`.** No configuration tested has
produced separation, so `OVERLAP` is the only verdict this instrument has ever emitted.
Until something makes it say `SEPARATED`, it is an instrument whose passing arm is unproven,
and every result above should be read with that caveat attached. Manufacturing a synthetic
pass to tick that box would test the harness, not the judge — the honest move is to leave
the gap named until a real fix trips it.

## 7. What is not decided

Every single-example variant lands on a constant. That points at the **task shape** — one
subjective five-level judgement across two dimensions, from a single example — rather than
at any particular wording. Which means the remedy is a design choice, not a bug fix:

- **Contrasting few-shot pairs** — show a graded GOOD *and* a graded BAD, so no single
  example can serve as the answer. Most likely to work; largest prompt.
- **One call per dimension** — halve what the model must decide at once. Doubles the calls.
- **A different or larger model** — the 24B GPTQ checkpoint may simply not do this task.
- **Drop the qualitative dimensions** — keep the Python-computed Length grade, which is
  unaffected and has never been in doubt. Removes the defect class and the LLM call, and
  loses the signal the feature was built for.

None tested. Recommending one without measuring it would repeat §4.

**Live impact while undecided**: `dm quality judgment enabled` is standing `True` per Rick's
2026-08-01 instruction, so this path is publishing Directness/Tone grades right now, and
every one of them under the word ceiling carries no information about the DM it is attached
to. **Length is Python-computed and unaffected.**

## 8. The transferable part

**A justification is a stronger signal than a verdict.** Two identical grades can be a
coincidence; a justification that describes a different message cannot. If the judge had
returned only labels, this would still be an open argument about whether two messages might
fairly score the same. It returned prose, and the prose is what made it provable. Where a
grader emits both, the free text is the load-bearing evidence — and it is the field
everyone treats as decorative.

**A containment placed at the edge of the evidence is not placed at the edge of the
mechanism.** `2a41e141`'s 150-word gate was built against the inputs where the problem had
been *seen*. The problem does not know about 150 words. The gate has read as a fix for as
long as nobody sampled below it.

**Full coverage of the handling code says nothing about the decision.** 100% lines and
branches on a package whose entire purpose is a judgement call, where every test supplies
its own hand-written input, measures plumbing.

**A one-run difference between two conditions is not a result** — especially when it agrees
with you. The retraction in §4 cost one re-run to find and would have cost a wrong fix to
discover later.

## 9. A second, separate bug fell out immediately — and it is the more interesting one

Building the few-shot prompt surfaced something that was never about prompts at all.

The live model emits its labels **spaced**:

```
RAW tone = 'needs _ improvement'
```

`normalize_grade_label` was doing two sequential replaces:

```python
key = str( label ).strip().lower().replace( " ", "_" ).replace( "-", "_" )
```

`needs _ improvement` → `needs___improvement` → **not in `GRADE_TABLE`** → silently
degrades to the `meh` fallback.

⇒ **The most frequently emitted multi-word label in a five-level scale was being thrown
away on every occurrence, and published as a considered neutral `0` instead of the real
`−1`.** That is why Tone read `meh` for everything.

This is the same sloppiness class as `a5f7b36d`, which was fixed **for tags** and never
for **label values**.

**The lesson is about the fallback, not the spacing.** `_FALLBACK_LABEL = "meh"` exists
for a genuinely unrecognizable label, and that is a reasonable thing to want. But it
cannot tell *"I do not recognize this"* from *"I failed to parse something I should have
recognized"* — and it reports both as a judgement of `meh`. A degrade-to-neutral with no
signal that a degrade happened is indistinguishable from an opinion.

**Fix**: collapse any run of whitespace / hyphen / underscore to one underscore.

```python
key = re.sub( r"[\s_\-]+", "_", str( label ).strip().lower() ).strip( "_" )
```

**Tests**: 10 new, all five levels × four sloppy spellings each, asserting the **weight**
and not merely the key — the defect was a wrong *number* reaching a reader. Plus a
negative control that a genuinely unknown label (`banana`, `needs_improvementt`, `___`)
**still** lands on `meh`/0, because "fix the normalizer" must not be satisfiable by
accepting everything.

**Mutation control**: restored the old two-replace normalizer at runtime → **7 of the new
tests go red**, and the negative control correctly stays green (it is not sensitive to
this mutation).

## 10. The candidate prompt, and what it measures

📄 [Open: dm-quality-judge-fewshot-candidate.txt](/app/docs?path=lupin/src/conf/prompts/dm-quality-judge-fewshot-candidate.txt)
— **a separate file. Nothing has been changed in the live prompt.**

Three changes from the live prompt, and all three land together, so the measurement below
is of the **combination** — it does not attribute to any one of them:

1. **Two contrasting worked examples**, at opposite ends of both scales, each a full DM
   plus its graded XML. Neither can serve as "the answer" because they disagree.
2. **The trailing `{{PYDANTIC_XML_EXAMPLE}}` is gone.** It was still being copied: with the
   worked examples in place, the GOOD body *still* came back carrying that example's own
   note (*"Leans on an aphorism instead of plain phrasing"*). A filled, plausible answer in
   the last position before `### Response:` is an attractor. The worked examples already
   demonstrate the tag shape, so nothing is lost but the attractor.
3. **Standard section vocabulary** — `### Instruction / Task / Input / Response Format /
   Response`, matching the other 14 prompts. This is the `ad560979` reshape, folded in.

### Measured, 4 runs per body

| Body | directness | tone |
|---|---|---|
| GOOD ×4 | `good` (1) | `good` (1) |
| MID ×4 | `meh` (0) | `meh` (0) |
| BAD ×4 | `meh` (0) | `meh` (0) |

**`SEPARATED` — on both dimensions, 4/4.**

This is also the first time the probe has **ever** emitted `SEPARATED`, which discharges
the caveat in §6: its passing arm is no longer unproven. The instrument can report both
verdicts, and has.

### ⚠️ What it still does not do — stated plainly

**BAD and MID score identically.** Nothing reaches the negative half of the scale. In
practice this is a **two-level signal** (good vs not-good) wearing a five-level scale's
labels. The BAD body is full of coined vocabulary and closes on an aphorism about its own
sharpness; `meh` is not a correct reading of it.

So: the judge now **discriminates**, which it did not before. It does not yet **grade**.
Whether that is good enough to turn back on is Rick's call, and it is the open question
this part ends on — not a claim that the bug is closed.
