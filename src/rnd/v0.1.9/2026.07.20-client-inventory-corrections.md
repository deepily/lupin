## 2. The predicate needs sharpening — this is the reusable part

Cheech's predicate reads: *"an **out-of-process caller** applying a transport budget to
a `:7999` request…"* — and it is a good predicate. The defect is in how it was
**applied**: as a property of the **call site**.

> 🔴 **Exposure is a property of the INVOCATION, not of the call site.**
> One `requests.post(…, timeout=5)` is exposed or not depending on how its module was
> entered. A site-level in/out classification cannot express that, so it silently picks
> one mode and drops the other.

Any module with a `__main__.py` is dual-mode by construction and must be classified
**per entry point**. That is the check that turns 10 into 11, and it is the check to
re-run on the rest of the exclusion list.

## 3a. 🔴 SHARPENED PREDICATE — only the READ leg is exposed (Cheech, `81a50925`)

During a reload the reloader parent **holds the listening socket**, so the kernel
**accepts**: the TCP handshake completes and the hang is entirely in the response. Verified
consistent with plan §4 and with the `TimeoutError`-not-`ConnectionRefused` signature.

⇒ **Exposure is not "has a timeout." It is "has a READ budget shorter than the reload
window."**

- Single-value timeouts (`requests.post(timeout=5)`, `urlopen(timeout=N)`) apply to connect
  **and** read ⇒ exposed.
- **Split tuples are NOT exposed.** `notify_user_sync.py:235` uses `( 3, timeout_seconds + 10 )`
  — a ≥190s read budget; the 3s is the **connect** leg, which a reload never trips. Confirmed
  at `:225-235`, where the comment already says so. **No change; removed from the diff.**

A site that drops out under this lens is as good a result as one that gets bumped.
