## 1. What the code actually does

`src/cosa/agents/utils/proxy_agents/base_listener.py`, with constants from `base_config.py`:

```python
RECONNECT_INITIAL_DELAY  = 1.0
RECONNECT_MAX_DELAY      = 30.0
RECONNECT_MAX_ATTEMPTS   = 10
RECONNECT_BACKOFF_FACTOR = 2.0
```

```python
self._attempt += 1                                    # ← increments BEFORE the min()
delay = min( RECONNECT_INITIAL_DELAY * ( RECONNECT_BACKOFF_FACTOR ** self._attempt ),
             RECONNECT_MAX_DELAY )
```

and, on a successful connect (line 279):

```python
self._attempt = 0  # Reset backoff on successful connect
```

**The increment-before-`min()` is the whole disagreement.** Reading `2^attempt` with
`attempt` starting at 0 gives delays 1/2/4/8/16 and cumulative wakes 1/3/7/15/31 — the
series Mr. Radio argued from. But the counter is incremented first, so the first delay is
`2^1 = 2`, not `2^0 = 1`.

**The reset matters too**: a listener that was healthy when the bounce hit starts this
sequence from zero, so the series applies cleanly to a bounce.

## 2. The gap structure — this is the part that matters

Gaps between consecutive wakes: **4, 8, 16, 30, 30, 30, 30 …**

Once past the fourth attempt the delay is pinned at the cap, so **every later gap is
exactly 30 seconds**. That single fact bounds everything downstream.

A listener reconnects at the **first wake at or after the server is ready again**. Let
`D` = downtime (disconnect → server serving). Then:

```
arrival, measured from gate start  =  (first wake ≥ D)  −  D
```

Because the gate runs post-yield in the lifespan startup hook, *gate start ≈ server ready*,
so that expression is exactly what the settle gate is waiting out.

## 4. 🔴 Arrival is a SAWTOOTH in downtime — a faster restart can arrive LATER

This is the counterintuitive part, and it is why the arc kept feeling unsettled.

Arrival after gate start is **not** proportional to downtime. Within each band it *falls*
as downtime rises, then jumps back to the top of the next band:

```
D = 30.1s  →  arrival +29.9s      ← a FASTER restart …
D = 41.4s  →  arrival +18.6s      ← … arrives 11 seconds LATER than a slower one
```

Rachel separately measured a bounce with a downtime around 8 seconds — that one would
arrive at **+6s** and the retired 15s deadline would have been fine. The two nights'
numbers were never in conflict; they were different points on a sawtooth.

**Consequence: you cannot tune the deadline by averaging observations.** The observable
depends on a quantity that varies bounce to bounce, and it is non-monotonic in that
quantity. Two samples cannot bound a sawtooth.

### What is genuinely NOT settled — and why nobody could settle it

The offset that Krishna and Mr. Radio each assumed and neither measured is the **downtime**.
It is **not recorded anywhere**: the reconnect log line

```python
print( f"{self.LOG_PREFIX} Connection lost. Reconnecting in {delay:.1f}s (attempt {n}/10)..." )
```

carries **no timestamp**, and it is written to stderr outside the timestamped log path — a
`grep` for a timestamped reconnect line over the entire 118 MB centralized listener log
returns **zero matches**. So the one number needed to close the arithmetic from the record
does not exist in the record. That is a missing instrument, not a disagreement, and it is
the honest reason two competent readings stood unreconciled.

**§3's fit is a derivation, not a direct measurement.** It explains both samples exactly
and I believe it, but until the reconnect line is timestamped, "downtime was ~40s" is
inferred from the arrival times rather than read off a clock. The instrument fix is one
line and is proposed in §6.

## 5. So what IS the right deadline?

**The worst case is bounded by the cap, not by anything observed.** A listener that misses
a wake by a hair waits the full cap:

```
worst case arrival  =  gate_start + 29.9s      (downtime just past 30s, or just past 60s, 90s, …)
```

| deadline | verdict |
|---|---|
| 15 (retired) | Misses everything past `D=14`. Both measured bounces lost the whole fleet. |
| **30 (shipped)** | **Sits exactly ON the worst-case bound.** Covers both samples with ~10s to spare, but the tail case lands at +29.9 — a coin flip decided by scheduling noise. |
| **40** | Clears the bound with ~10s of real margin. **Correct, if the deadline is the lever.** |
| anything < 31 | Cannot be safe at any value, for any fleet size, because the cap is 30. |

So Mr. Radio's corrected 40 is the right number — though his stated reason ("the fleet
lands on the 30-second cap wake") was not the operative one. The operative reason is that
**every late gap in the series is 30 seconds wide**, so the worst-case wait after the
server is ready is 30 seconds regardless of which wake the fleet happens to catch.

### 🔴 But the deadline is the wrong lever

**`RECONNECT_MAX_DELAY = 30` is what sets the floor under the deadline.** Lower the cap and
the whole problem shrinks proportionally:

| cap | worst-case wait | smallest safe deadline |
|---|---|---|
| 30s (today) | ~30s | ~40s |
| 15s | ~15s | ~20s |
| **10s** | **~10s** | **~15s** |

A 10s cap would let the deadline go *back down* to roughly where it started, and every
bounce would settle on coverage rather than riding the deadline out. The current 30s cap
also means a listener that misses its early attempts waits half a minute doing nothing —
which is what pushed reconnection past every sane all-clear window in the first place.

### 🔴 And jitter does NOT fix this — it is aimed at a different problem

Jitter is the right fix for the thundering herd: nine sessions hitting `/auth/login` inside
8 milliseconds is a load spike, and randomizing the delay spreads it. That is real and
worth doing.

**But jitter does not lower the worst case.** Each listener still waits up to the cap; all
jitter does is decorrelate *which* listener waits how long. Under the settle gate's
**coverage** predicate — which is a completion test, satisfied only when *every* rostered
session is back — the gate waits for the **slowest** session. Jitter does not move the
slowest session earlier; if anything it guarantees somebody is near the cap on every
bounce, where today the fleet at least misses or makes it as one block.

**Jitter makes partial coverage arrive sooner and full coverage no sooner.** For a gate
that only fires on full coverage, that is not an improvement.

## 6. Recommendation

Three changes, in the order they should land:

1. **`RECONNECT_MAX_DELAY` 30 → 10** (`base_config.py`). This is the actual fix. It cuts
   the worst-case reconnect wait to ~10s and lets the settle deadline come back down to
   ~15s. Fleet-wide blast radius — every proxy listener shares these constants — so it is
   Rick's call, which is exactly why it is stated here rather than done.
2. **Settle deadline 30 → 40 if the cap stays at 30**; **30 → 15 if the cap drops to 10.**
   The deadline is derived from the cap; the two must not be chosen independently again.
3. **Timestamp the reconnect log line** — one line, no blast radius, and it is the
   instrument whose absence made this whole argument unresolvable. With it, the next
   bounce *measures* the downtime instead of leaving it to be inferred.

Jitter is worth adding for the load spike, but it should be described as what it is — a
herd fix — and **must not be sold as making the deadline robust.** It does not.
