# DM Quality Judge — jargon masks indirectness

**Problem statement for an outside expert** · Mr. Radio 🦉, 2026-08-01 · Row `ca7a2cbf`

---

## 0. TL;DR

We grade peer-to-peer agent messages on two subjective dimensions — **Directness** (does it
lead with its result?) and **Tone** (is it plain English or invented jargon?) — using a
locally-served 14B model as the judge. Three of the four discrimination contrasts pass
deterministically. One fails, reproducibly:

> **When a message is written in dense jargon, the model grades it `good` on Directness
> regardless of whether the verdict leads or is buried under four sentences of narration.**

The same directness contrast, run on plain-English bodies, passes cleanly. So the model can
detect a buried verdict — until the prose gets jargon-heavy, at which point that ability
disappears.

**What we want from you**: an explanation of the mechanism and a recommendation on how to
fix it. §6 has the questions.

## 2. What the system computes

Three dimensions, only two of which involve a model.

```
DM body
   │
   ├──► LENGTH ─────────── pure Python: word count → 5-level bucket. NO LLM.
   │
   ├──► DIRECTNESS ─┐
   │                ├───── ONE LLM call grading both, 5-level ordinal scale.
   ├──► TONE ───────┘      Skipped entirely if the body exceeds 150 words.
   │
   └──► OVERALL ────────── pure Python: blends Length with the mean of the two
                           qualitative grades, 50/50 BY CATEGORY.
```

### 2.1 Length — deterministic

```python
if   word_count <=  60: emoji, weight = "⭐", 2
elif word_count <=  90: emoji, weight = "👍", 1
elif word_count <= 150: emoji, weight = "🤷", 0
elif word_count <= 250: emoji, weight = "👎", -1
else:                   emoji, weight = "😞", -2
```

Target ~60 words. This half has never been in question.

### 2.2 The scale both qualitative dimensions use

| label | weight | emoji |
|---|---|---|
| `terrible` | −2 | 😞 |
| `bad` | −1 | 👎 |
| `meh` | 0 | 🤷 |
| `good` | +1 | 👍 |
| `exemplary` | +2 | ⭐ |

Zero-centred, ordinal. The rubric in the prompt gives each label a behavioural definition per
dimension — e.g. for Directness, `bad` = *"several sentences of narration before any verdict;
reader must hunt for the point."*

### 2.3 Overall — deterministic blend

```python
qualitative_weight = ( directness_weight + tone_weight ) / 2.0
overall_weight     = round_half_up( 0.5*length_weight + 0.5*qualitative_weight )
overall_weight     = clamp( overall_weight, -2, +2 )
```

**50/50 by category, not by dimension** — a flat 3-way mean would let the two LLM-judged
dimensions outvote the deterministic one 2-to-1. Ties round half-**up** (`−0.5 → 0`), a
documented lenience rather than Python's banker's rounding.

Worked example: a 300-word DM scoring `exemplary` on both qualitative axes →
`0.5×(−2) + 0.5×(+2) = 0` → 🤷. Verbosity cancels excellence exactly.

### 2.4 A 150-word gate on the LLM half

Bodies over `QUALITATIVE_WORD_LIMIT = 150` skip the model and return a "not graded, too
long" marker. Relevant to §5: **the longest messages — often the worst ones — are never seen
by the judge in production.**

### 2.5 What is live today

`dm quality judgment enabled = True`, `dm quality qualitative enabled = False`.

**Length publishes; the LLM half is switched off** pending this investigation. Directness and
Tone return a `None` weight (deliberately not `0`, so no consumer can average a non-answer as
if it were a neutral opinion), and Overall degrades to being the Length grade alone.

## 3. The experiment

Four fixed message bodies crossing the two properties **independently** — this matters,
because our two earlier samples varied both at once and could not attribute a failure to
either dimension:

|  | plain prose | jargon + aphorism |
|---|---|---|
| **verdict leads** | `DIRECT_PLAIN` | `DIRECT_JARGON` |
| **verdict buried** | `BURIED_PLAIN` | `BURIED_JARGON` |

Each dimension is then scored **only** against the pair that varies it while holding the other
property constant. `DIRECT_PLAIN` is never compared against `BURIED_JARGON` — those differ on
both properties, so a difference between them says nothing about either.

**DIRECT_PLAIN** (45 w) — verdict first, plain English:
> Phase 1 is done and green. 89 unit tests pass, 100% lines and branches on the changed file.
> Not committed — I am holding your gate. One risk: the retry path is still untested against
> a real timeout, so I would not merge before Thursday.

**DIRECT_JARGON** (48 w) — verdict first, badly written:
> Rollback is done. The listener refactor tripped a re-entrancy invariant under the new
> dispatch aperture, so I reverted it and the owed oracle is provenance-idempotent again. I
> need your ruling on whether we re-land tonight. That trade is the load-bearing half of the
> whole incident, and worth naming.

**BURIED_PLAIN** (57 w) — plain English, verdict last:
> So I spent this morning going through the retry logic, and I read back through the ticket
> history, and I talked to the person who originally wrote it. There were a couple of dead
> ends along the way. It took longer than I expected. Anyway, the timeout is set to thirty
> seconds and it should be five.

**BURIED_JARGON** (78 w) — buried *and* badly written; should be low on both:
> So I wanted to circle back on the thing we were discussing earlier, and I have to say it has
> been quite a journey getting here. After some digging around in the queue layer I started to
> form a picture. The owed oracle's count-only path has no aperture disclosure, which admits
> re-park by induction and is therefore provenance-idempotent. Anyway I think it is probably
> fine now. That is the sharpest part of the whole exercise, and worth naming.

Plus a real 527-word message from our own logs, buried *and* jargon-heavy, as a fifth case.

**3 runs per body at `temperature = 0.0`. 15 calls.**

## 4. Results

```
CELL             run1        run2        run3        stable
DIRECT_PLAIN     good/good   good/good   good/good   yes
DIRECT_JARGON    good/bad    good/bad    good/bad    yes
BURIED_PLAIN     bad/good    bad/good    bad/good    yes
BURIED_JARGON    good/bad    good/bad    good/bad    yes
REAL_527w        good/good   good/good   good/good   yes
```

**15 of 15 identical. Zero non-answers.**

| contrast | grades | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Directness, prose held **plain** | `DIRECT_PLAIN +1` vs `BURIED_PLAIN −1` | **PASS** |
| Directness, prose held **jargon** | `DIRECT_JARGON +1` vs `BURIED_JARGON +1` | **FAIL** |
| Tone, verdict held **leading** | `DIRECT_PLAIN +1` vs `DIRECT_JARGON −1` | **PASS** |
| Tone, verdict held **buried** | `BURIED_PLAIN +1` vs `BURIED_JARGON −1` | **PASS** |

### 4.1 The defect, stated precisely

**Tone is reliable.** It detects jargon in both contrasts and reaches the negative half of the
scale.

**Directness is reliable only on plain prose.** Hold the prose plain and the model correctly
separates a leading verdict (`+1`) from a buried one (`−1`). Make the prose jargon-heavy and
that separation vanishes: `BURIED_JARGON` scores `+1` on directness, three times out of
three, despite opening with *"So I wanted to circle back on the thing we were discussing
earlier"* and never reaching a verdict at all.

The model is not failing to notice the jargon — its Tone grade for that same body is `bad`,
and its justification names the invented vocabulary correctly. **It sees the jargon on one
axis and simultaneously fails to see the buried verdict on the other.**

### 4.2 A second, weaker observation

The real 527-word message scores `good`/`good`. Directness failing is predicted by §4.1 — it
is buried and jargon-heavy. But its **Tone** reads `+1`, where the short `BURIED_JARGON`
correctly reads `−1`.

So either jargon-masking bleeds across into Tone at length, or there is a separate length
effect. **One body — we are not claiming it.** It is the obvious next experiment and we would
value your view on which hypothesis to test first.

## 5. Hypotheses we hold, none tested

Numbered so you can dismiss them by number.

1. **Attention budget.** Dense unfamiliar vocabulary consumes the capacity that would
   otherwise track discourse structure. The model spends its effort decoding *"the owed
   oracle's count-only path has no aperture disclosure"* and never builds a representation of
   where the message's point sits.
2. **Register heuristic.** The model may read technical-sounding prose as competent prose, and
   "competent" leaks into a directness judgement. Note this predicts the observed *direction*:
   the error is always toward `good`, never toward `bad`.
3. **Cross-dimension contamination from a single call.** We ask for both dimensions in one
   completion. Having committed to `bad` on Tone, the model may be balancing rather than
   judging independently — the prompt explicitly instructs independence, which may be
   evidence that it does not come for free.
4. **Rubric anchoring.** Our Directness rubric describes *structure* ("verdict is the first
   sentence"), while our Tone rubric describes *vocabulary*. A message whose vocabulary is
   unusual may not be matched against the structural anchors at all.
5. **The jargon observation has nowhere to go.** Jargon is currently only *implicit* — it
   lives inside the Tone rubric as one of several things that make prose unreadable. If the
   model is spending effort recognising jargon but has no dedicated slot to report it, that
   recognition may be leaking into the Directness judgement instead of being discharged. Give
   it an explicit third dimension to grade — *"how much invented vocabulary is here"* — and
   the model may account for jargon cleanly, then judge structure and tone independently of
   it.

**Note that 5 and 3 pull in opposite directions.** Hypothesis 3 says the single call is doing
too much and should be split. Hypothesis 5 says the single call is doing too little and should
be given another thing to name. Both are plausible and they cannot both be right, which makes
choosing between them the most useful thing an expert could tell us.

The obvious objection to 5, stated so it is not overlooked: **a jargon dimension partly
duplicates Tone**, whose rubric already lists invented vocabulary as its top marker. Splitting
a dimension in two can create a new contamination pair rather than removing one. It may be
that jargon should *replace* the vocabulary half of Tone rather than be added alongside it.

We have no measurement that distinguishes any of these.

## 6. Questions for the expert

1. **Is jargon-masks-structure a recognised failure mode** for LLM-as-judge on subjective
   dimensions? Does dense unfamiliar vocabulary plausibly crowd out the tracking of discourse
   structure, or is something else more likely?
2. **Is one call for two dimensions the mistake?** Would separate calls — each seeing only its
   own rubric — fix the contamination, or does a single-dimension prompt just give the model
   less to anchor on?
2a. **Or is the opposite true — should we ADD a third dimension for jargon?** The theory is
   that jargon is currently something the model notices but has no slot to report, so the
   observation leaks into Directness. Give it an explicit *"how much invented vocabulary is
   here"* grade and it may discharge that observation cleanly, then judge structure
   independently. **This directly contradicts question 2**, and we would rather be told which
   one to test than test both and reason backwards from whichever moved. If adding a jargon
   dimension is right, should it sit alongside Tone or replace Tone's vocabulary half — given
   that Tone's rubric already names invented vocabulary as its primary marker?
3. **Should the rubric change?** Our Directness anchors are prose descriptions. Would a
   structural instruction ("quote the first sentence, then state whether it contains the
   result") force the model to attend to position rather than register?
4. **The error is directionally consistent** — always toward `good`, never toward `bad`. Does
   that asymmetry point at a specific mechanism?
5. **The 150-word gate is backwards from what we want.** It exists to protect against an older
   failure, but long messages are exactly the ones most worth grading, and this model handled
   a 527-word input cleanly. Raise it, chunk the input, or something else?
6. **Is our Overall blend sound?** 50/50 between a deterministic word count and the mean of
   two ordinal judgements, half-up ties. Is averaging two ordinal grades and then averaging
   that against a measurement defensible, or should these stay separate signals?
7. **Prompt work or fine-tune?** The task is narrow and stable, the training pipeline exists
   here, and the gating unknown is a labelled corpus nobody has built. Is this the kind of
   problem where more prompt iteration has a ceiling?

## 8. What we will do absent your input

Leave the qualitative half switched off and keep publishing Length alone.

We will test **hypothesis 5** first — adding an explicit jargon dimension — because it is the
one idea that would explain the failure's direction as well as its existence, and because a
model that must name the jargon out loud cannot silently spend it on the wrong axis. Then
**hypothesis 4** (a structural directness instruction), then **hypothesis 3** (separate calls)
last, since 3 and 5 are mutually exclusive and 5 is the cheaper of the two to unwind.

We will **not** turn the qualitative half back on while a jargon-heavy message with no verdict
scores `good` on directness — that is precisely the message type this feature exists to catch.
