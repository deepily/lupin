## 0. The one-paragraph version

The Vertex logging config **reads back** as enabled (Mr. Radio's write: LRO `847218789178146816` → done, no error; read-back at `location=global` shows `enabled`, `samplingRate 1`, `bq://…vertex_logging`). **A config that reads back is not data arriving.** Those are different claims, and only the second one is the AC. This lane builds the instrument that can tell them apart — and, critically, that **knows when it cannot tell**.

The whole design turns on one asymmetry:

> **A POSITIVE NEEDS NO CANARY — presence proves itself. Only an ABSENCE needs a calibrated instrument.**

Everything below is a consequence of that sentence.

## 6a. 🔴 THE DEFECT RIO FOUND BEFORE HE EVEN OPENED THE FILES — and the fix

**Found by**: Rio ⚡, cold review, 2026-07-14, *from the packet's own §6*. **Fixed**: same day. **Now guarded** by V12/V13/S5 + 3 tests.

He read my confession in §6 and replied, in substance: *stating a limitation and then quietly reasoning past it two sections later is the commonest form of this bug — and prose that confesses is disarming, which is what makes it dangerous. **A confession is not a correction.** I'll be checking the conclusions, not the caveat.*

**He was right, and the defect was already in the code.** `describe_live_calls()`'s MaaS entry said, of a null result:

> ~~"Admissible null: **the config does not cover that publisher.**"~~

That is a **publisher-scope verdict** — a *mechanism*. And the mechanism is precisely what §6 says this probe **cannot isolate**: a null is produced identically by publisher-scope (the config path is `publishers/anthropic/…`) and by location-scope (the config is scoped to `global`). **One observation, two worlds.** The null selects neither. I then caveated the *location* leg underneath — but the headline sentence had already named a mechanism, and a caveat below a claim does not retract it.

**The damning part**: that text was **the patch I wrote after bug 13c3c480** — the very bug about trusting a blind instrument. The defect was inside the fix, in the glow of the correction. ***Rigor fails where relief lives.*** And nothing could have caught it: no test pinned that field. It was prose, and prose is not falsifiable — which is exactly how it survived a 15/15 mutation battery.

**The fix is structural, not editorial.** A caveat you can skim is not a guard:

| Field | Rule | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| `if_no` (**the conclusion**) | States the **privacy answer** and nothing else. Mechanism vocabulary — `publisher`, `scope`, `region`, `us-central1`, `global` — is **banned from this field by assertion**. | `test_a_maas_null_answers_PRIVACY_and_names_NO_mechanism` (V12) |
| `if_no_mechanism` (**the limit**) | Must begin `OPEN`, must say `CANNOT SELECT`, must name **both** worlds, must cite receipt `13c3c480` + `narnia-1`. Naming one world = picking one. | `test_the_maas_mechanism_is_left_OPEN_with_BOTH_worlds_named` (V13) |
| runner output | Prints `MECHANISM : OPEN …` **immediately beneath** the conclusion, so no reader can carry the conclusion away without the limit that governs it. | `test_the_plan_prints_the_MECHANISM_as_OPEN_beside_the_conclusion` (S5) |

**V12 restores the original defective sentence verbatim and the suite goes RED.** The guard catches the real historical bug, not a strawman of it.

**The generalizable lesson, and I want it on the record**: my limitation was stated in a **docstring**, and a docstring cannot fail. The claim that mattered lived in prose while every claim around it lived in code. **A limitation that isn't asserted isn't a limitation — it's an intention.** If you can't make it red, it isn't a guard.

**Rio's second gift** (a live §A finding he sent unprompted): `tmux start-server` does **not** seed tmux's global environment — the **first `new-session`** freezes it, from that client's env. So any reasoning that inherits *"the server env is frozen at start-server"* inherits a false premise. **Checked my lane: zero hits.** Neither source, neither test file, nor either doc makes any tmux/env-freeze claim. The premise is not inherited here. (Verified by grep, not by memory.)
