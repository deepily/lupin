## Why the cap reached 800

The cap did not rise on 13 Nov. The INI key `external repo lupin duplicate check ceiling` has said 800 since `9289c5a6c` (11 Nov 18:35). An hourly slot's chunk is `chunk_for( folder, ceiling )`: `3600 * HOUR_FILL (0.8) / s` calls, where `s` is the median seconds per call over the last five runs that made at least 20 calls and did not fail (`status.median_seconds_per_call`), capped at the ceiling. A baseline run gets the chunk minus what the daily run used.

| slot | sizeable runs in the median | seconds per call | chunk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 Nov daytime baselines | answered runs of 581 to 749 calls | about 2.9 to 4.4 (calls per hour 826 to 1,227) | 581 to 749, below the ceiling |
| 11 Nov 21:00 and 22:00 daily runs (329 and 362 calls, 189 and 193 s) | calls per hour 6,279 and 6,737 | about 0.5 to 0.6 | 800, the ceiling |
| 13 Nov 14:00 | 10:13, 11:00, 12:00 (0.443, 0.434, 0.467 s per call, empty-handed), 13:00 (2.91), 13:20 (5.10) | median 0.467 | 3600 * 0.8 / 0.467 = 6,167, so 800 |

So the chunk reached the 800 ceiling because runs that got nothing back finish in a fraction of a second per call, and the median treats them as a fast model. The first such runs in the history are the daily runs of 11 Nov evening (21:00 and 22:00 measured; the 19:00 and 20:00 runs were not looked at). On 13 Nov the three empty-handed morning runs made it true again, and the 14:00 baseline was the first run that was handed the whole 800. The arithmetic was done by hand from `runs.jsonl`; `chunk_for` was read, not run. The ceiling itself is a per-run limit with nothing above it, so 12 hourly slots at 800 allow 9,600 calls a day.
