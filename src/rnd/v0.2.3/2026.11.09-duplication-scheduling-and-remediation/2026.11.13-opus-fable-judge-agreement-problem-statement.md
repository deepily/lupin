# Problem statement: sample size for Opus-vs-Fable judge agreement on code duplication

Nov 13, 2026 · @Richard P Ruiz

## The question

How many code pairs must both Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 judge, side by side, to estimate their agreement on code duplication with a useful 95% confidence interval? Every accuracy figure we hold for our duplicate checker was measured with Fable as the judge. For the next month the judge must be Opus, because Fable is too costly to clear our backlog of about 5,500 unjudged definitions. We need to know what Opus rulings are worth before we act on them, and we want to spend as few Fable calls as possible finding out.

## What the judge does and how it is scored

The judge makes one model call per new code definition and rules on each of its retrieved candidates. Retrieval (embedding similarity plus structural matching) picks the candidates; the model only judges them.

- **Unit of judgment:** a pair, meaning one new definition and one existing candidate. One call covers all candidates of one definition, about 10 on average, so pairs are clustered within calls.
- **Scale:** four ordered verdicts: distinct, related, near-duplicate, duplicate. A **flag** is near-duplicate or duplicate; everything downstream uses the binary flag.
- **Blindness:** names that could reveal the answer are scrubbed before the call.
- **No temperature control:** the judge runs through Claude Code, which has no temperature setting, so two runs of the same input can differ.
- **Proof:** a closed pool is judged twice. The flags are scored against human labels for recall ("found") and precision ("correct"), and the two passes are compared with each other ("agreement"). The bars are 0.80, 0.80 and 0.90.

## What we measured with Fable as the judge

With Fable 5.1 as judge, the official proof of 31 October 2026 passed all three bars. It judged 111 new definitions against a pool of 12,313, which gave 1,110 pairs judged in both passes. Human labels cover 222 pairs; reviewers marked 21 of them unsure.

| Measure | Result | 95% interval | Bar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Found (recall), flags on true duplicate pairs | 72 / 80 = 0.90 | 0.81 to 0.96 | 0.80 |
| Correct (precision), flags that are true duplicates | 127 / 157 = 0.81 | 0.74 to 0.87 | 0.80 |
| Agreement, same flag in both Fable passes | 1,075 / 1,110 = 0.968 | 0.956 to 0.978 | 0.90 |

Recall by kind of true pair: rewrites planted by Fable 45 / 45; work-alikes written by Opus 13 / 15; structural pairs in one large script 10 / 15; neighbour pairs in that script 4 / 5. Without the 21 unsure pairs, recall is 66 / 72 and precision 116 / 144.

We also recomputed Fable's test-retest figures from the proof's ledger, reconstructing 1,120 pairs against the record's 1,110. The flag rate is 0.147 in one pass and 0.152 in the other. **Cohen's kappa is 0.87**, Gwet's AC1 0.96, and exact agreement on all four verdict levels is 998 / 1,120 = 0.89. Across the 2,240 rulings the verdicts split related 1,003, distinct 902, near-duplicate 228 and duplicate 107.

## The backlog, the cost and the fixed decision

The judge will be Opus 5.5 for about a month; that is decided, and this note does not reopen it. Fable access returns in about a month, and we can then re-judge part of the work if needed.

- **Backlog:** 5,470 definitions were still unjudged after the last baseline run (11 Nov 2026, 17:00 EST). New definitions waiting since then add 381 (13 Nov, 10:13 EST).
- **Throughput while Fable was available:** on 11 Nov the baseline runs judged about 6,700 definitions in about 600 to 740 calls an hour.
- **Why the judge stopped:** since 18:00 EST on 11 Nov every judge call has returned no tokens, so nothing has been judged.
- **Price per token (list):** Fable 5.1 is $10 input / $50 output per million; Opus 5.5 is $4 / $20, so 2.5 times cheaper. Our runs use a fixed-price subscription, so these are notional prices; the binding limit is how much Fable access we have.
- **Notional cost of a Fable call:** about $0.17 on average over all runs, and $0.12 to $0.31 for baseline runs on 11 Nov.

## Questions for the expert

1. **Which measure should we estimate?** About 15% of pairs are flagged, so Cohen's kappa runs well below raw agreement (Fable against itself: 0.87 against 0.968). Should we report kappa on the binary flag, weighted kappa or Krippendorff's alpha on the four levels, Gwet's AC1, or positive and negative agreement given separately?
2. **What sample size, for what precision?** What 95% half-width on that measure is reasonable for a go or no-go on Opus rulings: ±0.10, ±0.075 or ±0.05? Is the large-sample formula in the next section adequate, or do we need the goodness-of-fit sizing of Donner and Eliasziw, or of Rotondi and Donner?
3. **How should we handle clustering?** Pairs are nested in definitions, about 10 to a call. Should the sampling unit be the definition, and what design effect should we plan for? Would a cluster bootstrap be acceptable for the interval?
4. **Simple or stratified sample?** Flags are rare. Should we oversample pairs that either model flags and weight back, or sample definitions at random from the backlog?
5. **Agreement or accuracy?** Agreement between two models does not say which one is right. We already hold 222 human-labelled pairs. Should the main measure instead be Opus's recall and precision against those labels, compared pair by pair with Fable's (for example with McNemar's test), with the backlog side-by-side as a second check?
6. **What is the ceiling?** Fable agrees with itself at kappa 0.87. How should an Opus-to-Fable kappa be read against that: as a ratio to it, or by comparing one Opus pass with one Fable pass against two Fable passes?
7. **Re-judging later.** When Fable returns, can we re-judge only a sample of the Opus-judged backlog and correct estimates over the whole backlog, for example with prediction-powered inference? How big must that sample be?
8. **Decision rule.** What result would let us keep Opus rulings as they stand, and what result should send them back for Fable re-judgment?

## Our preliminary numbers, for the expert to check

If pairs were independent, about 280 to 500 pairs would put Cohen's kappa within ±0.10 to ±0.075 at our flag rate. The table uses the large-sample standard error of Fleiss, Cohen and Everitt (1969), with two raters, a binary flag, equal flag rates and a 95% interval. It counts independent pairs and ignores clustering, which question 3 asks about.

| Flag rate | Expected kappa | Pairs for ±0.10 | Pairs for ±0.075 | Pairs for ±0.05 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.10 | 0.7 | 569 | 1,012 | 2,276 |
| 0.10 | 0.8 | 401 | 713 | 1,603 |
| 0.10 | 0.9 | 209 | 371 | 833 |
| 0.15 | 0.7 | 398 | 707 | 1,590 |
| 0.15 | 0.8 | 281 | 498 | 1,121 |
| 0.15 | 0.9 | 147 | 260 | 585 |
| 0.25 | 0.7 | 266 | 473 | 1,064 |
| 0.25 | 0.8 | 188 | 334 | 750 |
| 0.25 | 0.9 | 99 | 175 | 394 |

A simulation check: at a flag rate of 0.15, kappa 0.8 and 281 pairs, 95% of 4,000 simulated kappas fell between 0.689 and 0.888. That matches the ±0.10 target, slightly skewed low.

Three observations follow, for the expert to confirm or correct:

- **The Fable side is cheap in calls.** One call judges about 10 pairs, so 500 pairs need about 50 Fable calls, about $10 notional. Opus is judging those definitions anyway. Clustering will raise the number of definitions we need, but probably not by an order of magnitude.
- **The human-labelled proof costs no new Fable calls.** Re-running it with Opus as the judge takes about 224 Opus calls. On 80 true pairs, a recall of 0.90 has a 95% half-width of about ±0.07; on about 157 flags, precision has about ±0.06. That is enough to check the 0.80 bars, but probably not to detect a small difference between the two models.
- **The ceiling.** Fable agrees with itself at kappa 0.87. An Opus-to-Fable kappa near that would mean the switch adds little disagreement beyond Fable's own run-to-run noise.

## Constraints on any design

- **Fable calls are the scarce resource.** Opus calls are not. A design that adds Fable calls only on a sample of definitions Opus is already judging fits best.
- **Same prompt, same retrieval.** Both judges see byte-identical prompts and candidate lists. Only the model id changes, and the reasoning-effort setting must be fixed and recorded for both.
- **Runs are not deterministic.** Without temperature control, one model disagrees with itself on about 3% of flags, so a single pass per model carries that noise.
- **The backlog is not the proof pool.** The proof pool was built to contain known duplicates, whereas backlog definitions are what the daily check finds, and their flag rate may differ from 15%.
- **Time.** The sample must finish in days, so it can steer the month of Opus judging rather than review it afterwards.

## Sources

**Published work (opened)**

- Rotondi MA, Donner A. [A confidence interval approach to sample size estimation for interobserver agreement studies with multiple raters and outcomes](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22560852). J Clin Epidemiol 2012;65:778–784. Sizes a study from target lower and upper limits on kappa.
- [kappaSize R package manual](https://cran.r-universe.dev/kappaSize/doc/manual.html) (Rotondi). Its CIBinary function implements interval-based sizing for a binary outcome with 2 to 6 raters.
- Sim J, Wright CC. [The kappa statistic in reliability studies: use, interpretation, and sample size requirements](https://academic.oup.com/ptj/article/85/3/257/2805022). Phys Ther 2005;85:257–268. Tabulates sample sizes and discusses prevalence, bias and non-independent ratings.
- Byrt T, Bishop J, Carlin JB. [Bias, prevalence and kappa](<https://doi.org/10.1016/0895-4356(93)90018-v>). J Clin Epidemiol 1993;46:423–429. Recommends reporting prevalence and bias indices beside kappa.
- Wongpakaran N, Wongpakaran T, Wedding D, Gwet KL. [A comparison of Cohen's Kappa and Gwet's AC1](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-13-61). BMC Med Res Methodol 2013;13:61. Finds AC1 more stable than kappa under changing prevalence.
- Buderer NM. [Incorporating the prevalence of disease into the sample size calculation for sensitivity and specificity](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8870764). Acad Emerg Med 1996;3:895–900. Sizes recall and precision estimates from target precision and prevalence.
- Angelopoulos AN, Bates S, Fannjiang C, Jordan MI, Zrnic T. [Prediction-powered inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633). Science 2023;382:669. Gives valid intervals from a small labelled set plus many model predictions.
- Angelopoulos AN, Duchi JC, Zrnic T. [PPI++: efficient prediction-powered inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01453). arXiv 2023.
