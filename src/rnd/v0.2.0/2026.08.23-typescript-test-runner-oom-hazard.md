## What is KNOWN but NOT explained

1. **`GlobalRegistrator.register()` is called with no matching `unregister`.** The test registers a
   full DOM into the global scope and never tears it down. This is *suspicious*, not *proven* — a
   218-line file should not turn that into tens of gigabytes.
2. **There is a family of HANGING Node tests here.** Seat #2 measured `queue_transport.test.ts`
   returning `RC=124` (timeout). A hang under a registered DOM grows until oomd picks the seat.
   **98 of the 119 test files are multiplexer tests**, the family the hangs were found in.
3. **77 of 119 `*.test.ts` files import happy-dom** — measured, not estimated. Quarantining
   `arg_interview.test.ts` therefore **does NOT disarm `npm test`**; 76 other happy-dom tests remain
   in the glob.
4. **The allocator is unidentified.** I looked in the test, the module, the runner flags, the
   dependency size, and the worktree. It is in none of them. **State this as unknown; do not let the
   next reader infer that "happy-dom is the cause" has been established. It has not.**

## Why the per-session ceiling does not save you

`a52d160c` (`MemoryMax=24G MemoryHigh=18G MemorySwapMax=0`) bounds **one runaway's RSS**. It does
**not** bound fleet-wide pressure:

- `systemd-oomd` fires on **PSI pressure against the whole `user@1001.service`**, so a scope sitting
  well under its own 24 GiB ceiling still dies.
- oomd kills **the worst cgroup at that moment — not necessarily the one that caused the pressure.**
  An innocent seat can be the one that goes.
- Our own `MemoryHigh=18G` produces exactly the *reclaim activity* oomd's criterion requires.

**What the ceiling did buy** is real and should not be dismissed: on 08-22 the whole
`tmux-server.service` unit was torn down and ~2 dozen sessions died together. On 08-23, **one scope
died and every other seat survived, twice.** It converts a fleet-wide collapse into a single-seat
one. That is worth having. It is not protection from being the seat.
