## Headline

**Measured on total tokens spent, the rejecting arm does not save tokens — it costs about 7% more.**

The saving everyone has been quoting is real but it is a saving *per message that lands*, and it is
computed over a population that excludes every draft the gate threw away. Put the thrown-away drafts
back in and the sign flips. This was predicted from Tuesday alone on 2026-08-04; it now holds on the
pooled corpus of 1,951 eligible attempts across seven days.

## The two numbers, side by side

Both are legitimate. They answer different questions, and the row that filed this gap
(`35d0a451`) recommended reporting both rather than replacing one. That is what this table does.

- **Published** = mean `est_tokens` over **delivered rows only** — *"how long is a message that lands?"*
- **All-in** = total `est_tokens` over **all attempts** ÷ delivered count — *"what did a landed message cost?"*

## Why the sign flips

Nothing subtle. The rejecting arm burned **75,071 tokens on 270 drafts that were composed, sent over
the wire, and refused**. Those tokens were genuinely spent by the sender — `est_tokens` is stamped on
the draft before the gate returns 413 (`dm.py:869`). The published average simply never looks at them.

The gate also selects for the **longest** drafts by construction, so every refusal discards an
above-average message and pays again for the rewrite. A refusal can never pay for itself on the
message it refuses: blind costs D, rejecting costs D burned plus R rewritten. The arm can only win by
changing behaviour on **future** messages.

**Neither co-primary crosses.** By the script's own pre-registered reading — *"Both move → genuine

**no effect at this dose**, not the "retries, not restraint" I recorded this morning.

I flagged 0.047 as knife-edge and said two more days might move it either way. It moved, on one
afternoon's rows, in the direction that removes the last significant result. Anyone citing p=0.047
from the interim section above is citing a number that a larger sample retracted.

⇒ **The final read on the rejecting arm at this dose: no measurable effect on message length, and it
costs 7.3% more tokens per delivered message.** Those are consistent, and together they are a clean
negative result. The blind-vs-rejecting corpus remains intact and re-analysable — suspension was not
deletion — so a future dose can be tested without re-collecting any of this.

Final per-arm detail: blind 841 attempts / 841 delivered / 0 refused, 228.5 tokens per delivered.
Rejecting 1,159 / 887 / 272, 160.1 published and 245.3 all-in. No rows exist for 08-14 or 08-15,

### Why it came out that way — the gate clips, it does not teach

Added after María 🌸 read the same corpus and found the gated arm cut the long tail while leaving
sentence count alone. Measured here on delivered rows, and her reading holds:

| arm | n | words median | words p90 | words p99 | words max | sentences median | sentences mean | words/sentence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| blind | 841 | 135 | 238 | 421 | **900** | 8 | 8.91 | 17.0 |
| rejecting | 887 | 111 | 143 | 149 | **150** | 7 | 7.37 | 15.1 |

The rejecting arm's distribution simply **stops at the threshold**. Its p99 is 149 and its maximum is
150 — that is not a behaviour, it is the gate's own setting appearing in the data. Meanwhile sentence
count barely moves (median 8 → 7) and sentences get modestly tighter (17.0 → 15.1 words each).

⚠️ **María's corollary, which is the sharper form and belongs to her**: a *delivered* tail under a gate
is the gate's configuration, not the senders' writing, so **delivered tails must never be compared
across a gated and an ungated arm**. Her report keeps its numbers labelled as submitted text with this
delivered cut beside them.

⇒ This is the mechanism behind the statistical result above. A word threshold **truncates the
distribution and forces a rewrite**; it does not make senders write shorter first time. That is exactly
what co-primary B measures and exactly why it did not cross — and it is why the arm pays a rewrite cost
without buying restraint.

## Which is exactly the thing that did not reach significance

From `analyze_arms.py` at the same corpus, 14 matched clock-hour pairs:

| co-primary | what it tests | n pairs | observed | p | crossed? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A — all attempts | did messages get shorter, retries included | 12 | −32.963 | **0.047** | yes |
| B — first attempts only | **genuine restraint** on the first try | 12 | −32.003 | **0.055** | no |

The pre-registered reading is written into the script's own output: *"Both move → genuine restraint;
only A → retries not restraint; neither → no effect at this dose."* Only A moved.

⚠️ **Read that with the caveat it deserves.** 0.047 and 0.055 straddle the line by a hair; this is a
knife-edge verdict, not a decisive one, and two more days of collection may move either. What is
**not** knife-edge is the cost arithmetic: +7.0% is a direct measurement of tokens spent and needs no
causal assumption at all.

Put together: the mechanism that could have made the arm pay for itself is the one that failed to
cross, and the arm's actual cost is a loss. Those two facts point the same way.
