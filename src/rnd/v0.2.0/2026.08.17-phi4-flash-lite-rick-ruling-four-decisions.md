## 1. Fabrication denominator — **NARROW**

```
fabrication_blocked / ( fabrication_blocked + rewritten )
```

Answers: *when this model tries to rewrite, how often does it invent something.*

The wide form — over every body the tutor saw — was rejected. Its defect is that a model which
rarely attempts a rewrite scores well by doing nothing: abstention reads as accuracy.

**Provenance note worth keeping.** The input plan left `DENOMINATOR-TBD` at `:602` deliberately,
and `:610-614` said why in as many words: *"the placeholder exists so anyone wiring the metric
hits it and has to go find the decision. A plausible-looking formula would not have done that."*
The Step-9 handoff then hardcoded the narrow form at `:165` — so an implementer honouring that
doc's own acceptance bar would have closed Rick's open decision by implementation, and the
answer would have been right by accident. Caught pre-build by Tiffany 💍.

## 2. Operational discordant floor — **10**

Below 10 disagreeing bodies we call it noise, regardless of p.

The arithmetic bound is 6 (a 6-0 split gives p=0.031; at 5, p=0.0625 cannot clear no matter how
lopsided). 10 sits above that bound with room for a split that is not perfect.

## 4. Latency is a tiebreaker — **Rick volunteered this; nobody asked**

Verbatim:

> "10 as a discordant floor is just fine but you have to understand even if it had as many
> disagreements — that is, they disagreed or fabricated at an equal rate — if one is 10 times
> faster than the other then I'm going to go with it."

**On a statistical tie, the faster arm wins.**

This changes what the study has to measure, not just what it reports. Two consequences already
pushed to the crew:

- **It is pre-stated.** It arrived before the numbers landed, which is the only reason it can be
  used at all. Applying a speed preference after seeing an inconclusive result would be choosing
  the criterion to fit the answer.
- **Report the ratio, not a verdict on it.** Nobody wires a threshold for "10 times" — the crew
  reports per-arm latency and Rick applies his own rule.
- **The measurement has to be clean enough to carry a decision** (raised to Tiffany): same
  machine, same batching, and no warm-up advantage to whichever arm ran first. If the phi-4 arm
  ran first and paid the cold start, the ratio is contaminated and the tiebreaker is unusable.

Sam's per-arm elapsed stamp — listed as a nice-to-have an hour ago — is now load-bearing evidence.

### 4a. The network objection, and Rick's second ruling on it — **YES, with two conditions**

Within minutes of receiving the rule, Sam reported that the arms are not network-comparable:
**phi_4 answers over the LAN, flash_lite across the public internet**, and the timer wraps the
whole tutor call rather than the model alone. Read as a confound, the tiebreaker would partly be
picking a model for where it happens to be hosted.

**Tiffany's counter reversed my recommendation** (`lupin-app.ini:227`): in production phi_4 *is*
the LAN hop and flash_lite *is* the internet round trip. The question is which model goes into
the DM tutor, and **both would be deployed exactly as measured** — so it is a real operational
number, not an artifact.

Put to Rick as a yes/no. **He ruled YES**, with two conditions, both now in the tool's output
rather than in anyone's memory of a thread (Sam, `80d737f9`):

1. **It is a deployment comparison, not a claim about model speed.** The header says so: a
   different deployment of either model moves these numbers without either model changing.
2. **p90 and p99 per arm**, nearest-rank so every printed figure was actually measured — and the
   reader states out loud that on a small sample p99 collapses onto the maximum, rather than
   implying a tail resolution the sample does not have.

**Tiffany also tightened the rule itself**, and it is accepted: the tiebreaker fires only on a
*powered* tie — b+c ≥ 10 **and** the Wilson interval spanning 0.5. Her earlier proposal to
interleave the arms row by row (A B A B) is adopted for any future run: it costs nothing and
removes the ordering confound, though not the transport one.

### 4b. It may not matter

On the first 8 bodies, measured by Clayton against Sam's banked rows rather than estimated:

| | phi_4 | flash_lite |
|---|---|---|
| median latency | **3.596s** | 5.603s |
| ratio | **0.642** — phi_4 faster | |
| fabrications blocked (of 8) | 1 | **6** |

**The honest arm looks like the slow one.** If that holds at 400 there is no tie to break: the
test decides it outright and the tiebreaker never fires. That ordering is written into the report
payload — *latency may break a tie and never outranks the test* — because a tiebreaker that reads
like a verdict is worse than none. `model_failed` rows are excluded from the latency figure (a
404 is not a measure of how long a model took to think) with `all_fired` reported beside it so the
exclusion stays visible.

Clayton pre-registered both of Rick's numbers at `e2c9dee3` **before** the file landed, and
dry-ran the refusal on the 8-row sample: b=0, c=5, refused at the floor exactly as designed — and
5 is below even the arithmetic minimum of 6, so that sample could never have reached significance.
**An unmet floor yields the refusal, never a loosened floor.**
