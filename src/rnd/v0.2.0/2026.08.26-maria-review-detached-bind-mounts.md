**Scope I was given**: can the WARN arm be misread as a pass, and does the failure text

---

## 0. Verdict

**The diagnosis is right and the message design is better than most shipped code.** Two
findings block the WARN arm as written, one of them safety-relevant. Neither touches §1–§3,
which I would ship as-is.

The core insight — a bind fails by losing its inode, and content divergence is a *later*
consequence — is correct, measured on all 15 binds, and demonstrated end-to-end in §5
including a prediction the author made, watched fail, and corrected. That is the standard.

---

## 1. 🔴 BLOCKING — one of §6's two WARN options is dead, and the other is one line in 148

§6 leaves this open: *"how a WARN surfaces in a pytest tier that only has pass/fail.
Options include `warnings.warn` plus a printed block, or a separate non-blocking probe."*

**I measured both.** Two passing tests, one printing, one warning, run under `-q` as a tier is:

```
..                                                        [100%]
=============================== warnings summary ===============================
test_warnvis.py::test_passes_but_warns
  UserWarning: ARMED: 1 of 15 file binds is severed
2 passed, 1 warning in 0.00s
```

- **The printed block produces ZERO output.** pytest captures stdout on a passing test and
  discards it. An ARMED block printed from inside a passing test is not "easy to miss" —
  it does not exist. That option must be struck, not weighed.
- **`warnings.warn` does surface.** But the unit tier I ran tonight at `b45fa75f` ended
  `18,872 passed … 147 warnings in 886.01s`. **One ARMED line in a bucket of 148.**

⇒ **Answer to the question I was asked: yes.** Under the printed-block option the WARN
*is* a pass with no trace whatsoever. Under `warnings.warn` it is a pass carrying one line
in 148 that nobody scrolls to at 8pm. Neither delivers what §4 designed.

This is the same class the fleet caught four times today — **a control that reports OK
under the condition where it cannot function** — and here it would be self-inflicted at the
surfacing layer, after the detection layer got it right.

**What I would do instead** (author's call, not mine): make ARMED its own tiny test that
**fails**, `xfail`-style or gated behind a marker the tier deselects by default, so the
signal has a place to live that is not the warnings bucket. §6 already names "a separate
non-blocking probe" — that is the surviving option and it should be promoted from
alternative to recommendation.

## 2. 🔴 BLOCKING (safety) — the pasteable remedy omits the precondition its own author honoured

§4b's remedy line is designed to be pasted without thinking. It says:

```
Do this:  docker compose up -d --force-recreate lupin-rest-test
```

But §5 records what the author actually did: *"after verifying `:8000` idle
(`venue_idle --port 8000` → exit 0, every lane empty)"*.

**That check is in the worked example and not in the text that gets pasted.** A recreate
fired while a job is running kills the job. The document's own §4b argues — correctly —
that a verdict making the reader go looking is a verdict that gets ignored; the same logic
says a remedy making the reader *remember* a precondition is a remedy that eventually gets
fired at a live box.

**Fix**: put the idle check in the pasteable block, above the recreate, as one command:

```
Do this:  venue_idle --port 8000 && docker compose up -d --force-recreate lupin-rest-test
```

The `&&` is the point — it makes the precondition unskippable rather than remembered.

## 3. ⚠️ NON-BLOCKING — the frequency argument cuts against the WARN, not for it

§4a establishes: **14 of 30 days** had a commit touching a bind file, so *"something arms
roughly every other day"*, and 12 of the 15 currently-healthy binds are healthy only
because nothing has committed them yet.

§4a offers this as the reason ARMED must warn rather than fail. It is equally an argument
that **ARMED will be present most days**, which is the wallpaper state row `554e5d3e` was
opened to escape — the document says so itself: *"red so often that nobody reads it."*
A yellow nobody reads is the same defect in a different colour.

I am not asking for a redesign. I am saying the one-action-clears property (§4a) carries
more weight than it is given, and it only holds if **someone actually runs the action**.
Worth stating in the doc what the expected steady state looks like — "a name or two showing
most days, cleared by a recreate every week or two" — so the first reader who sees a
standing warning knows that is the design working, not the control rotting.

## 4. ⚠️ VERIFY, do not change on my say-so — inode alone vs device+inode

The check compares `stat -c %i` across the container boundary. Inode numbers are unique
**within a filesystem**, not globally, so in principle `%d:%i` is the sound comparison.

**I did not test whether `%d` is meaningful across that boundary** — a bind mount may well
report a different device inside the container for the same file, which would make `%d:%i`
produce a false ARMED on every bind and be strictly worse than what is proposed.

⇒ One measurement settles it. **Do not adopt `%d:%i` on this note alone.** The §3 census is
evidence that inode-only works correctly on this setup today; I am flagging a robustness
question, not a defect.

## 5. What I am NOT raising

- **§1–§3**: the diagnosis, the mechanism, and the 15-bind census. Correct, measured, and
  the DIVERGED/ARMED split is exactly the right cut.
- **§3a**: pinning the container to `71d5efaa` by md5-matching `pyproject.toml` against
  recent commits — a named pin rather than an estimate, corroborated independently by line
  numbers. That is how a claim should be made.
- **§5's correction**: predicting the mount would clear on commit, watching it not clear,
  and writing *"a commit never reattaches a severed mount"* into the remedy text so the next
  person does not lose the same hour. The correction is worth more than the prediction cost.
- **§6's scope discipline**: no automatic recreate, remedy stays a human action.
- **`MISSING` left alone**: right call, distinct mode, distinct cause.

- Any of the numbers in §3 or §4a. I reproduced neither the census nor the 30-day commit

- Whether the coverage-floor consequence in §3a (`fail_under` 96 vs 98) had a real victim —
  the document does not claim one, and I did not look.
