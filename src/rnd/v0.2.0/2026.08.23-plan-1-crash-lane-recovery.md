### Two corrections to the incident doc's framing

1. **The fleet did not die because the kernel picked unlucky PIDs.** The whole
   `tmux-server.service` unit was marked `oom-kill` and systemd tore down *every* process in
   it — that is why ~2 dozen sessions dropped together, and why in-flight `pytest` runs died.
   The unit-level teardown is the mechanism; the per-session slice from `a52d160c` is
   precisely the right countermeasure.
2. **No transcript on the box supports the "poisoned transcript" hypothesis (H2).** The
   largest `.jsonl` written on crash day is **3.7 MB** — four orders of magnitude below the
   3.8 GB case in the issue H2 leans on. H2 should be **downgraded, not carried forward**.

### The finding that governs this whole plan

**The second crash arrived ~60 seconds after the restore.** Whatever the trigger was, the
resurrection reproduced it almost immediately. A naive "bring them all back at once" replay
is the single most likely way to cause a third event, so the sequencing in Phase 3 is not
ceremony — it is the deliverable.

## Phases

### Phase 0 — Freeze the evidence (do this before anything else)

Nothing here resurrects anything; it makes the rest reversible.

1. `git -C <wt> stash list` and a **bundle** of every non-merged branch:
   `git bundle create io/recovery-2026.08.23/<branch>.bundle <branch> --not <integration>`
2. Copy the 3 modified + 10 untracked files out to `io/recovery-2026.08.23/loose/` **before**
   any checkout, rebase or worktree operation touches them.
3. Record `git -C <wt> rev-parse HEAD` for all 8 worktrees into a manifest.

**Gate**: the manifest exists and the bundles restore in a scratch clone. Until that passes,
no lane is resurrected.

### Phase 3 — Resurrect, one lane at a time, under the new ceiling

The ordering exists because the last restore caused the next crash.

1. **One session at a time.** Bring up a single worker, confirm it is inside its own
   `ccworker-*.slice` (`systemd-cgls --user-unit ccworker.slice`), and let it work for a full
   turn before starting the next.
2. **Watch `memory.current` on the scope**, not the host. A lane that climbs past
   `MemoryHigh` (18 GiB) will throttle and reclaim — that is the early warning the old shared
   slice could not give.
3. **Do not `--resume` any crash-day transcript.** Seed from the memento or the store row.
   The 12:38 event happened a minute after a restore; until the trigger is identified, replay
   is the one action with a demonstrated correlation to a crash.
4. **🔴 CAP EVERY PARALLEL TEST RUNNER, AND ALWAYS WRAP IT IN A TIMEOUT.**
   *(Rewritten 2026-08-23 13:35 after this rule, as originally written, failed to prevent the very
   thing it was for — see the incident note below.)* The original text said "keep `pytest` off the
   critical path," which named ONE runner. The rule is runner-agnostic: `pytest -n`, `node --test`,
   `tsx --test`, `vitest`, `jest` all fan out to CPU-count workers by default, and this box has
   **32 cores**. Cap it explicitly (`node --test-concurrency=4`, `pytest -n 4`) and put a `timeout`
   in front of every run. Land the work first; run suites afterward, deliberately, one at a time.

### Incident, 2026-08-23 13:02:50 — Mr. Radio's session killed during this very recovery

`systemd-oomd` killed `run-r8d9a4fce….scope` inside `ccworker-cc_tmux_session_5ed54994.slice`,
taking **52 processes**, *"due to memory pressure for /user.slice/user-1001.slice/user@1001.service
being 74.67% > 50.00% for > 20s with reclaim activity."*

**Three things this establishes:**

1. **The containment WORKED.** One scope died. Mine, Cheech's, Cheech's reviewer, and Clayton's all
   survived — where yesterday the whole `tmux-server.service` unit was torn down together. The
   per-session slice from `a52d160c` converted a fleet-wide collapse into a single-seat one.
2. **The killer was `systemd-oomd`, not the kernel and not `MemoryMax`.** oomd watches *PSI memory
   pressure on the parent* `user@1001.service` and kills the worst cgroup under it. Our own
   `MemoryHigh=18G` produces exactly the reclaim activity oomd's criterion requires — so the ceiling
   shapes *which* seat dies, it does not prevent the kill.
3. **The trigger was an uncapped Node test runner**: `./node_modules/.bin/tsx --test
   src/tests/unit/shared/arg_interview.test.ts`, run with no `timeout`, on 32 cores. An earlier
   attempt in the same lane *did* carry `timeout 180`; the re-run dropped it.

**Nothing of his was lost** — his Phase 0 freeze (manifest + all loose files) completed at 12:59,
three minutes before the kill, and his transcript is intact and resumable.

#### ⚠️ Do not read "containment already shipped" as "you are covered" (Cheech, 2026-08-23)

`a52d160c` bounds **one runaway's RSS**. It does **not** bound fleet-wide memory pressure, and those
are different failure modes:

- `systemd-oomd` fires on **PSI pressure against the whole `user@1001.service`**, so a scope sitting
  comfortably under its own 24 GiB ceiling still dies.
- The seat oomd kills is the **worst cgroup at that moment — not necessarily the one that caused the
  pressure.** An innocent lane can be the one that goes.

⇒ The per-session ceiling changes a fleet-wide collapse into a single-seat one. That is worth having,
and it is not protection from being the seat.

#### Runner note: the fan-out cap is not universal (Cheech, 2026-08-23)

In the Python lanes here, `pytest-xdist` is **not installed** and `pytest.ini`'s `addopts` carries no
`-n`, so the full unit tier runs single-process and `-n 4` is not even available to get wrong.
**In those lanes the thing to police is the missing `timeout`, not the fan-out.** Cheech's scoped runs
all carried `timeout 900`; his full tier went out under `nohup` with none, and finished in ten minutes
by luck rather than by discipline. Both halves of the rule matter, and which half bites depends on the
runner.

### Phase 4 — Attribute the trigger (bounded)

The incident doc's forensic steps stay unexecuted. Now that the ceiling exists, the next event
is survivable, so wire capture rather than hunting the last one:

- On the 16 GB watcher crossing, snapshot `/proc/<pid>/fd`, `smaps_rollup`, and `status`.
- `fd` is the tiebreaker: a pipe to a Bash child ⇒ H1; a large `.jsonl` ⇒ H2 (now unlikely,
  given 3.7 MB is the crash-day maximum); a socket ⇒ H3/H4.
