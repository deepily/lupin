## 1. The defect

Live, 2026-08-14 03:51:21, Mr. Radio → María, captured verbatim in the corpus:

> **Sent**: "lupin's working tree still holds 7 modified files, so tonight's 72 commits
> undercount by whatever is in them."
> → the 72 **are counted**; the undercount is the 7 uncommitted files.
>
> **Delivered**: "The roll-up undercounts by 72 commits plus whatever is in the 7
> modified files."
> → the 72 are now the **missing** amount.

María had just published the daily roll-up with those 72 commits in it. As delivered, a
peer appeared to be telling her the number was wrong. She asked whether the line was
mine — that question is the only reason this was caught.

**Why `_fabricated_facts` is blind to it**: nothing was invented. Both numbers (72, 7)
appear in the input. What changed is which clause a quantity is bound to. That guard
compares *sets of values*; it has no notion of binding.

---

## 2. The three candidate predicates, priced on real traffic

Measured against every rewrite pair in the corpus — not assumed, the same discipline the
fabrication guard used.

| Rule | Blocks | Catches the inversion |
|---|---|---|
| R1 — refuse any altered sentence carrying a numeral | **104 / 197 (54%)** | yes |
| R2 — refuse on ANY scope word gained before a quantity | **5 / 197 (2.5%)** | yes |
| R3 — refuse on a **ledger marker** gained before a quantity | **1 / 197 (0.5%)** | yes |

R1 is unusable: paraphrasing is the tutor's entire job. R2's four extra blocks were each
read in full and are benign compressions — *"Section D **is** plan sections 7, 9, 10"* →
*"Section D **includes** plan sections 7, 9, 10"*. **R3 ships**: it refuses the real
inversion and nothing else in a day's traffic.

The ledger vocabulary is a closed set: `by plus minus not no never without missing
undercount(s|ed) exclude(s|d|ing) dropped lost short`.

**Answering the row's third question** — *are there other inversions of this shape in the
corpus?* No. R3 finds exactly one across 197 numeric pairs, and the wider net's four
extras were read and cleared.

## 3. Two defects found by controls, not by reading

Both were in my own measuring instrument, and both would have shipped a number that
looked like a finding.

1. **The tokeniser split hex row ids.** `0c4e8cfa` became a `0`, `41333974` read as a
   quantity. The first run reported 8 corpus hits; **6 were id fragments**. A count says
   how many, only reading says whether they are the right things.
2. **The quantity regex excluded a trailing `"."`**, so every **sentence-final** quantity
   was invisible to the guard while every mid-sentence one was seen. The suite was green.
   What surfaced it was a **branch that never ran** — the `continue` arc for "a quantity
   the original never had" could not fire, because the test's `400.` was not being
   matched at all.

## 5. Known limit, stated rather than glossed

The ledger vocabulary is closed, so this catches **the shape that occurred**, not every
re-scoping. A rewrite that inverts a meaning without one of those markers passes
untouched. That is a deliberate trade for a guard that costs 0.5% of real traffic and
will therefore still be switched on next month.
