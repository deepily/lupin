## Result — does the verdict survive removing the artifact?

Three variants, same payload, one pass each. All clear the pre-stated discordant floor of 10.

| variant | phi_4 | flash_lite | b (flash only) | c (phi only) | b+c | McNemar exact p | Wilson |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V0** — as shipped | 67 | 130 | 93 | 30 | 123 | **1.08e-08** | 0.756 (0.673–0.824) |
| **V3-naive** — any dictionary word exempt | 24 | 40 | 27 | 11 | 38 | **0.0139** | 0.711 (0.552–0.830) |
| **V3-strict** — common words only | 30 | 54 | 38 | 14 | 52 | **0.0012** | 0.731 (0.598–0.832) |

**The verdict holds in all three.** Significance weakens as the sample of disagreements shrinks —
which is arithmetic, not a change of direction. Flash-Lite is flagged more often whether or not the
capitalised-word class is in play.

This is a genuinely different result from Tiffany's sensitivity check, which **deleted** the name
class entirely and got p = 0.42. **Narrowing the class is not the same intervention as removing it**,
and the two answer different questions.

*Cross-check*: every p above was computed twice — once by hand (exact two-sided binomial) and once
through the study's own scipy-backed `mcnemar_exact`. Agreement to all 16 significant digits.

## 🔴 A defect in V3 as specified — proper nouns are in the dictionary

`/usr/share/dict/american-english` carries **20,494 capitalised entries**. Every fleet persona name
is one of them:

```
rachel IN DICT   clayton IN DICT   krishna IN DICT   tiffany IN DICT   rick IN DICT
```

So **V3 implemented against the plain word list stops flagging invented PERSON names** — the guard's
founding purpose, the "there was no reviewer" incident. Measured: `Rachel`, `Clayton`, `Tiffany's`,
`Krishna's` all fall out of the blocked set under V3-naive and come back under V3-strict.

**V3-strict** — exempt only entries that are lowercase in the word list — fixes it and still
delivers the drop Rick asked for: **flash_lite 130 → 54 (−58%)**, **phi_4 67 → 30 (−55%)**.

Residual quirk worth naming: `Rick` still falls out, because *rick* (a stack of hay) is a genuine
lowercase common word. A short fleet-name allowlist closes that; a dictionary alone cannot.

## What this does and does not establish

**Establishes**: this guard refuses Flash-Lite's rewrites roughly twice as often as Phi-4's, and
that gap is not an artifact of capitalised common words. Flash-Lite is ~1.77× faster as deployed.

**Does NOT establish**: that Flash-Lite invents more facts. The guard is a proxy with, on this
corpus, **one** demonstrated true positive. Converting "flagged more" into "fabricates more" still
needs the blind hand-labelled sample, which needs a person.
