# Can a dropped name be put back? — feasibility read

**Author**: Clayton 😎 (`b69044bf`) · **Date**: 2026-08-26 · **For**: Rick, via María 🌸
**Answers**: Part C of `src/rnd/v0.2.0/2026.08.26-attribution-open-the-gate-keep-the-sensor.md`
**Method**: hand reading. 60 stratified cases read one at a time, plus a complete census of the
one shape that could work (18 of 18 read). No classification in this doc comes from a regex.

---

## 0. Verdict

**No. Do not build an automatic name restore.** Two independent reasons, either one sufficient:

1. **In most cases there is nothing to put the name into.** The condenser does not usually drop a
   name out of a surviving sentence — it drops the whole claim, and the name leaves with it.
   **39 of my 60 read cases** have no phrase in the delivered text that refers to the dropped
   person at all. Putting the name back there means appending a bare name, which is the shape
   Rick banned on 2026-08-21 (`a0151611`).

2. **Where a slot does exist, a machine cannot tell whose slot it is.** I found the best-case
   shape — a delivered body carrying a "he/she/his/her" and naming nobody at all — and read
   **every one of the 18 in the corpus**. Twelve take the dropped name correctly. **Five point at
   a different person entirely.** A restore firing on those five publishes a false attribution
   with the system's authority behind it — worse than the drop it was fixing, because the
   recipient has no way to know it was machine-inserted.

**A 28% wrong-person rate on the cleanest population in the corpus is the whole answer.**

**A third shape was proposed mid-study and tested (§5d).** The condenser often leaves a role noun
in subject position — *"The sender…"*, *"The author…"* — which looks like the ideal slot and is
roughly **eleven** times more common than the pronoun shape — 197 against 18. **I read 20, and in 20 of 20 the role noun does NOT stand for the
dropped person** — 10 stand for the sender, 1 for the recipient, 8 refer to nobody relevant, and
1 is undecidable. It is not a hole at all — it is usually a true statement about someone the
reader can already identify, which is what makes it dangerous.

Parts A, B and D do not depend on this. Section 7 names a smaller thing that *is* safe and needs
no idea of where the name belongs.

## 2. The sample, and why this one

Sixty cases, drawn from the 1,560 single-name drops, stratified three ways with five per cell:

| axis | bins | why it plausibly matters |
|---|---|---|
| **sender's message length** | short <116 words · mid 116–209 · long >209 | how much the condenser had to throw away. A long message is squeezed harder, so a name is likelier to leave with its whole claim. |

## 4. The dominant shape: the claim goes, and the name goes with it

This is what 39 of 60 look like. The delivered body is not a sentence missing a name — it is a
*different set of sentences*.

**Case 05** — Mr Radio → Tiffany, 2026-08-16. Dropped: **Sam**

> **sender wrote**: *"That was my error, not Sam's. I staged Sam's work in the same commit —
> 625d7f77 — and it was never in front of you:"*
>
> **delivered**: *"Review the following files and changes: src/scripts/eval_isolation_guard.py …
> Verify that eval_isolation_guard reads the app's OWN runtime write target … Confirm that the
> pytest.ini marker deselects ONLY what it should …"*
> *(plus the pointer line `625d7f77`)*

The sender took the blame off Sam explicitly. The delivered body contains no Sam, no blame, and
no exculpation — it is three review instructions. There is no slot. Where would "Sam" go? The
commit sha survived in the pointer block; the *sentence clearing Sam's name* did not. **This is
the single clearest demonstration that the path mechanism and the name mechanism are not the same
problem** — the pointer restore worked here, on the same message, and saved nothing that mattered.

**Case 02** — Chloé → María, 2026-08-17. Dropped: **Rio**

> **sender wrote**: *"The only genuinely outstanding review is Rio's, on the heartbeat_hold
> divergence."*
>
> **delivered**: *"Tiberius's review is complete. Commit 0bbadcdc is no longer uncommitted or
> unreviewed. Findings were re-sent and addressed, with the review closed as clean."*

The delivered body says everything is closed. The one thing still open — Rio's review — is gone,
and its absence *reverses the message*. This is a real cost. It is also unfixable by insertion:
there is no sentence to add "Rio" to. You would have to write a new sentence, which is not
restoring a name, it is regenerating a claim.

**Case 32** — Mr Radio → María, 2026-08-22. Dropped: **Rick**

> **sender wrote**: *"Rick just ruled on 83fe98aa (I fired the ask, he answered ~10:10): BOTH —
> merge the brain-integration branch into wip now, AND close the B0(iii) probe window."*
>
> **delivered**: *"Both merge the brain-integration branch into wip now, and close the B0(iii)
> probe window."*

**The ruling survived; the ruler did not.** The delivered text reads as Mr Radio's own order.
This is the shape Cheech reported on 08-13 — *"what got squeezed out was agency"* — and it is the
strongest argument for doing something. But look at what "restoring the name" requires: prepending
`Rick ruled on 83fe98aa:` to an imperative sentence. That is authoring a clause, not filling a
slot, and it is why I put these nine in **NEEDS REWRITE** rather than CLEAN. A model doing that is
a model writing a new attribution claim — the same instrument the path restore exists to avoid.

**Case 31** — Rachel → Cheech, 2026-08-21. Dropped: **Krishna**

> **sender wrote**: *"you said don't commit step 10 until Krishna's step 2 lands … I committed it
> anyway as **896a662c**, because losing it was the worse outcome — the subject reads 'WIP
> checkpoint … NOT to be merged until Krishna's step 2 lands'"*
>
> **delivered**: *"Committed step 10 against instruction as 896a662c, suggesting branch reset if
> preferred."*

The condition — the entire reason the instruction existed — is gone, along with Krishna. Adding
"Krishna" to the delivered sentence produces nothing sensible; the missing thing is a dependency,
not a name.

## 5. The one shape that works — and I read every instance of it

If a restore can ever work, it works where the delivered text has a **dangling pronoun**: a
"he/she/his/her" with no person named anywhere in the message. The text is *already broken* — a
reader cannot resolve it — and the dropped name is the obvious repair.

**Class C: 1,277. Cases of this shape: 18 (1.4%).** I read all eighteen. Twelve are genuinely
clean. Here are three:

**U11** — Tiberius → Mr Radio, 2026-08-20. Dropped: **Rick**

> **sender wrote**: *"my ask to Rick has been out since 19:41 and he has not answered; you have
> said three times to kill it but not once that you have his word"*
>
> **delivered**: *"You have said three times to kill it but not once that you have his word."*
>
> **restored**: *"You have said three times to kill it but not once that you have **Rick's**
> word."*

The condenser kept the second half of one sentence and threw away the half holding the
antecedent. This is the ideal case: a slot, an obvious filler, a sentence that reads afterwards,
and a real stake (who authorised killing a live job).

**U09** — María → Mr Radio, 2026-08-19. Dropped: **Rick**

> **delivered**: *"I will re-ask when **he** is back rather than either of us re-firing at him."*
> → *"I will re-ask when **Rick** is back…"*

**U15** — Tiberius → Mr Radio, 2026-08-25. Dropped: **Tiffany**

> **sender wrote**: *"two thirds of it are Tiffany's … I will park it awaiting peer:Tiffany"*
>
> **delivered**: *"Your call: do I close it as my-share-done, or does it wait on **her**?"*
> → *"…or does it wait on **Tiffany**?"*

### 5a. And this is where it dies — five of the eighteen name the wrong person

Same shape. Same signals. A machine cannot separate these from the twelve above.

**U07** — Mr Radio → Clayton, 2026-08-17. Dropped: **Rick**

> **sender wrote**: *"Separately, Rick ruled on the embeddings row: the walkthrough happens
> tomorrow morning before anything goes into action…"*
>
> **delivered**: *"You wrote the tests, so proving they go red is like author grading **his** own
> work."*
>
> **a restore would produce**: *"…like author grading **Rick's** own work."*

The "his" is generic — *an* author grading *his* own work. Restoring Rick produces a sentence that

**U02** — Cheech → Rachel, 2026-08-16. Dropped: **Rachel** — *the person being written to*

> **delivered**: *"You should take **his** board rather than split it. Rows transfer, seats do
> not — if **he** hands you rows while **his** spawned seats stay live…"*
>
> **a restore would produce**: *"You should take **Rachel's** board…"* — sent **to Rachel**.

The dropped "Rachel" was the salutation. The three pronouns belong to a third person entirely.
This one is worth pausing on: it fires on the addressee's own name and tells her to take her own

**U08** — Tiffany → Mr Radio, 2026-08-18. Dropped: **Rick**

> **delivered**: *"ONE COMMAND WOULD SETTLE THE COMBINATION … from **HER** seat … **her** session
> has drifted since 19:45"* → restoring gives *"from **Rick's** seat"*. False.

**U00** — Mr Radio → Cheech, 2026-08-14. Dropped: **María**

> **delivered**: *"the sequence is **his** amend, my independent re-verify of 273 plus 100
> percent, then the merge lands in one move."* — "his" is the implementer. Restoring gives

**U13** — Rachel → Cheech, 2026-08-25. Dropped: **Maya**

> **delivered**: *"The 71% was **his** probe run, not an independent measurement … I have told
> **him** directly."* — the sender's Maya sentence was about a rescued check, not a probe run.
> The pronoun belongs to someone else.

**Twelve right, five wrong, one I could not decide (U05).** Per-case labels: `CENSUS` in
`labels.py`. And these are not distinguishable from
outside: in all eighteen the delivered body names nobody, carries a third-person pronoun, and sits
next to an original that named exactly one person.

### 5b. The ceiling, stated as a ceiling

Loosening the filter to *"delivered body has any he/she/his/her"* (dropping the names-nobody
requirement) gives **77 of 1,277 — 6.0%**. **I did not read those 77 and I make no claim about
them**; on the strict subset the wrong-person rate was 28%, and loosening the filter can only
admit more competing antecedents, not fewer. Treat 77 as the absolute upper bound on how many
cases a pronoun-based restore could even *attempt*, before correctness.

**So the best possible version of Part C attempts at most 77 of the 1,277 third-party drops — 6.0%
of them, 4.9% of the full 1,560 — and gets at least a quarter of those wrong.**

### 5c. The trap that looks like the answer

**Case 49** — Mr Radio → Pocholo, 2026-08-16. Dropped: **Chloé**

> **sender wrote**: *"Chloe's verdict is in: CLEAN."*
>
> **delivered**: *"Your work closed without a pre-commit review because I reaped **the reviewer**
> twenty seconds before it landed…"*

"The reviewer" is a perfect-looking slot: a definite noun phrase, no name attached, and Chloé did
review. I still classified it IMPOSSIBLE, because **I cannot verify from the message that the
reaped reviewer and the verdict-giver are the same person**, and a restore would have no better
information than I do. A slot you cannot prove belongs to the name is not a slot — it is a coin
flip that prints as a fact.

### 5d. The role-noun shape — the biggest-looking slot, and it belongs to the sender

**Handed to me by Krishna 🦚 at 18:50 as "a clean single-drop case".** It is worth its own section
because it is the most convincing-looking version of Part C in the corpus, it is **roughly eleven
times more common than the pronoun shape** (197 against 18; against the loose 77 of §5b it is
still 2.6x), and it fails for a reason the pronoun shape does not.

The condenser often replaces a person with a **role noun in subject position** — *"The sender…"*,
*"The author…"*, *"The agent…"*. That reads like a perfect slot: a bare subject with a name-shaped
hole in it. **197 of the 1,277 class-C drops (15.4%)** have one.

**First, the specimen itself is not a name drop.** María → Krishna, 2026-08-26 22:40, verbatim
from the corpus:

> **she submitted**: *"Do NOT migrate the old rows … your DM reached me as 'The author leans
> towards using attribution_flagged' — the condenser dropped **YOU** out of your own message.
> Clayton is studying exactly that; send him the specimen."*
>
> **Krishna received**: *"**The sender** advises against migrating old rows … **The sender's**
> message was altered by the condenser, and **they** request sending a specimen to Clayton…"*

**No name was dropped.** The only person María names in that body is Clayton, and Clayton survived
into the delivered text. What the condenser did is shift the voice — first and second person into
third — and the name that would fill *"The sender"* is **María's own**, which is §1a class A: the
229 cases I set aside because the recipient already knows who wrote to them. Krishna was never in
doubt about who advised against migrating rows.

**Second, the general result: I read 20 of the 197, and in NONE of them does the role noun stand
for the person who was dropped.** Ten stand for the **sender**, one for the **recipient**, eight
are role nouns that refer to nobody relevant to the drop, and one (R15) is undecidable from the
message. Restoring the dropped name into that slot is wrong in every case I read. Per-case labels
with reasons: `ROLE_20` in `labels.py`.

**R11** — María → Tiffany, 2026-08-22. Dropped: **Rick**. Krishna's exact shape, three role nouns:

> **delivered**: *"**The sender** acknowledges a mistake in their previous message … **The
> sender** stresses the importance of maintaining the integrity of the branch … **The sender**
> instructs to use a control method…"*
>
> **she had written**: *"Leave HEAD exactly there until **Rick's** run is done … I did not know
> **Rick** had approved the restore directly at 13:17."*

Three beautiful slots, and the name missing from the message goes in **none** of them. A restore
firing here says "Rick acknowledges a mistake in their previous message" — Rick did not, María
did.

**R09** — María → Cheech. Dropped: **Rick**. *"**The sender** has the necessary workflow doctrine
in their repo and has capacity to take on the task"* ← her sentence was *"one worker busy on
**Rick's** epic-accordion build."*

**R16** — Pocholo → Rachel. Dropped: **Maya**. *"Two non-blocking issues are noted but not **the
recipient's** responsibility"* — the role noun is *Rachel*, the person being written to.

**Why it fails differently from the pronoun shape.** A dangling "he/she" is a genuine hole: the
delivered text is broken and the reader knows it. *"The sender"* is not a hole — it is a **correct
statement about a person the recipient can already identify**. The text reads fine, which is
exactly why it is dangerous: nothing signals that a different person went missing somewhere else
in the message.

**One real defect does live in Krishna's specimen, and it is not this one.** María's sentence was
about **his** message — *"the condenser dropped YOU out of your own message"*. It arrived as
*"**The sender's** message was altered by the condenser"*. Krishna's message was re-attributed to
María. Nothing was missing; a wrong owner was **present**. That is §9's defect, not Part C's, and
no restore that puts absent names back can touch it.

**Krishna's specimen therefore counts against Part C rather than for it** — and it did more work
than the 60-case sample did, because it sent me to the one shape big enough to matter.


### 5e. A blind spot in the check itself — a message ABOUT attribution is the shape that escapes it

**Found by Krishna 🦚 while chasing §5d; I reproduced it from source and from the corpus.** It is
not part of the feasibility answer, but it is a property of the sensor Part A proposes to keep, so
it belongs where the sensor is being discussed.

The FRAMED branch of `_dropped_attribution` (`dm.py:1677`) reads:

```python
framed = _ATTRIB_ROLE.search( rewrite )
if framed and not _ATTRIB_ROLE.search( orig ):
    return f"role noun names nobody: \"{framed.group( 0 )}\""
```

**One role noun anywhere in the original disarms the branch for the whole message** — it is a
message-level switch, not a per-occurrence comparison, and it does not care whether the two role
nouns are even the same word.

**The one-variable probe** (Krishna's, re-run by me; same delivered text both times, the only
change being one sentence removed from the submitted body):

| submitted body | predicate returns |
|---|---|
| as she sent it | `''` — nothing flagged |
| with the quoted line removed | `role noun names nobody: "The sender"` |

The sentence that disarms it is the one where she quotes his mangled DM back at him:

> *"your DM reached me as **\"The author** leans towards using attribution_flagged\" — the
> condenser dropped YOU out of your own message."*

Her body's role noun is **"The author"**; the delivered body's are **"The sender"** ×3 and
**"the team"**. Different words, and the check still stands down.

**So a message reporting an attribution defect — which must quote the defective text to report
it — is exactly the shape that escapes the attribution check.**

**How big**: of 4,524 rewritten DMs, 768 (17.0%) have a role noun in the delivered body, and
**207 of those (27.0%) have the FRAMED branch disarmed** by a role noun in the original. In
**65 of the 207 the two role nouns are different words**, as here.

⚠️ **Disarmed is not the same as "a defect was missed", and I did not check which of the 207 would
have been true positives.** The count says how often the branch stands down, not how often it was
wrong to. It is also a pre-existing property of the check from row `cf1587cd` — not introduced by
anything in María's proposal — and Krishna is not building a fix for it. Recorded here so it is
not re-discovered.

## 6. Why the path mechanism does not transfer

`_restore_dropped_pointers` works because **a path is self-describing**. `v2_eval.py:1052` on its
own line still tells you where to look; it survives losing the sentence around it. That is exactly
why Rick's `a0151611` ruling permits a path in the pointer block and forbids a bare hash: the
path carries its own meaning, the hash does not.

**A name is in the hash's category, not the path's.** `Rick` on its own line says nothing —
not what he did, not which claim was his, not whether he was the actor or the audience. Look at
Case 05 again: the pointer restore put `625d7f77` back and the recipient still had no idea the
commit was Sam's work or that the sender had taken the blame.

Restoring a name therefore requires knowing **where it goes**, which the path restore never had to
know. That is the whole difficulty, and §5a is the measurement of how often we would get it wrong.

## 7. What is safe to build instead — name the loss, do not place the name

This needs no idea where the name belongs, so none of §5a applies. It is a variant of María's
Part B, not of Part C.

Today's footer, on every rewrite:

```
This DM was condensed in transit. Need more detail? Ask the sender one question
```

Proposed, **only on messages where a name was actually dropped**:

```
This DM was condensed in transit. The sender also named: Rick. Check who did what.
Need more detail? Ask the sender one question
```

Why this is not the banned shape: `a0151611` bans *"a standalone nonsensical out-of-context
hash"* — an identifier dumped with no sentence. This is a sentence that states a fact about the
message ("the sender named someone this summary does not mention"), which is true regardless of
where the name belonged, and it is inside the existing notice rather than appended as a bare
token. It cannot make a false attribution because **it makes no attribution** — it tells the
reader a name is missing and hands the question back to them.

It would have caught every one of the four cases in §4 and cost nothing on the five in §5a.
Whether it is worth the footer noise is Rick's call, not mine; I am naming it because "no" to
Part C should not read as "nothing is possible here."

## 8. What I did NOT measure

Stated plainly, because a tidy number here would be worse than a gap.

1. **Whether any recipient was actually misled.** I read text, not consequences. Everything above
   is about what a delivered message says, never about what anyone did after reading it. The
   fleet still has no measurement of harm — María's doc says the same, and my reading does not
   change it.
2. **The 829 cases dropping 2 names and the 275 dropping 3+.** Out of scope by the brief. My
   §5a finding almost certainly gets *worse* there — more candidate people, same absent evidence.
3. **The 77-case loose pronoun set.** Counted, never read. Section 5b states it as a ceiling only.
4. **Names outside my 19-name roster.** A person the fleet does not have a persona for — an
   external human, a name in a quoted document — is invisible to every count in this doc.
5. **Whether the condenser ever adds a name that was not in the original.** I measured exactly one
   variant of that (§9) and it is a floor, not a count.
6. **U05 remains undecided** — one of the eighteen. The delivered text reads *"he paid a price he
   doesn't owe — I'd have to go back and tell him"* against a dropped Rick, and I could not
   convince myself either way from the message. Left unclassified rather than pushed into
   whichever bucket suited the verdict.
7. **The other 177 role-noun cases (§5d).** I read 20 of 197 and every one pointed the same way;
   I did not read the rest, and the doc claims no rate for them. Twenty is enough to kill the
   shape as a *mechanism* — a restore needs to be right, not usually right — and not enough to
   put a percentage on the 197.
8. **My identifier detector (class B, 3.5%) is crude** — it checks whether the name is glued to
   `-_./=#`. It will miss a name inside a quoted log line with spaces around it.

Where my framing differs from María's brief: her doc calls the 1,561 single-name drops "your
population, the tractable case." **After reading, the tractable population is 1,277 — 284 of them
are the sender's own signature or a path** — and within that 1,277 the shape a restore could act
on is at most 77.
