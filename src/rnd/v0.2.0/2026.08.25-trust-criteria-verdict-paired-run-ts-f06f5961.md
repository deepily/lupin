### How far off is it? — and the trap inside that question

Krishna computed the sensitivity: the deferred work would have to be **>= 3415.3 ms** to bring
the gate down to its bar, i.e. v2's p50 would have to reach **3998.2 ms**.

🔴 **That figure invites the arithmetic withdrawn below, and it must never be quoted without
this sentence.** The obvious next move is to reach for a deferred-work estimate and compare —
and one is lying right there in the two-era table: 3440.0 ms inline against 1042.0 queued, a
difference of roughly 2400. Plug it in, get *"2400 < 3415, so v2 still wins after
correcting"*, and you have subtracted an inline-era number from a queued-era one across two
runs, two days, two server states and n=60 against n=20. **3415.3 says how much it would TAKE.
We have no defensible estimate of what it IS.**

**Both edges travel together, always** (Krishna's rule, adopted): *an invalid comparison that
passes by a wide margin cannot be called immaterial on account of the margin, because the
margin is computed by the same broken instrument.* A large number from a mis-aimed measurement
is not more reassuring than a small one — it is just larger.

### Why this changes what "readable" requires

The closing section asks for a v2 pass that polls to terminal. That was right for the weaker
reason — recovering cache and degradation figures. The stronger reason is this: **polling to
terminal is what makes both arms stop at the same event.** Without it the primary number is not
weak, it is invalid.

**Ruled on row `a2e360f8`**: build the poll; do not flip the INI key back to inline. If queued
is how v2 ships, an inline run buys valid numbers about a system users will not get.

### A withdrawal, recorded rather than quietly superseded

Two derived "deferred work" figures were published and **both are withdrawn**: ~2400 ms (mixed
the cold pass into both sides) and ~1909 ms (warm-to-warm, but still across two runs, two days,
two server states, and n=60 vs n=20). Krishna withdrew both himself. His own note on why the
second was worse is the durable lesson: **correcting 2400 to 1909 made the method look careful
while resting on exactly the same mismatched pair.** A refined wrong number is more dangerous
than a crude one, because the refinement signals care.

**What survives, and needs no subtraction** — a fact about the *instrument*, not about v2:
