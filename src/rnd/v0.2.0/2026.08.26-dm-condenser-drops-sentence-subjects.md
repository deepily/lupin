## 1. What the condenser actually does

Read before anything was theorised, which is the row's first instruction.

```
POST /api/dm/send                       src/cosa/rest/routers/dm.py
  └── _apply_dm_tutor( body_text )                      dm.py:1249
        ├── _count_claims( body )                       dm.py:1225  ← deterministic, no model
        │     └── sentences.count_sentences()           dm_tutor/sentences.py:419
        ├── if claims <= 4:  deliver the original untouched
        └── if claims  > 4:  rewrite_dm( body )         dm_tutor/agent.py:191
              │                 phi_4, prompt at src/conf/prompts/agents/dm-tutor.txt
              ├── _restore_dropped_pointers()           dm.py:756   ← guard 1, the prior art
              ├── _invented_id_labels / _strip_…()      dm.py:1152  ← guard 2
              ├── _fabricated_facts()                   dm.py:937   ← guard 3
              ├── _rescoped_quantities()                dm.py:1050  ← guard 4
              └── deliver rewrite + DM_TUTOR_NOTICE     dm.py:711
```

Five things follow from the read, and none of them were guessable from outside:

1. **The trigger is deterministic and the rewrite is not.** `count_sentences` is
   hand-written code precisely because "LLMs do not count well" — but everything after
   the trigger is one call to a small local model (`dm_tutor/phi_4`), and nothing
   checks what the model did to the sentence's subject.

2. **The prompt never mentions the subject.** `src/conf/prompts/agents/dm-tutor.txt`
   asks for "the most important declaration or question", two supporting statements and
   one path, in "direct and to-the-point conversational English". Nine `Requirement:`
   lines govern paths, identifiers, string literals and commentary. **Not one of them
   says who a claim is about must survive.** Dropping the subject is not the model
   disobeying the prompt; it is the model doing what the prompt asks.

3. **The non-condensable mechanism is real and already shipped — four times.** Paths are
   lifted out of the model's reach and put back (`_restore_dropped_pointers`, Rick's
   ruling `a0151611`); invented type-nouns on ids are stripped; invented facts are
   refused; quantities moved across a ledger are refused. The docstring on guard 1
   states the design rule in one line: *"Asking the model to reproduce a path verbatim
   is a request that fails silently and only in the cases that matter. Lifting the
   pointers out of the model's reach and putting them back is a guarantee."*
   **Attribution is simply not in that set.** So the row's framing is right: this is
   extending a ruled mechanism to a second class of element, not inventing one.

4. **Every guard is fail-closed toward the sender's own words.** A refusal delivers the
   original. That is the shape any new guard has to take.

5. **The corpus is a ready-made paired fixture.** `_persist_dm_row` (dm.py:483) writes
   `body` (what the sender submitted) and `delivered_body` (what the recipient got) on
   every row, so measurement needs no reconstruction.

## 2. The measurement

### 2.1 The definition, written before anything was counted

> A condensed DM has **lost the subject** when a reader holding only the delivered text
> can no longer recover **who a claim is about**, and the sender's original text made
> that recoverable.

Three countable signals; a pair is subject-lost if any fires.

| | signal | what it catches |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | attribution dropped | the original binds claims to a person — a first/second-person pronoun in subject position, or a fleet persona name — and the delivered prose carries no person at all |
| **B** | role-noun substitution | the delivered prose says "the developer", "the agent", "the reviewer" — a role that names nobody — where the original never used that frame |
| **C** | invented command | the delivered prose issues a bare imperative whose verb the original never used, turning a report into an order |

**Deliberately NOT counted, and reported separately:** "the sender" / "the author" /
"the writer" decode to the sender every time, so they are a change of voice rather than
a loss of subject. Counting them would inflate the rate with cases a reader can still
resolve. They occur in **439 of 4,457** condensed DMs (9.9%).

**A stated blind spot:** attribution **inversion** — the delivered text names a person
and names the *wrong* one. Two of the first fifty pairs are this shape
(`Cheech → maya`: maya set the standard and maya was being instructed, delivered says
Cheech did both; `maria → Cheech`: Cheech caught three issues, delivered credits María).
No signal above can see it. **The rate below is a floor, not a total.**

### 2.3 The rate

**75 pairs read and hand-labelled by eye against the definition above** — 50 to
calibrate the automatic check, then 25 held out and labelled only after the check was
frozen.

| | pairs | subject-lost | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| calibration set | 50 | 13 | 26% |
| **held-out set** | **25** | **9** | **36%** |
| **combined** | **75** | **22** | **29.3%** (95% CI 20.2 – 40.4%) |

⇒ **Between one in five and two in five condensed DMs lose the subject of a claim.**
On the 4,449 rewritten DMs in the corpus that is roughly 900 – 1,800 messages.

### 2.4 Agreement between the hand labels and the automatic check

| | calibration (tuned on) | **held-out (the honest number)** |
|---|---|---|
| agreement | 44/50 = 88% | **18/25 = 72%** |
| precision | 0.73 | **0.60** |
| recall | 0.85 | **0.67** |

**The automatic check is a weak instrument and the rate above does not rest on it.**
Run over all 4,457 pairs it flags 2,018 (45.3%) — signal A 1,877, C 199, B 128 — but at
0.60 precision and 0.67 recall on data it was not tuned against, that figure corroborates
the order of magnitude and cannot sharpen the digit. The hand-labelled 29.3% is the
measurement; the sweep is the cross-check.

Of the 22 hand-labelled losses, signal **A** caught 14, **B** 3, **C** 1, and **5 were
missed entirely** — two inversions and three where a person-word survived elsewhere in
the message while the load-bearing attribution went.

## 3. The fix

### 3.1 "Condense better" was tried, measured, and is not enough

Bar item 3 says the fix is probably not "condense better" and asks for that to be argued
rather than assumed. It was argued **against the live model**, not on principle.

Two arms over the same real DM bodies, both scored by the same detector so its errors
land in both columns and cancel in the delta:

| arm | prompt | subject-lost |
|---|---|---|
| A | the shipped `dm-tutor.txt`, verbatim | **40/80 = 50.0%** |
| B | the same, plus three `Requirement:` lines: keep the subject of every claim · never replace a person with a role noun · never turn a statement into an order or an order into a statement | **29/80 = 36.2%** |

Discordant pairs: **19 fixed by B, 8 broken by B.** McNemar exact, two-sided:
**p = 0.052** — short of the conventional bar, so a second, disjoint batch of 140 bodies
was run rather than reporting a suggestive result as an answer.

| pooled, 220 bodies | subject-lost |
|---|---|
| arm A, shipped prompt | **110/220 = 50.0%** (95% CI 43.4–56.6) |
| arm B, keep-the-subject rules | **85/220 = 38.6%** (95% CI 32.4–45.2) |

Discordant **48 fixed by B, 23 broken by B**; McNemar exact two-sided **p = 0.004**.
(Batch 2 alone: A 70/140, B 56/140, p = 0.049.)

⇒ **Telling the model to keep the subject helps — significantly — and does not settle
it.** An 11-point cut that still leaves **nearly two in five** condensed DMs
unattributable. That is
the same reasoning the shipped path already applies to paths, in its own words: *"Asking
the model to reproduce a path verbatim is a request that fails silently and only in the
cases that matter."*

**No prompt change is shipped here.** The evidence now supports one — p = 0.004 over 220
bodies — but it is a change to a prompt Rick tuned, and it lands in the same decision as
the refusal cost below, which is already in front of him. The measurement is recorded so
both halves can be taken together, on numbers.

### 3.2 What shipped: a fifth guard, and it REFUSES

`_dropped_attribution( original, rewritten )` in `src/cosa/rest/routers/dm.py`, called
from `_apply_dm_tutor` after the fabrication and re-scoping checks.

🔴 **Why refuse rather than restore — the whole design decision.**
`_restore_dropped_pointers` restores because **a path is self-contained**: it still says
where to look with the sentence around it gone. **A subject is not.** Appending "María"
on a line under three sentences does not tell a reader which sentence it belongs to — it
produces exactly the bare-token line Rick banned for hashes in `a0151611`, one class
over. The three guards that *cannot* repair — invented facts, re-scoped quantities,
unreachable id labels — all deliver the sender's own words instead. Attribution belongs
with them, so the half of the ruled mechanism being extended here is the **refusal** half.

Two conditions, either of which refuses:

| | fires when |
|---|---|
| **DROPPED** | the original points at people ≥ `min persons` times and the rewrite points at none |
| **FRAMED** | the rewrite introduces a role noun that names nobody — "the developer", "the reviewer", "the sender" — where the original used no such frame |

**Regex, not a parser, deliberately.** The measurement instrument uses spaCy; this does
not, because a 50 MB model loaded at import would be a new dependency on the send path
for a check the other four guards make with `re`. The cost is stated rather than hidden:
on the 75 hand-labelled pairs the shipped predicate agrees **83%** of the time at **0.71
precision, 0.68 recall** — better than the spaCy instrument's held-out 72/0.60/0.67, and
with no new dependency. Every false fire costs exactly one thing: the sender's own
message goes out uncondensed.

**The threshold is measured, not chosen.** Across the same 75 pairs: `min 1` → 0.62
precision, `min 3` → 0.71, `min 5` → 0.75 at half the recall. Shipped at **3**.

**Config**, both read per send so a dial moves with no bounce:

```ini
dm tutor attribution guard enabled         = True
dm tutor attribution guard min persons     = 3
```

Persona names come from the **live** `cc session voice persona pool`, not a hardcoded
list — a hardcoded roster would stop recognising a real name the day somebody joins and
would start refusing that person's DMs.

**Corpus schema 4 → 5**, adding `tutor_attribution` (the refusal reason, in words) and
the `attribution_blocked` outcome. A null must mean "the reader could attribute this
one", never "this row predates the guard".

## 5. Why the rate doubled — row `4fd86a21`

Filed by María out of the reconciliation in §3.2a, and her framing is why it outranks the
cost question it came from: a cost you can price is a decision, but **a quality metric
moving under you with no cause identified** means any threshold chosen today is a
threshold against a moving number.

Three of her interventions shaped everything below and none of them were mine:

1. **Measure the drop rate conditional on person-density** — a denser corpus mechanically
   offers more subjects to drop, so a raw rise cannot separate a traffic change from a
   condenser change.
2. **The whole day-by-day trend is checker-derived** — check the checker against human
   labels before anybody acts on it.
3. **Do not pool the labelled sets** — they were drawn under different designs and measure
   different things.

Early = 08-13 → 08-19, late = 08-21 → 08-26.

### 5.1 The traffic DID change — and it does not explain the rise

| period | rewrites | median persons | median claims | median words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| early | 2,592 | 6.0 | 7.0 | 132.0 |
| late | 1,824 | 8.0 | 9.0 | 196.5 |

DMs got denser and half again as long, so the confound is real. **It is not the cause:**

| persons in original | early n | early | late n | late | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | 376 | 10.4% | 141 | 2.1% | **−8.2** |
| 3–5 | 741 | 53.2% | 389 | 73.3% | **+20.1** |
| 6–9 | 837 | 37.8% | 599 | 60.3% | **+22.5** |
| 10–14 | 461 | 34.7% | 429 | 53.4% | **+18.7** |
| 15+ | 177 | 29.9% | 266 | 54.5% | **+24.6** |

The only bucket that falls is 0–2, where the dropped-attribution branch cannot fire at all
below a threshold of 3 — structure, not signal.

Length is the same confound one level over and dies the same way. Holding length **and**
person-density both fixed, **13 of the 16 cells with ≥25 rows in each period rose**, by
+5.4 to +32.8 points; the three that fell are the 0–2 person bucket again.

### 5.2 There is no step at any commit — so it is not a code regression

Every corpus row carries the git sha its server booted at. A code cause would step at one
boot boundary. It does not; it climbs smoothly across **26 separate boots**:

```

a51be609  554 rows  31.4%  08-17        e1ffa089   92 rows  53.3%  08-24
1c75e979  301 rows  34.2%  08-17        3725c81f  403 rows  57.3%  08-25
5e71ce27   97 rows  44.3%  08-18        b0b07bb9  119 rows  60.5%  08-25
d8352857  190 rows  53.2%  08-20→21     21d0e283  230 rows  61.3%  08-25→26
```

**The named suspect accounts for almost none of it.** The V3-strict fabrication flag
(08-18) does exactly what it was measured to do — the fabrication-blocked share of fired

worse: among late rewrites, the ones a non-strict guard would have blocked lose the

removing them moves the late rate by **1.2 points of a ~20-point rise**.

Real, measured, and a rounding error against the thing being explained. The row warned
against letting the two named commits become the answer by repetition; this is what

### 5.4 The trend is NOT a tool artifact — checked by hand, blind

Everything above is the frozen checker's output, and the checker is weak (0.60 precision
on held-out data). So: **40 fresh pairs, 20 from each period, shuffled together with the
dates hidden from the labeller, labelled by eye, and only then revealed.**

| | n | **hand-labelled lost** | checker | agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| early | 20 | **15%** | 35% | 70% |
| late | 20 | **55%** | 70% | 85% |

**Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.019 on the hand labels**, against p = 0.056 for the checker
on the same forty. ⇒ The trend is real, and the checker was **understating** it.

**This stratified 40 is the estimate.** The two earlier hand-labelled sets are context and
**are not comparable to it** — they were drawn from the whole pool with no period
stratification, so their early and late halves differ in density and length as well as in
date, and a difference between them cannot be attributed to the period:

| set (context only — different design, not comparable) | early | late |
|---|---|---|
| first 50, unstratified | 8/31 = 26% | 5/19 = 26% |
| holdout 25, unstratified | 5/15 = 33% | 4/10 = 40% |

⚠️ They are recorded rather than dropped because they disagree with the estimate, and a
disagreement deleted is a disagreement hidden. **Direction: solid, and now human-confirmed.
Magnitude: one stratified sample of 40, which is an estimate and not a settled number.**

### 5.5 What actually separates the periods: the bodies

The decisive cut — take real bodies from both periods and re-condense them **with the same
model, the same prompt, at the same moment today**. Whatever still differs is a property of
the bodies; whatever moved against what was delivered at the time is the environment.

| body vintage | n | lost WHEN SENT | lost RE-RUN TODAY |
|---|---|---|---|
| early | 123 | 34.1% | **43.1%** |
| late | 91 | 57.1% | **59.3%** |

**Late bodies are intrinsically 16 points harder to condense without losing the subject —
43.1% against 59.3%, Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.027** — and §5.1 already ruled out length
and person-count as the reason. Something else about how the fleet writes now makes
attribution harder to carry, and naming it is what this row still owes.

|---|---|
| lost the subject **when they were actually sent** | 99/259 = **38.2%** |
| lost the subject **re-run today** | 127/259 = **49.0%** |
| discordant | **48 worse today, 20 better** |
| McNemar exact, two-sided | **p = 0.0009** |

**The same message, through the same prompt, loses its subject ten points more often today
than it did two weeks ago.** And §5.2 already established there is no step at any repo sha
across 26 boots — so whatever moved is **not the code in this tree**. The remaining
candidates are the model server at `:3001` and the model itself, and neither is something
this corpus can settle.

### 5.7 The answer, in two halves

The rise is **not** traffic shape (§5.1), **not** a repo code change (§5.2), **not** a
shift in who DMs are about (§5.3), and **not** a tool artifact (§5.4 — human labels
confirm it and the checker was understating it). It is two real things at once:

| | effect | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **The bodies got harder** | late bodies lose the subject 16 points more often than early ones **when both are condensed at the same moment** | 43.1% vs 59.3%, Fisher p = 0.027 |
| **The condenser got worse** | identical early bodies lose the subject 10.8 points more often today than when they were sent | 38.2% → 49.0%, McNemar p = 0.0009 |

⚠️ **What is still owed, and it is not answerable from this corpus:** *why* late bodies are
harder, when length and person-count are ruled out; and *what* changed outside this repo to
make the same input condense worse. The second is the more actionable of the two — a model
endpoint that silently degrades is a dependency nobody is watching — and it wants an
instrument pointed at `:3001` rather than another pass over the corpus.
