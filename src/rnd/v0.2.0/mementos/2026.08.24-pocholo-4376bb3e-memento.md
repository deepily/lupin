## DEFECT FIVE IN THE KILL PATH — a NEW shape, not the tree-reach bug

Sixteen processes were resident from `test_the_kill_LEAVES_NO_SURVIVORS`: 3 hogs at 2.1 GB each
plus 13 chain processes — three complete chains at depths 4, 6, 6. **The test asserts no
survivors and PASSES.**

**Every chain ROOT had PPID 1281 = `systemd --user`. They had been REPARENTED**; their PGID still
pointed at a long-dead pytest group.

⇒ `_jstest_descendants` finds processes **by parentage**, and an orphan has no parent to be found
by. **Deepening the fixture past depth 6 would not have caught this** — the link was cut before
enumeration began. Depth was never the variable.

**THE FIX, in the order that matters:**
```bash
setsid "$@" &            # 1. the TARGET must OWN a group…
target_pgid=$!           #    …before killing a group is safe
kill -9 -"$target_pgid"  # 2. THEN kill THAT group, never the ambient one
```
🔴 **Half of this is a self-kill, not a smaller fix.** Measured: today the backgrounded child
inherits the **watchdog shell's own PGID**, so a group kill without the `setsid` first takes down
the watchdog and its caller.

⚠️ **What a group kill still cannot do**: a child can call `setsid()` itself and leave the group
exactly as it can leave the tree. Group membership is **better evidence than parentage, not
proof**. Belt and braces = group-kill ∪ descendant-walk.

⚠️ **The test needs a NEGATIVE**: "no survivors" passes just as well when *everything* died,
including the watchdog. It must also assert the watchdog and its caller are **still alive**.

All sixteen killed by **explicit named PID, deepest-first** — never a pattern (row `cd332d2b`).
