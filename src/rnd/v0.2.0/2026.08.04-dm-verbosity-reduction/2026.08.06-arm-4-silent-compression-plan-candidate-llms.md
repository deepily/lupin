# Model Selection for Faithfulness-Preserving DM Rewriting

**Task:** compress agent-to-agent DMs, strip jargon, and suppress intra-ecosystem neologisms and memetic drift, without introducing content absent from the source.

**Priority order:** faithfulness > token cost > latency. No jurisdictional constraints. Open-weight and frontier APIs both in scope.

**Local incumbent:** Phi-4 14B (vLLM, dual RTX 4090).

**Compiled:** 2026-08-06. All pricing and benchmark figures date-stamped; both move fast.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Three conclusions, in descending order of consequence.

**No hosted model has public evidence of beating Phi-4 14B on grounded-summarization faithfulness.** Phi-4 14B posts a 3.7% hallucination rate on Vectara's HHEM leaderboard. Only two candidates in the entire online field are lower: GPT-5.4-nano (~3.1%) and Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite (3.3%). A 0.4–0.6 point delta measured on a 7,700-document news and enterprise corpus is not a defensible basis for switching. The case for a hosted arm is context headroom, throughput without consuming 4090 VRAM, and capability ceiling on hard de-jargoning judgments — not fidelity.

**The discriminating axis is coverage, not hallucination.** Phi-4's 3.7% comes with an 80.7% answer rate, among the lowest in the leaderboard's top tier. Its end-to-end success rate is therefore ~77.7%, against ~95%+ for models that answer nearly everything. See §4 — this is the single most important finding for a pipeline where every DM must yield an output.

**Turn reasoning off.** Multiple 2025–2026 studies show thinking modes amplify summarization hallucination 2–3×. DeepSeek-R1 is ~4× worse than V3. Grok-3 → Grok-4-Fast (reasoning) went 5.8% → 20.2% on HHEM. Reasoning tiers are simultaneously more expensive (thinking tokens bill as output) and less faithful. This applies to Phi-4-reasoning variants as much as to hosted reasoning tiers.

## 2. Empirical evidence base

### 2.1 What each benchmark measures, and its limits

**Vectara HHEM Hallucination Leaderboard** (github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard) — the closest public proxy to the "don't introduce content not in the source" constraint. Feeds ~7,700 documents to each model, prompts a grounded summary at temperature 0, scores factual consistency with HHEM-2.3 (commercial; open variant HHEM-2.1-open, 110M params, on HuggingFace). Reports hallucination rate = 100 − factual consistency, plus an answer rate. Current dataset last updated 2026-05-11.

*Limits:* news and enterprise prose, not agent DMs. Short reference summaries. HHEM is itself an imperfect detector. Models can improve their standing by abstaining — hence always read the answer rate alongside the headline number. Vectara has begun supplementing HHEM with FaithJudge, a few-shot LLM-as-judge.

**FACTS Grounding** (Google DeepMind / Kaggle, arXiv:2501.03200) — 1,719 examples (860 public / 859 private), documents up to 32k tokens, three-judge ensemble (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) with two-phase eligibility-then-grounding scoring. The ensemble exists specifically to mitigate same-family self-preference, measured at ~3.2 points.

On the FACTS Benchmark Suite (Dec 2025), Gemini 3 Pro leads overall (68.8), but on the *Grounding* slice Gemini 2.5 Pro (74.2) beats Gemini 3 Pro (69.0) — direct evidence that newer and larger is not reliably more faithful to source. All models score below 70% overall.

*Limits:* long-form Q&A grounding, not compression or rewriting.

**FaithBench** (NAACL 2025, arXiv:2410.13210) — human-annotated challenging summarization hallucinations across 10 LLMs / 8 families. GPT-4o and GPT-3.5-Turbo produced the fewest; Command-R and Mistral-7B the most; Claude-3.5-Sonnet produced many "benign" hallucinations. Critically, **even the best automated detectors reach only ~50% accuracy on hard cases** — which is the empirical argument for running an ensemble rather than a single metric.

*Limits:* older model set.

**SummHay / Summary of a Haystack** (Salesforce, arXiv:2407.01370) — long-context summarization scored on Coverage and Citation, and it includes a *conversation* domain, the closest public analogue to DM threads. Each Haystack is ~100 documents / ~100k tokens. Even oracle-retrieval systems trail human performance (56%) by 10+ points; without a retriever, GPT-4o and Claude 3 Opus scored below 20%.

*Relevance:* if you batch many DMs into one long context, expect coverage loss. Prefer per-thread windows.

**Plain-language adaptation (PLABA / APPLS, TREC)** — directly relevant, because the task is rewrite-with-meaning-preservation rather than pure compression. PLABA found the LLM era shifted errors from grammatical to *factual*, but that the most fluent systems were also rated most factual; top systems matched or exceeded human faithfulness and completeness on TREC 2023 Task 1. APPLS (arXiv:2305.14341) provides a perturbation testbed isolating faithfulness from simplicity and coherence — a usable template for a bespoke eval.

**Reasoning-vs-faithfulness literature (2025–2026)** — Vectara (R1 ≈ 4× V3); PhantomBench; "The Reasoning Trap" (arXiv:2510.22977), which finds the effect is method-agnostic across RL, distillation, and native thinking toggles.

**Arena scores** — LMArena and Arena-Hard-Auto track style and helpfulness preference, not faithfulness. They should not influence this decision.

### 3.2 Cost mechanics

- **Prompt caching.** A fixed rewriting rubric reused across every DM is the dominant input-token cost. Anthropic gives 90% off cache hits; OpenAI ~10% of standard rate on cached input; Google offers context caching; Mistral bills cached prompt tokens at 10%. This can change the real cost ordering substantially.
- **Batch APIs.** OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Mistral all offer 50% off asynchronous batch. If DM rewriting is not real-time, this halves the hosted arm's cost.
- **Thinking tokens.** Billed as output on reasoning tiers. Combined with the faithfulness penalty, a second independent reason to disable reasoning.

### 3.3 Provider-level quantization

Quality is priority #1, so precision matters and most hosts do not disclose it. FP8 typically costs 0.5–2% on standard benchmarks, usually imperceptible for summarization. INT4 costs 5–15% perplexity and hits smaller models harder. One 2026 study (arXiv:2601.00282) found faithfulness specifically degrades under quantization more severely for small models.

Practical consequence: a hosted endpoint and a local bf16 deployment of the same weights are **not the same artifact** for evaluation purposes. Pin the provider and the precision, and re-run the eval if either changes.

### 3.4 Structured output

Tam et al., "Let Me Speak Freely?" (EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2408.02442) documented significant reasoning degradation under strict format constraints — secondary literature quantifies it at ~10–15%, up to 30% under the strictest settings. A 2026 replication (Lee et al., arXiv:2604.13006) found closed-weight models largely resist the format tax while open-weight models do not.

The literature is largely about grammar-constrained JSON decoding. Tag-delimited XML emitted as ordinary tokens is considerably gentler. Recommendation: generate the rewritten prose free-form and wrap it programmatically rather than asking the model to satisfy both constraints in a single decode.

## 4. The Phi-4 14B answer-rate problem

This is the most decision-relevant finding in the document.

### 4.1 What the metric means

Vectara feeds each model ~7,700 documents with a prompt to summarize using only the document's content. Some fraction of the time a model does not produce a usable summary — it refuses, returns empty output, produces something degenerate, or otherwise fails to comply. The **answer rate** is the percentage of documents for which it produced a scorable summary. The hallucination rate is computed **only over that answered subset**.

So Phi-4 did not hallucinate on 3.7% of 7,700 documents. It hallucinated on 3.7% of the ~6,200 documents it chose to answer, having declined roughly 1,500.

### 4.2 Why this matters

**The selection is not random.** The documents a model bails on are plausibly the hard ones — ambiguous, badly formatted, contentious, thin on extractable content. Those are also the documents where it would most likely have confabulated. Abstention removes precisely the cases that would have damaged the score. The model is graded on a subset it selected for tractability.

**The end-to-end number is much worse.** For "what fraction of submitted documents come back as a faithful summary," the two rates multiply:

```
Phi-4 14B:   0.807 × (1 − 0.037) = 0.777   →  77.7%
Flash-Lite:  ~0.99 × (1 − 0.033) = ~0.957  →  ~95.7%   [answer rate unverified]
```

A 0.4-point hallucination advantage is swamped by an ~18-point availability deficit.

### 4.3 Why the framing differs for this pipeline

For QA or RAG, abstention is defensible — "I don't know" genuinely beats a confident fabrication. For a rewriting pipeline it is not. Every DM must come out the other side. A refusal or empty return is a pipeline failure requiring a retry, a fallback model, or a passthrough of the unprocessed original. If jargon-dense threads are what trigger the failure, the system fails on exactly the traffic it exists to handle.

### 4.4 Immediate action

Before any faithfulness measurement, run Phi-4 over a few hundred real DMs and count non-outputs, truncations, and degenerate returns. If the rate is low on your data, the leaderboard's answer rate reflects Vectara's corpus rather than your domain and can be discounted. If it is high, that settles the question independent of any fidelity metric.

### 4.5 Other Phi-4 characteristics

- **Synthetic-data training profile.** Phi is trained heavily on synthetic data — strong at benchmarks and format-following, weaker on factual breadth, creative writing, and multilingual tasks. For grounded compression with strict format adherence this is an asset, not a liability. The weakness is open-domain factual recall, which this task does not exercise.
- **Context.** Phi-4 14B is 16K (Phi-4-mini is 128K). Verify against your build. 16K binds if you want cross-thread windows for drift detection rather than per-thread rewriting; it is ample for single threads.
- **Family successors.** Phi-4-reasoning and Phi-4-reasoning-plus (14B, trained on o3-mini traces) and Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B (March 2026) exist. All are the wrong direction here per §1 — plain Phi-4 is faster and better suited to format-following and constrained rewriting.

## 5. Evaluation protocol

Public leaderboards measure grounded summarization on news and enterprise prose. They do not measure de-jargoning, neologism suppression, or agent-DM register. Treat every ranking above as a prior, not a verdict.

### 5.1 Corpus

150–300 real DM threads spanning the full range of jargon and slang phenomena, including the pathological cases. Oversample threads that are dense in ecosystem-native coinage — those are both the target workload and the likely failure mode.

### 5.2 Axes

**Availability (primary discriminator).** Non-outputs, truncations, degenerate returns, and refusals as a first-class failure class. Do not drop these from the denominator — that is the error the leaderboard makes.

**Coverage / omission.** Pre-annotate salient propositions per thread; score recall. SummHay-style.

**Faithfulness.** Ensemble of HHEM-2.1-open (110M, free), MiniCheck (the 770M FT5 variant reaches GPT-4-level fact-checking at ~400× lower cost — Tang, Laban & Durrett, EMNLP 2024), and AlignScore. Add RAGAS faithfulness for a claim-decomposition view. Disagreement among the three is itself signal, and given FaithBench's ~50% detector accuracy on hard cases, keep a human-adjudicated slice.

**Constraint adherence (bespoke).** Build a jargon/neologism lexicon from the ecosystem, then score:
- residual banned-term rate in outputs
- **newly introduced terms not present in the source** — the "don't substitute new jargon" check, which nothing public measures

### 5.3 Judge panel

Three heterogeneous judges, majority vote, reporting Krippendorff's alpha for inter-rater agreement. Spot-check ~50 items against human labels to calibrate.

**Contamination flag:** if Phi-4 14B serves as both rewriter and judge-panel member, exclude it from the panel for this experiment. Generator/judge weight sharing produces self-preference inflation — precisely why FACTS Grounding uses a three-family ensemble.

### 5.4 Configuration controls

- temperature 0
- reasoning/thinking disabled on every arm
- free-form output; wrap in XML programmatically rather than in-decode
- pin provider and precision on every hosted open-weight arm; re-run if either changes
- calibrate detector thresholds on your labeled slice rather than using defaults (t=0.5) — aggressive rewriting produces faithful paraphrases that naive thresholds flag as unfaithful

### 5.5 Decision thresholds

- Phi-4 non-output rate high on real DMs → hosted arm wins on availability regardless of fidelity
- Hosted arm's measured hallucination exceeds Phi-4 by >1–2 points on your corpus → stay local
- Banned-term leakage or *new* neologism introduction dominates over factual hallucination → test Claude Haiku 4.5 despite its higher HHEM; Anthropic models are strong on negative-constraint adherence
- Throughput becomes binding at scale → Groq/Cerebras-hosted open weights offer far higher tok/s, but re-run faithfulness because those hosts often serve aggressive quantization

## 7. Caveats

- **HHEM dataset ambiguity.** The current headline table is the 7,700-document enterprise dataset (HHEM-2.3, updated 2026-05-11). Some third-party aggregators mix legacy (~130-document) and current numbers. Figures here are the current dataset unless noted; the legacy branch was not retrievable.
- **Answer rates largely unverified.** Only Phi-4's 80.7% was confirmed. The end-to-end computation in §4.2 assumes ~99% for Flash-Lite. Verify before relying on it.
- **Model churn is extreme.** As of August 2026 the frontier turns over monthly. Many recent releases have no HHEM or FACTS numbers yet; Vectara lags launches by 4–8 weeks. Pricing moves comparably fast — GPT-5 input fell 50% in 90 days.
- **Some figures come from secondary aggregators** (pricepertoken, BenchLM, codingfleet, suprmind) rather than primary leaderboards. Where possible, anchored to Vectara GitHub, DeepMind FACTS, arXiv, and provider pages.
- **Do not conflate AA-Omniscience with this task.** Those "hallucination" numbers measure open-knowledge overconfidence, not grounded summarization. A model can be poor at admitting ignorance yet excellent at faithful summarization — the Flash-Lite tier is exactly that profile.
- **Detector imperfection cuts both ways.** HHEM, MiniCheck, and AlignScore can miss real hallucinations and can flag faithful paraphrases as unfaithful — common under aggressive rewriting and simplification, which is what this pipeline does by design.
