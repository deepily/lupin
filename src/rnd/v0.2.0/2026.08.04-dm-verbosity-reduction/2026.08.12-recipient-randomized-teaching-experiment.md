# Does reading short DMs teach you to write them? — the recipient-randomized design

**Date**: 2026-08-12 · **Author**: Mr. Radio 🦉 (`4073ce01`) · **Status**: DESIGN ONLY — nothing implemented
**Rick's call, 2026-08-12**: set the two-arm data aside as its own finished question; **randomize only on recipients, one question, no third arm mixed in**; add reply linkage so replies can be separated from thread-openers.
**Supersedes**: the three-arm designs in `…-three-arm-experiment-mechanics.md` and `…-v2-session-level.md`. Their schema and endpoint reasoning survive; their arm structure does not.

---

## 1. The single question

> **Does being shown short, well-formed DMs make a session write shorter DMs itself?**

That is the claim behind *"agents mirror the tutor's format"*, and it is the only thing this
experiment tests. **The word saving is not in question** — it is realized at the moment of rewriting
and was measured offline at n=200 (delivered output is a near-constant 3.6 claims / 50 words). This
block buys the **teaching** answer and nothing else.

**What the set-aside two-arm block still answers on its own**: whether *refusing* an over-long DM makes
senders write shorter. It has run for over a week, it is a clean pre-declared comparison, and it should
be analysed and reported as its own result rather than folded into this.

---

## 2. Unit, arms, and why the arm rides the recipient

| | |
|---|---|
| **unit** | the **recipient session** |
| **arms** | `tutored_inbound` — every DM addressed to this session is rewritten before delivery · `raw_inbound` — delivered as written |
| **measured on** | the recipient's **own outbound**, as submitted, before any rewriting |

**Why the recipient and not the sender**: with sender-side arms the unexposed group does not exist. A
recipient hears from a **median of 2 sender sessions and up to 17**, so most sessions would see a mix
of tutored and untutored inbound. Riding the arm on the recipient makes exposure **100% or 0% by
construction**, which is exactly the contrast the question names.

**A session is both reader and writer, and that is fine**: a message is rewritten if and only if its
*recipient* is in the tutored arm, while the corpus always records what the sender *submitted*. So a
session's own writing is measured raw regardless of which arm it or its partners are in.

---

## 3. 🔴 The confound, and the only cut that survives it

**The threat**: a shorter inbound message gives you **less to reply to**. A tutored-arm session
receiving 50-word messages instead of 160-word ones may write shorter replies for mechanical reasons
— same direction as the hypothesis, plausibly the same size.

**The mitigation I proposed first does not exist in this fleet.** DMs here are conversations, not
broadcasts: of 3,175 messages, **7 go to a partner the sender never heard from — 0.2%.** There is no
third-party population to measure.

**What survives: thread-OPENERS.** A message that opens a thread is not a response to anything, so
*"less to reply to"* cannot explain its length. Measured with a 10-minute silence proxy (the real cut
uses `in_reply_to`, §5):

| | share of traffic | median words |
|---|---|---|
| thread-opening | **442 (13.9%)** | 124 |
| replying | 2,739 (86.1%) | 117 |

⇒ **Primary endpoint: submitted words on thread-opening messages only.** It costs ~86% of the sample
and it is the only slice the confound cannot reach.

## 4. Endpoints, in the order they will be reported

| # | endpoint | status | confounded? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | submitted words on **thread-openers**, per session | **primary — confirmatory** | no |
| 2 | submitted words on **replies**, per session | secondary | **yes** — reported beside 1, never merged |
| 3 | **dose–response**: outbound length against count of inbound exposures | exploratory | partially |
| 4 | submitted **sentences** (claim count) on thread-openers | secondary | no |

**If 1 and 2 move together, the reading is teaching. If only 2 moves, we measured the reply reflex.**
That is the same shape as the two-arm block's co-primary logic, and it is the reason 2 is kept rather
than dropped.

## 5. Schema — what has to be recorded before the block opens

| field | why |
|---|---|
| `submitted_words` / `submitted_sentences` | the corpus records ONE body; the tutor replaces it in the send path, so without this the sender's own text is lost |
| **`in_reply_to` / `thread_id`** | **the primary endpoint is undefined without it** — the 10-min proxy in §3 is for sizing only, never for analysis |
| `recipient_arm` + `assigned_at` | the arm the delivery actually used |
| `role` — `manager` / `worker` / **`unresolved`** | stratum, recorded as resolved (María, row `e5d600bd`: `is_manager_figure` fails closed, so unresolved silently reads as worker today) |
| `tutor_fired`, `tutor_outcome` | the trigger is runtime-configurable; a later reader cannot recompute it |
| **`context_epoch`** | **a requirement here, not a sharpener** — see §6 |

`dm_send` already carries `reply_to` and `thread_id` as parameters; they are simply not persisted.

---

## 6. Why `/clear` matters more in this design than in the last one

`/clear` keeps the session id and wipes the memory, and it is invisible in the corpus today.

**In the sender-side design that was tolerable** — a hidden clear only diluted the treatment. **Here
it corrupts the independent variable**: exposure is a *dose accumulated over a session's life*, and a
clear resets the memory of every exposure before it while the id keeps counting. A session recorded
as 40-exposures may be four episodes of ten.

⇒ **Stamp a `context_epoch` that bumps on SessionStart (which already fires on `/clear`) and assign
per `(session_id, epoch)`.** Dose is central to this experiment; without the stamp the primary
endpoint is measured against an overstated exposure.

---

## 7. Assignment mechanism

| element | choice |
|---|---|
| moment | first **inbound** DM to a `(session, epoch)` |
| method | permuted blocks of 2 within stratum, order drawn from the recorded seed |
| strata | `manager` / `worker` / `unresolved` — kept separate rather than folded into worker |
| record | append-only ledger: session, epoch, role, arm, assigned_at, block_id |
| block | new id, new seed, fixed end date declared **before** the first assignment |

**Reproducibility, stated plainly**: the permutation sequence is byte-identical from the seed; the
session-to-arm mapping is not, because it depends on arrival order. **The ledger is the record, not a
cache of something recomputable.**

**Stratification is insurance, not power** — it buys ~13% variance, but the manager/worker gap is 30
words against an effect near 17, and the top five sessions are 32% of traffic.

---

## 8. Power — on the clean endpoint, which is the one that counts

Sessions must contribute **≥3 thread-openers** to enter the primary analysis. That threshold is a
pre-declaration, and it is where the noise settles: at ≥2 the between-session SD is 65.8, at ≥3 it is
**41.8**, and at ≥5 it rises again (44.6) while losing a third of the sample.

| recipient-sessions | usable at ≥3 openers | MDE | ~weeks at 41/wk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | ~36 | 30.2 w | 1.5 |
| 120 | ~73 | 21.4 w | ~3 |
| **200** | **~122** | **16.6 w** | **~5** |
| 300 | ~183 | 13.5 w | ~7 |

**Recommend a five-week block declared up front**, giving ~17 words. For scale, the tutor takes a
rewritten message from 161 words to 51 — so 17 words is roughly a sixth of the mechanical cut, which
is a reasonable bar for a second-order teaching effect.

---

## 9. Two things that must change for the duration of this block

1. **🔴 Sender disclosure goes OFF.** Under the standing ruling the sender is shown what went out. Here
   that breaks the design: a manager writing to both arms sees a mix of rewrites, learns from it, and
   writes shorter **to everyone** — carrying the treatment into the control. **Block-scoped departure
   from María's visibility ruling, recorded as one, not quietly applied.**
2. **The word saving is halved during the block.** Only messages addressed to the tutored arm are
   rewritten, so roughly **half of traffic is treated → ~25% of all DM words cut** during the block,
   against ~50% under a fleet-wide rollout. **That is the price of the answer**, and it is the main
   thing to weigh against simply shipping.

---

## 10. Limitations, named rather than discovered later

- **Interference is real and biases toward the null.** A tutored-arm session that learns writes shorter
  to its partners, some of whom are in the control — partially treating them. That *understates* the
  effect, so a positive result stays trustworthy and a null is ambiguous.
- **The 10-minute proxy is not the endpoint.** Every §3/§8 number is a sizing estimate; the analysis
  uses `in_reply_to`.
- **Between-session SD rests on 88 sessions** of observed traffic. Re-estimate when the current
  two-arm block closes 2026-08-15.
- **A teaching effect cannot outlive a re-spin.** Whatever this measures is bounded to one context
  lifetime — 1.4–2.7 hours for a worker. The experiment can say *whether* reading teaches; it cannot
  make the lesson persist. That is the memento's job, and María notes the instruction lever is already
  pulled (the sentence rule landed in the global `CLAUDE.md` and the spawn rider today), so the tutor
  must beat an **already-instructed** population.
