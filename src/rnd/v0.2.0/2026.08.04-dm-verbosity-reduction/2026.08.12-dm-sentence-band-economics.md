# DM tutor — the corpus re-cut by sentences, and what a ">4 sentences" trigger actually costs

**Date**: 2026-08-12 · **Author**: Mr. Radio 🦉 (`4073ce01`) · **Status**: ANALYSIS — RULED 2026-08-12, see §0.0. No implementation in this doc.
**Asked by**: Rick, broadcast `f4479560` · **Companion plan**: María 🌸, `planning-is-prompting/src/rnd/2026.08.12-words-to-sentences.md`
**Store row**: `4deb26f4`

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

Rick's proposed trigger — **rewrite only DMs longer than four sentences** — sounds selective and
is not. On the fleet's own sentence counter it fires on **79% of all DMs** (2,328 of 2,951),
because the median DM already carries **7.8 claims**. That is not an argument against it: skipping
the other 21% costs only **5.6% of the achievable word saving** while removing 623 model calls. But
the number "4" should be chosen knowing it means *"rewrite almost everything, skip the short ones"*
rather than *"rewrite the offenders."* **Four sentences is about 62 words** at this corpus's rate of
15.6 words per claim.

**The re-measured output side (§5) makes the case for the trigger stronger than the forecast did.**
The tutor does not compress proportionally — it rewrites every message to a near-constant **3.6
claims / 50 words** regardless of input size. So the saving from any message is roughly *its length
minus fifty words*, which is why short DMs pay so poorly. **Of 191 delivered rewrites, the only 6
that came back longer are all in bands the >4 trigger already skips.** Skipping the ≤4-claim
messages costs **17 words each** and avoids a model call apiece.

**One finding cuts the other way and should not be buried**: only **59%** of rewrites land at or
under the three-sentence bar the mandate would state. The tutor approaches the bar rather than obeys
it, missing by exactly one claim in 60 of 78 cases. Gating its output to fix that costs **+41% model
calls at any trigger height** (§5.4.1).

| **to** | 3.6 | 50 |

### The messages it skips — 21% of traffic

3.4 sentences / 57 words, **untouched**.

### The fleet's DM traffic as a whole

| | sentences | words | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **from** | 7.8 per DM | 123 per DM | 361,615 words |
| **to** | 3.5 per DM | 51 per DM | 151,875 words |

## 2. Two decisions that had to be made before any number could be quoted

### 2.1 Which sentence counter — because Lupin has two, and they disagree

María flagged this before the first run, and she was right to. Lupin counts sentences in two places:

| counter | rule | where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **naive** | split on `.` `!` `?`; table rows, headings and bullets all count | `dm.py:_count_sentences` — **this is what the corpus rows store** |
| **claim** | only units that *carry a claim*; structure contributes nothing | `dm_tutor/sentences.py:count_sentences` — **this is what the tutor's trigger calls** |

**Every band in this document is cut on the claim counter**, because a policy must be priced with
the counter that will actually fire on it. Measuring with the other one prices a rule nobody
implements.

**The divergence, measured rather than asserted**: overall inflation is **1.02×**, and **55 of 2,951
messages (1.9%)** land on opposite sides of the ">4" line depending on which counter is asked. That
is a *different set of messages*, not merely a bigger number — which is the reason the trigger must
name its counter in writing.

One correction to the prior expectation, and it is worth recording because it ran the other way:
the divergence was expected to be **widest** in the 250+ band, where 42% of DMs carry a list or a
table. It is **narrowest** there — 1.01×. The claim counter throws away fences, headings and table
rows but **keeps bullets that carry prose**, and in this traffic bullets-with-prose are most of the
structure.

### 2.2 Which population — the sample answers a different question from the corpus

| population | n | what it can answer |
|---|---|---|
| the **200 sampled DMs** (50 per word band) | 200 | "what did we see last night" — the set Rick asked about |
| the **full corpus** | 2,951 | "what will the policy actually meet" — real traffic |

The sample is stratified by construction, so it **over-represents long DMs**: its average message is
168 words against the corpus's 123, and 16% of it sits in the 16+ claim band against 4.2% of real
traffic. **Savings quoted on the sample are not a forecast.** Both are given below; the corpus number
is the one that forecasts.

### 2.3 A note on where last night's numbers went

Rick asked for the average **output** per band. Last night's 400-call run wrote its per-message
detail to `/tmp`, and `/tmp` is swept nightly — **the measurement was gone before the question about
it was asked.** The output half of §5 is therefore a **re-run**, not a re-read: 200 messages through
the same agent, one arm, with every delivered body persisted under `live-runs/` inside the repo.

## 4. Questions 2 and 3 — what the trigger costs, and what "4 sentences" means in words

### 4.1 Four sentences ≈ 62 words, with a caveat that matters

At the corpus rate of **15.6 words per claim**:

- **4 sentences ≈ 62 words** (the trigger height)
- **3 sentences ≈ 47 words** (the bar the mandate would state)

But the *membership* reading is not the same as the *width* reading, and the gap is the finding:

| set | n | median words | p10 | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exactly 4 claims | 345 | 65 | 41 | 96 |
| ≤4 claims (skipped) | 623 | 55 | — | 89 |
| >4 claims (rewritten) | 2,328 | 129 | 77 | — |

**A word threshold cannot stand in for the sentence trigger.** 123 messages at or under four claims
are *longer* than the p10 of the rewritten set, and 418 messages over four claims are *shorter* than
the p90 of the skipped set. Any attempt to keep triggering on words while stating a sentence rule
will misfire on hundreds of messages in both directions.

**For the backstop question** (María's — a word or character ceiling as an undisclosed floor, since
one sentence can run long): the tail is real but thin. The widest message the >4 trigger skips is
**250 words in 4 claims**; the widest single-claim message is **200 words**, once in 2,951. But of
all DM words, only **2.8%** sit in skipped messages over 80 words, **0.9%** over 100, **0.2%** over
120. A ceiling at the p99 of compliant messages — **94 words** — catches the genuine outliers
without pretending to be load-bearing.

### 4.2 The saving, if only >4-claim DMs are rewritten

Forecast applies each word band's measured compression from last night (76% / 41% / 30% / 17% of
words kept). **This is a forecast, and §5.3 replaces it with measured pairs.**

| | the 200 sample | the full corpus |
|---|---|---|
| messages | 200 | 2,951 |
| rewritten (>4 claims) | 158 (79.0%) | 2,328 (78.9%) |
| skipped (≤4 claims) | 42 (21.0%) | 623 (21.1%) |
| words in the rewritten set | 92.9% of all words | 90.1% of all words |
| **saved, trigger at >4** | 22,886 w = **68.1%** | 207,910 w = **57.5%** |
| saved, rewrite every DM | 23,780 w = 70.7% | 220,293 w = 60.9% |
| **the trigger keeps** | **96.2%** of the max | **94.4%** of the max |
| model calls avoided | 42 (~2 min) | 623 (~35 min) |

**Where the saving comes from** (full corpus): the 9–15 band supplies **51.2%** of it, the 5–8 band
**35.2%**, and 16+ only **13.6%**. The longest messages are the most compressible but there are only
125 of them. **The middle of the distribution is where the money is.**

### 4.3 The dial Rick actually has — trigger height

María asked for this, and it reframes the decision. If the *model call* is the cost, the question is
not "4 or not 4" but "how high":

| trigger | fires on | % of messages | saved words | % of all DM words | % of max saving | **words saved per call** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rewrite ALL | 2,951 | 100.0% | 220,293 | 60.9% | 100.0% | 75 |
| >3 | 2,673 | 90.6% | 216,568 | 59.9% | 98.3% | 81 |
| **>4** | **2,328** | **78.9%** | **207,910** | **57.5%** | **94.4%** | **89** |
| >5 | 1,931 | 65.4% | 191,783 | 53.0% | 87.1% | 99 |
| >6 | 1,634 | 55.4% | 175,962 | 48.7% | 79.9% | 108 |
| >7 | 1,348 | 45.7% | 155,875 | 43.1% | 70.8% | 116 |
| >8 | 1,079 | 36.6% | 134,650 | 37.2% | 61.1% | 125 |
| >10 | 643 | 21.8% | 92,436 | 25.6% | 42.0% | 144 |

**The curve is graceful — there is no cliff to fall off.** Moving from >4 to >7 nearly halves the
model calls (2,328 → 1,348) while still keeping **71%** of the achievable saving, and each call
earns more (89 → 116 words). Moving from >4 down to >3 buys 3.9 more points of saving for 345 more
calls.

**This is Rick's dial to set, not mine.** The table is put in front of him rather than a
recommendation, because the right height depends on how he prices a 3.5-second model call against a
word of a colleague's attention — and that is a judgement, not a measurement.

### 4.4 The side-effect of a high bar — and it is ONE cost, not two

Raising the trigger widens the gap between the bar the mandate **states** (three) and the height the
tutor **enforces**. Everything inside that gap is a message that reads as over the limit and gets
rewritten by nothing.

**María caught a framing error here before it reached Rick.** The first version of this section
presented "saving forgone" and "words left unpoliced" as two penalties. They are one quantity
measured two ways — the words in the gap band *are* the words whose saving is forgone — and showing
both invites double-counting.

| trigger | gap band | msgs in it | **words left unpoliced** | saving forgone | gap-band compression | stated:enforced |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| >3 | none | 0 | 0.0% | 0.0% | — | 1.00× |
| **>4** | 4–4 | 345 | **6.4%** | 5.6% | 37% | 1.33× |
| >5 | 4–5 | 742 | 15.8% | 12.9% | 43% | 1.67× |
| >6 | 4–6 | 1,039 | 23.9% | 20.1% | 47% | 2.00× |
| >7 | 4–7 | 1,325 | **33.2%** | 29.2% | 51% | 2.33× |
| >8 | 4–8 | 1,594 | 42.8% | 38.9% | 53% | 2.67× |

**Read the unpoliced column and ignore the next one** — the second is the first, compression-weighted.

**Why the two columns differ at all, and the direction matters.** The gap band is **less**
compressible than the corpus: 37% at >4 rising to 53% at >8, against a **61%** corpus average. That
weighting is exactly why saving-forgone sits below the unpoliced word share at every height. So the
*"the ones we skip were mostly fine anyway"* argument survives, weakly — there is less to take out of
those messages — and it strengthens as the bar rises, which mildly favours a higher trigger. (María
first read the tracking as equal compressibility; measuring it settled the direction.)

**One constant that drops out of the dial entirely**: the ≤3-claim tier is 278 messages carrying
**3.5% of all words**, and it is never rewritten at any height. Not part of the trade.

**What is genuinely a second axis** is not a number: at >4 the stated bar and the enforced gate are
one claim apart and holding the stated number is cheap; at >7 they are four apart, and the mandate
says three while the machine tolerates eight.

## 5. Question 4 — the output side

200 messages re-run through `DmTutorAgent`, sentinel arm, **191 delivered (95.5%)**; the 9 failures
fail closed and send the original. Rows: `live-runs/sentence-band-outputs-2026.08.12.jsonl`.

### 5.1 The headline: **the output is a constant**

| sentence band | n | ok | avg in sent | **avg out sent** | avg in words | **avg out words** | words kept | claims kept | ≤3 claims out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 6 | 5 | 1.8 | **3.0** | 26 | **30** | 116% | 167% | 5/5 |
| 3–4 | 36 | 33 | 3.7 | **3.3** | 64 | **42** | 66% | 91% | 23/33 |
| 5–8 | 51 | 51 | 6.4 | **3.4** | 110 | **48** | 44% | 53% | 30/51 |
| 9–15 | 75 | 71 | 11.6 | **3.8** | 197 | **52** | 26% | 33% | 39/71 |
| 16+ | 32 | 31 | 21.6 | **3.7** | 344 | **52** | 15% | 17% | 16/31 |
| **ALL** | **200** | **191** | 10.2 | **3.6** | 170 | **49** | **29%** | **35%** | 113/191 |

**The tutor does not compress proportionally — it rewrites to a fixed size.** A 26-word DM and a
344-word DM both come back at about **3.6 claims and 50 words**. Input length explains almost nothing
about output length.

That single fact governs the whole economics: **the saving from any message is essentially
`its length − 50 words`.** The percentage figures in §4 are an effect of that, not a property of the
compressor. It also explains the old word-band table cleanly — the 76% / 42% / 28% / 17% ladder from
last night is just 50 words divided by 53 / 115 / 185 / 323.

### 5.2 The finding that most supports Rick's trigger

**Across all 191 delivered rewrites, exactly 6 came back longer in words — and every one of them is
in a band the >4 trigger already skips** (four in 3–4 claims, two in 1–2). Above four claims the
tutor never lengthened a message.

The 1–2 band's headline "116% words kept" is **an artifact of one degenerate input** and should not
be quoted as a band property. The five delivered messages individually:

| in | out | words kept |
|---|---|---|
| 28w / 2 claims | 22w / 3 | 79% |
| 45w / 2 claims | 35w / 3 | 78% |
| 26w / 2 claims | 37w / 3 | **142%** |
| **1w / 1 claim** | **28w / 3** | **2800%** |
| 31w / 2 claims | 30w / 3 | 97% |

Strip the one-word message and the band is roughly break-even on words. But the **claim count rises
in every single case** — all five arrive at exactly 3 claims, including the two that got shorter.
**The tutor pads a short message up to the bar rather than leaving it alone.** With n=5 that is
directional, not settled; it points the same way as the word data and the same way as Rick's
instinct.

One rewrite in that band is worth Rick's eye for a different reason. Input: *"🙏🏼 Noted — clean
heartbeat baseline on my next full run. My DM-queue pollution is cumulative (each subset passes, the
union reds), still hunting the culprit."* Output: *"The agent is committed to identifying the root
cause… The agent plans to clean the heartbeat baseline on their next full run."* It went from
first-person to third-person report language, got **longer**, and lost the acknowledgement. **This is
a faithfulness question, and faithfulness is Rick's read, not a harness's.**

### 5.3 The trigger priced on measured pairs, not forecast

This replaces §4.2's forecast for the 200-message sample. A failed rewrite saves **nothing** and is
counted that way — fail-closed means the original went out.

| | forecast (§4.2) | **measured** |
|---|---|---|
| rewritten (>4 claims) | 158 (79.0%) | 158 (79.0%) |
| saved, trigger at >4 | 22,886 w = 68.1% | **22,514 w = 67.0%** |
| saved, rewrite every DM | 23,780 w = 70.7% | 23,208 w = 69.0% |
| **the trigger keeps** | 96.2% of the max | **97.0% of the max** |

**The forecast held.** Measured saving is 1.1 points below the forecast — the gap is the 9 fail-closed
messages the forecast credited with compression.

**What skipping the ≤4-claim messages actually costs: 694 words across 42 messages — 17 words each.**
For that, 42 model calls (136 seconds) are not made. On the corpus that scales to roughly 623 calls
and ~35 minutes of model time avoided.

### 5.4 The one number that should worry us

**Only 113 of 191 rewrites (59.2%) land at or under three claims.** The tutor misses the bar it is
being asked to teach, four times in ten.

| delivered claims | messages | |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 113 | at the bar |
| 4 | 60 | over |
| 5 | 9 | over |
| 6 | 4 | over |
| 7 | 3 | over |
| 8 | 1 | over |
| 9 | 1 | over |

Median 3, p90 4, max 9. The overshoot is mostly by **one claim** — 60 of the 78 misses are exactly
four. So this reads as a bar the tutor approaches rather than obeys, not as a compressor that has
lost control. It matters for the mandate because **a tutor that lands on 4 while teaching 3 will
mark its own output as needing a rewrite** if the trigger is ever set at >3.

#### 5.4.1 What an output gate would cost — the number María's §3.3 proposal needs

María's plan recommends gating the tutor's output against the stated bar and re-asking once when it
comes back over. **The deciding is free** — the claim counter is deterministic, no model call — but
**the redo is not**, and at a 40.8% miss rate it is a large term:

| trigger | fires on | re-asks | total calls | **what the gate adds** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| >4 | 2,328 | 950 | 3,278 | **+41%** |
| >5 | 1,931 | 788 | 2,719 | **+41%** |
| >6 | 1,634 | 667 | 2,301 | **+41%** |
| >7 | 1,348 | 550 | 1,898 | **+41%** |

**The gate adds a flat 41% to whatever the trigger fires on, at every height.** So the honest reading
is: the gate is a fixed proportional surcharge, and **it does not constrain the choice of height at
all.**

> 🔴 **A correction to this section, made before it reached Rick.** The first version put a
> "rewrite every DM once, ungated" row (2,951 calls) at the top of the table and concluded that a
> gated >4 *"costs more than rewriting everything once"*, with a crossover at >5 — which María then
> carried into her plan as a recommended floor. **That crossover is an artifact of the comparison,
> not a property of the gate.** 2,951 is simply the number of DMs in the corpus; no one proposed
> rewriting all of them ungated, so it is not a budget line. A vivid comparison hardened into a
> constraint in one hop between two documents. The gate's real cost is the +41% column, which is
> constant.

The gate is still the right call — an output that lands on four while the mandate teaches three is a
real problem, and §5.4 shows it happens four times in ten. But it should be argued on that ground,
and priced at +41% of whatever height Rick picks, rather than sold as free or as forcing a floor.

## 6. What this analysis does NOT establish

- **Faithfulness.** Every check here is structural — word counts, claim counts, band membership.
  Whether a rewrite quietly reverses a meaning is still Rick's read, and it is still owed from the
  08-11 run.
- **That the skipped 21% should be skipped.** The economics say skipping them costs little. Whether
  a 4-claim message *should* be left alone is a house-style question, not a cost one.
- **Anything about the calls that never deliver.** This run failed closed on **9 of 200 calls
  (4.5%)** — last night's 400-call run measured 3.2%, and neither run was built to explain the
