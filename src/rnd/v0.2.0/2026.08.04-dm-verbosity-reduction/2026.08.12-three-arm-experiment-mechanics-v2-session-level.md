# Three-arm experiment, v2 — randomize by SESSION, not by clock hour

**Date**: 2026-08-12 · **Author**: Mr. Radio 🦉 (`4073ce01`) · **Status**: PLAN ONLY — nothing implemented
**Supersedes**: `2026.08.12-three-arm-experiment-mechanics.md` §3 (schedule) and §4 (power). Its §2 (schema), §5 (endpoints) and §5.2–5.3 (visibility, discriminator) still stand.
**Why v2 exists**: Rick's correction, 2026-08-12 — sender memory is wiped at every re-spin, so learning cannot cross a context lifetime. Following that through breaks the hour-based design.
**Companion**: María 🌸, `planning-is-prompting/src/rnd/2026.08.12-three-arm-tutor-treatment.md`

---

## 1. What Rick's correction did to v1

**His point**: senders may be human or Claude Code, transient or all-day, and **everybody gets re-spun to manage context**. Unless a memento carries the lesson, every re-spin starts from scratch.

**He is right, and my v1 framing was wrong in the direction he named**: I treated exposure as
permanently contaminating, so a fleet-wide rollout would forfeit the causal question forever. With
memory reset at every re-spin, it does not — a later arm measures fresh senders.

**But the same fact relocates the problem and makes it acute.** If learning cannot cross a re-spin,
it can only happen **within one context lifetime** — and v1 randomized by **clock hour** while
sessions span hours. Measured on the live experiment corpus:

| | |
|---|---|
| median distinct clock hours per sender session | **3** (max 9) |
| sessions spanning more than one hour | 70% |
| **messages from a session that saw BOTH arms** | **96%** — 1,880 of 1,955 |

**For the `rejecting` arm this was tolerable** — a 413 is a discrete per-message event, and the
hour-level unit absorbs it. **For a teaching treatment it is fatal**: a session tutored at 10:00 and
"blind" at 11:00 carries the lesson across the boundary, so the control hours are contaminated by the
treatment. **An hour-randomized design cannot measure a treatment whose effect persists inside a
session.**

⇒ **The randomization unit becomes the session.**

---

## 2. The unit, and the one thing we cannot currently see

| reset mechanism | new session id? | visible in the corpus? |
|---|---|---|
| re-spin / fresh spawn | yes | ✅ |
| **`/clear`** | **no — id survives, memory does not** | ❌ **invisible** |

**So a session id is an UPPER BOUND on the learning unit, not the unit itself.** A long manager
session may contain several context lifetimes.

**This is survivable without a fix, and that matters for schedule.** If a whole session id holds ONE
arm for its life, a hidden `/clear` inside it only **dilutes** the treatment — the sender forgets
mid-exposure — it never leaks treatment into a control. **Dilution attenuates; it does not bias.**

⇒ **Start with session-id assignment. Sharpen later** by stamping a **context epoch** that bumps on
SessionStart (which already fires on `/clear`), then assigning per `(session_id, epoch)`. That is a
hook change, not a blocker, and it belongs in a later block so the first one is not gated on it.

## 3. Assignment mechanism — this replaces the schedule file, it does not extend it

The clock-hour schedule JSON cannot express a session-level assignment, so the third block uses a
different mechanism under its own block id. **The 2-arm blocks keep working untouched.**

**Permuted-block randomization within role stratum, recorded in an append-only ledger.**

| element | choice | why |
|---|---|---|
| assignment moment | first DM of a session | the earliest instant the sender is observable |
| assignment key | `session_id` (later `session_id + epoch`) | §2 |
| method | permuted blocks of 3 (one of each arm), order drawn from the recorded seed | guarantees balance at every multiple of 3 — a pure hash does not |
| strata | `manager` / `worker` | §4 |
| record | append-only ledger row: session, role, arm, assigned_at, block_id | the assignment IS the artifact; it must be readable without re-deriving it |

**Reproducibility, stated honestly.** The permutation *sequence* per stratum is byte-identical from
the recorded seed. **The mapping of sessions to arms is not reproducible**, because it depends on
arrival order, which is not. That is a real difference from the 2-arm design's byte-identical
schedule, and it is why the ledger exists: **the ledger is the record, not a cache of something
recomputable.**

⚠️ **Role must be recorded as used, not re-derived at analysis time.** Persona rosters change. A row
that carries the role the assigner actually applied stays analysable; one that does not becomes
unanalysable the first time someone is promoted.

## 4. Why stratify by role — and it is NOT mainly a power argument

Measured across 88 sender sessions:

| | sessions | share of traffic | median msgs | **mean excess words** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| managers | 23 (26%) | **50%** | 46 | **76.1** |
| workers | 65 (74%) | 50% | 21 | **45.7** |

**Stratification's power gain is small — pooled within-role SD 33.0 against 35.5 overall, a 13%
variance reduction and ~7% off the MDE.** I nearly sold it as a power win; it is not one.

**Its real job is guarding a confound bigger than the effect.** The manager/worker gap is **30 words**.
The effect we are trying to detect is ~19 words at a realistic sample size. With ~18 sessions per arm,
a chance imbalance of two or three manager sessions moves the estimate by more than the thing being
measured. **Stratification is insurance against the design being decided by which arm the big sessions
happened to land in** — and the concentration is real: the largest single session is **9% of all
traffic**, the top five are 32%.

⇒ **Also: the analyzer weights by SESSION — one number per session — never by message.** Otherwise a
few long manager sessions carry the estimate regardless of how assignment went.

## 5. Power — the number that changed most

Between-session SD of the analyzer metric: **35.5 words overall, 33.0 pooled within role.**
Observed rate: **~41 sender sessions per week.**

**MDE for tutor-vs-blind in a three-arm split** (80% power, two-sided 0.05, `n_per_arm = N/3`):

| sessions in block | per arm | unstratified | **stratified** | ~weeks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 53 | 18 | 33.4 w | **31.1 w** | 1.3 |
| 120 | 40 | 22.2 w | **20.7 w** | 2.9 |
| **200** | **67** | 17.2 w | **16.0 w** | **~5** |
| 400 | 133 | 12.2 w | **11.3 w** | ~10 |

**Against v1's hour-based design: MDE 38.9 words for one block, and ~211 triples (5–15 months) for a
10-word effect.** Session-level assignment reaches **16 words in about five weeks.**

🔴 **The v1 figure was an artifact of the wrong randomization unit, not a property of the question.**
Rick's correction is what surfaced it.

⚠️ **These rest on 88 sessions of observed data and a between-session SD estimated from them.**

## 6. What this means for the teaching question itself

**The tutor can only teach inside one context lifetime** — 1.4–2.7 hours for a worker, 3.8–5.0 for a
manager. Anything durable must travel in the **memento or the spawn brief**, which is María's phase 1,
not the tutor.

| | mechanism | timescale | what it can achieve |
|---|---|---|---|
| **tutor** | demonstration | within one lifetime | the mechanical cut — **50% of all DM words**, needs no learning |
| **doc / rider / memento** | instruction | across lifetimes | durable habit, if carried |

⇒ **Two consequences.**

1. **Keep the teaching endpoint exploratory and do not let it drive the trigger height or the rollout
   date.** Its ceiling is bounded by construction: a lesson learned at hour two of a worker's life
   dies at hour three. The mechanical cut is certain, large, and needs no learning at all.
2. **A cheaper experiment is now visible and worth queueing**: does putting the brevity lesson in the
   **memento template** beat the tutor? That is a one-line template change against a model call on 55%
   of DMs — and it is the only lever that can survive a re-spin.
