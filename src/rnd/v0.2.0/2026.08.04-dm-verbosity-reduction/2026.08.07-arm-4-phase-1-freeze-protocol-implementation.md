## 0. What this is

The deterministic kernel that makes a lossy rewrite safe for the literals it
carries: **extract → placehold → validate → restore**. Nothing in it calls a
model. It exists so that when the rewriter lands in phase 2, a corrupted line
number cannot reach a recipient.

The claim, stated exactly as the review demanded:

> Exact preservation of selected byte spans through a lossy rewrite.

**Not** meaning preservation. A placeholder can survive perfectly and end up
attached to the wrong claim. That is relocation, it passes every structural
check here, and it is a separate layer's problem.

## 2. Five places the plan was wrong, and what the measurement said

Every one of these was found by measuring rather than reasoning.

### 2.1 The placeholder format was costing more than the literals

`⟦L00⟧` is elegant and unaffordable. U+27E6/U+27E7 are in no BPE vocabulary, so
each fragments into byte tokens.

| Format | Tokens over 14,244 spans | vs the literals |
|---|---|---|
| the literals | 77,205 | — |
| **`[[L00]]`** | **57,195** | **−20,010** |
| `⟦L00⟧` | 114,171 | **+36,966** |

The plan's own choice would have **added 37,000 tokens per corpus-day before
compressing a single word.** `<L00>` measures cheaper still and is deliberately
not used: the plan rejected angle brackets because models read them as markup,
and a cost benchmark cannot see a behavioural risk.

### 2.2 Some literals can never afford a placeholder — so they are not frozen

A bare integer averages **1.06 tokens**. No substitution beats that; the
arithmetic is independent of format. They are still load-bearing (`2710 passed /
1 skipped`, `census 31/31`).

So Phase 1 has **two tiers**:

- **FREEZE** — substitute. For classes whose literals exceed the ~3-token
  placeholder cost: paths, file:line, shas, quoted spans, code, identifiers.
- **VERIFY** — check in place, substitute nothing. For classes cheaper than a
  placeholder: bare integers, ports, percentages, units, section pointers,
  deltas, issue ids, inline glyphs. The assertion is that the **multiset is
  unchanged**. Zero added tokens.

The verify tier catches omission and mutation. It is **blind to relocation** by
construction — `3 failed / 21669 passed` with the numbers swapped has an
identical multiset — and there is a test asserting that blindness so nobody
mistakes a green verify result for a semantic guarantee.

### 2.3 The typed, clause-scoped placeholder was a bad trade

The review floated `⟦S02_PATH00⟧` so the model could see structure. Two
measurements killed it:

1. The type label **is not trustworthy** — the port pattern fires on line
   numbers, so a `PORT` placeholder frequently is not one.
2. Every extra character is paid on every placeholder across all traffic.

Tokens now carry an ordinal and nothing else. Kind, clause and equivalence group
live in the `Placeholder` record, which holds strictly more than the token ever
could. A test relabels every kind and asserts the verdict is unchanged, making
type-independence permanent rather than merely true today.

### 2.4 Freezing everything important is itself a failure

Rick's second goal is stripping ceremony. In this fleet the ceremony is largely
glyphs: **58% of messages carry one**, 78% leading and decorative — 🦉 574, 🫡
487, 🕊️ 141. Verify those and the rewriter fires fail-closed across most of the
corpus **by obeying its instructions**, and the fallback rate reads as a bug.

So a **leading glyph run is declared removable boilerplate** — the sender's
identity is already in the envelope's `from` field. An **inline** glyph is not:
mid-sentence ✅ ⚠️ ✓ are content markers.

`_REMOVABLE_BOILERPLATE` is the first concrete piece of the review's issue 1.
Something has to say what may be dropped, or every deletion looks like damage.

### 2.5 "Env/CONST names, 8,330 occurrences" is counting English

Bare ALL-CAPS across the corpus is 12,766 hits. The top of the list:

`NOT` 810 · `HEAD` 473 · `KISS` 449 · `AND` 231 · `NO` 185 · `PASS` 181 · `IS` 168

Requiring an underscore is correct. The genuine misses were snake_case and
dotted calls (`cu.get_project_root()`, `_slide_budget`), which now have their own
class.

## 3. Defects found and fixed during the build

Each of these produced a **valid-looking wrong result** — the failure mode the
whole protocol exists to prevent.

| # | Defect | Consequence if shipped |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `file.py:\d+` truncated **ranges and comma lists** — `job.py:58,106,249` froze as `job.py:58` | `,106,249` travels as compressible prose. Delivered: a confident citation naming fewer sites than the author did, passing every structural check |
| 2 | Gate pointer regex swallowed the next word — `gate the` → `gate t` | 1,307 junk spans, the single largest over-freezer. Invisible at the maximum because it spread thin |
| 3 | Boundary check judged adjacency **absolutely** | Rejected ordinary corpus text where a literal legitimately abuts a word character. Now judged against how the token was *sent* |
| 4 | "Invented placeholder" check matched any bracketed span | `[done]` and `[dropped]` are ordinary status markers here; would have bypassed a large slice of real traffic |
| 5 | `(L207)` in prose read as a mangled placeholder | Now triggers a per-message namespace, paid only on messages that need it |

Defects 3, 4 and 5 were found by running the identity round trip across the full
corpus. None would have surfaced from handwritten examples.

## 4. The test suite, and why it is shaped this way

A literal-preservation suite that has never failed is not evidence.

- **Mutation tests are the centre of gravity** — one deliberate corruption per
  check, each asserting *that* check fires: omission, duplication, six delimiter
  manglings, absorption, truncation, in- and out-of-namespace invention, and one
  per verify class.
- **The negative control sits after them, not instead of them.** Turning
  freezing off does not reliably turn a suite red: a model that preserves a
  literal by luck looks exactly like a literal that was protected.
- **Property-based fuzzing**, 400 cases over an alphabet including `⟦⟧`, CJK,
  emoji and dashes — covering what nobody thought of.
- **Full-corpus identity**, all 2,951 bodies.
- **Two tests assert limitations rather than capabilities**: integer swapping is
  *not* caught, and relocation warns rather than fails. Both would be deleted by
  a future reader who mistook them for bugs, so both say so in the docstring.

Two fixtures failed on first run, both authoring errors rather than product
defects: the sample message was so literal-dense it bypassed its own pipeline,
and the simulated rewrite had gone stale into a no-op. The helper now asserts it
actually changed something.

## 5. What Phase 1 does NOT do

Named plainly, because a green suite here says nothing about any of it:

1. **No semantic-preservation contract.** Negation, modality, actor/action and
   status are unprotected. The review's issue 1 is only started — the removable
   list exists; the preserve list does not.
2. **No relocation detection.** Clause confinement is a *warning*.
3. **No privacy review.** The corpus contains real email addresses and discusses
   unreleased work. Placeholders protect recognised values; the surrounding
   prose still leaves the system verbatim. This belongs in the sourcing gate.
4. **No model has been called.** Every number here is about the kernel, not
   about any rewriter's behaviour.
5. **Extraction recall is a stride sample**, not the review's full stratified
   labelled set. The classes are real; their frequencies are not measured.

## 6. Open questions still Rick's

1. **Covert or disclosed?** The review is firm that covertness should not be
   load-bearing — it makes debugging and accountability worse. Disclosed, this
   is honest transport compression that still banks the tokens.
2. **Where does the bypass floor belong?** 40 compressible words bypasses 5.9%
   of traffic (median 31 words). Defensible, and tunable in one constant.
