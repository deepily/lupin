## 0. The verdict, before any schedule design

**A third arm can prove the tutor cuts delivered words. It cannot prove the tutor teaches anyone to
write shorter — not at any schedule length we would tolerate.** Those are two different experiments
and only one of them is worth slots:

| endpoint | effect size | detectable? | worth running? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **delivered** words per DM | **55.7 words/attempt** (forecast at `>6`) | yes — 14 pairs detects 38.9 | ❌ **it is a mechanical certainty**, already measured offline at n=200 |
| **submitted** words per DM — does exposure change what senders write? | unknown, probably small | **no** — see §4 | ✅ the actual open question |

**So the design question is not "how do we add a third arm to the existing analyzer."** It is
"what do we measure, given that the obvious endpoint is already known and the interesting one is
out of reach?" §4 and §5 are the load-bearing sections; §2–§3 are the plumbing that follows once
the endpoint is chosen.

## 2. 🔴 The schema change that has to land first

**The corpus records ONE body.** `dm.py:412-435` writes `body`, `words`, `chars`, `sentences` — all
derived from a single `body_text`. The tutor sits in the send path and replaces that text.

⇒ **Whichever side of the tutor the recorder sits on, the other side is lost forever.** Record after
the rewrite and every tutor-arm row understates what its sender wrote; record before and the arm's
delivered length is unmeasurable. **Both are needed, and no analysis in §4 or §5 is possible without
them.**

| new field | value in the tutor arm | value in the other two arms |
|---|---|---|
| `submitted_words` / `submitted_sentences` | what the sender wrote | same as delivered |
| `words` / `sentences` (unchanged meaning: what was DELIVERED) | the rewrite | what the sender wrote |
| `tutor_fired` | true / false — **the trigger's own answer, recorded not inferred** | false |
| `tutor_outcome` | `rewritten` · `failed_closed` · `not_triggered` | `not_applicable` |

**`tutor_fired` must be recorded, never re-derived at analysis time.** The trigger is
runtime-configurable per Rick's ruling, so a later reader cannot recompute which messages it fired on
without knowing the height in force at that instant. A row that does not carry its own answer becomes
unanalysable the first time the config changes.

**This is a corpus schema change on a live experiment**, so it lands in the gap between blocks — after
the week-2 block closes Sat 2026-08-15 14:00, before the third block opens. Rows on either side stay
comparable because the added fields are additive and the existing ones keep their meaning.

## 3. Schedule mechanics — pairs become triples

The current design pairs each clock hour: Wednesday mirrors Tuesday, one arm each, and the analyzer's
unit of evidence is that matched pair. **Three arms need each clock hour to carry all three**, which
means mirror groups of three days rather than two.

| | 2-arm (today) | 3-arm |
|---|---|---|
| mirror group | 2 days | **3 days** |
| slots per clock hour | 2 | **3** |
| unit of evidence | matched pair | **matched triple** |
| randomization | which day gets which arm (2 orders) | which day gets which arm (**6 orders**) |
| slots for 14 hours of coverage | 28 | **42** |

**Concretely**: a block of 42 slots covers 14 clock hours × 3 arms, in three-day mirror groups —
the same shape as today, one dimension wider. New block id (`dm-verbosity-three-arm-v1`), new
recorded seed (the ruling date), `EXPERIMENT` tag bumped so the analyzer's classifier separates it
from `two-arm-v1` rows by construction.

**What must NOT happen**: reusing the `two-arm-v1` tag, or back-labelling any existing slot. The
analyzer already treats the 776 legacy `signal_only` rows as a third condition it refuses to pool;
the same discipline applies here.

### 3.1 Runtime changes, in order of blast radius

| # | change | risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `VALID_ARMS` gains the tutor arm | low — anything outside the set already falls back to baseline |
| 2 | arm override honors the new label | low — same validation path |
| 3 | the tutor's call site reads the arm and only runs in the tutor arm | **the one that matters** — a tutor that runs in `blind` silently destroys the control |
| 4 | corpus fields of §2 | additive |

**Item 3 is the whole experiment.** Recommend it be asserted by a test that sends under each arm and
checks `tutor_fired` is false in two of the three — not by reading the code and agreeing it looks
right.

## 4. 🔴 Power — and why the interesting endpoint is out of reach

Measured from the **live** two-arm corpus (1,945 in-experiment rows, `src/tmp/dm_traffic.jsonl`), on
the analyzer's own metric of mean excess words over 60:

| | |
|---|---|
| matched clock-hour pairs available | 10 |
| SD of the paired difference | **51.9 words** |
| between-cell SD (hour × arm) | 38.6 words |
| median cell size | 20 messages |

⚠️ **That SD is itself estimated from 10 pairs and is not precise.** Everything below moves if it is
wrong, and the honest way to firm it up is to recompute once the week-2 block closes with ~28 pairs
in hand. Treat the slot counts as an order of magnitude, not a specification.

**Minimum detectable difference** (80% power, two-sided 0.05):

| pairs / triples | MDE |
|---|---|
| 14 | 38.9 words |
| 28 | 27.5 words |
| 42 | 22.4 words |
| **211** | **10 words** |
| **431** | **7 words** |

### The two endpoints against that table

**Delivered words — detectable, and not worth detecting.** The forecast tutor effect at `>6` is
**55.7 words per attempt**, comfortably above the 38.9 MDE of a single 14-triple block, and above the
pre-declared ±46 null band. **But this is arithmetic, not a hypothesis**: the tutor rewrites to ~51
words, 60 is the target, so the excess metric collapses to near zero by construction. We measured it
offline on 200 messages this morning. **Spending a fortnight of fleet traffic to re-confirm it would
buy nothing.**

**Submitted words — the real question, and unreachable.** Whether seeing your own DM rewritten
teaches you to write shorter is what *"agents mirror the tutor's format"* actually claims, and it is
unmeasured. A behavioural shift of 10 words per DM would need **~211 triples**; 7 words needs ~431.
At the week-2 block's rate (56 slots ≈ 28 pairs per week) that is **five to fifteen months.**

**And the one relevant prior says the effect is small**: arm B of the compression experiment told the
model its exact per-message target and moved 51,854 tokens by **73** (2026-08-07). Explicit
instruction is a weak lever in this fleet's own data; whether demonstration is stronger is precisely
the open question, but nothing suggests it is ten times stronger.

## 5. What I recommend the third arm actually be

Three options, and the choice is Rick's. **My recommendation is B.**

### Option A — tutor arm, delivered-words endpoint

Add the arm, keep the existing analyzer metric, run 42 slots. **Cheap, fast, and proves a certainty.**
Its only real yield is operational: does the tutor survive live traffic, what is its failure rate
outside a harness, does anything break. That is a **shakedown**, and it should be called one rather
than dressed as a result.

### Option B — tutor arm, dual endpoint, honest about which is which ✅

Same 42-slot block. **Two pre-declared endpoints with different status, written down before it runs:**

| endpoint | status | what it can conclude |
|---|---|---|
| delivered words | **confirmatory of a mechanical claim** | the tutor works in production as it did offline |
| **submitted words, per sender, over time** | **exploratory — underpowered by design** | a direction, never a verdict |

The second is the one worth the schema work in §2, and **its unit is the sender, not the clock
hour.** A per-sender trajectory (does *this* persona's submitted length fall across its own exposures?)
uses each sender as its own control and sidesteps the between-hour noise that makes §4's table so
grim. **It cannot reach significance in one block and must never be reported as if it had** — but it
accumulates, and after several blocks it is the only design here that could ever answer the question.

### ~~Option C — don't add an arm; run the tutor on everything and watch the trend~~ — **DEAD**

Turn the tutor on fleet-wide and read the submitted-length trend against the pre-tutor corpus as a
historical baseline. **Killed by María before it was costed: her phase 1 ships today**, so the
fleet's written brevity instructions change this week. A months-long trend read against a pre-tutor
baseline would confound the doc rewrite with the tutor, and there is no way to separate them after
the fact.

⇒ **B**, because it costs the same as A, and the only extra work — recording what the sender actually
wrote — is the thing without which the interesting question stays permanently unaskable.

### 5.1 What the doc change means for B's exploratory endpoint — and the fix

The same confound that killed C threatens B, and the wording of the endpoint is what saves it.
María's point: a doc change landing mid-stream is **common-mode** — it hits every arm equally — so
**between-arm** comparisons survive it and **raw trends** do not.

⇒ **The exploratory endpoint must be stated as a between-arm contrast of within-sender trends**, never
as a trend:

| ❌ not this | ✅ this |
|---|---|
| "does a sender's submitted length fall over time?" | "does it fall **faster during that sender's tutor-arm exposures than during their blind-arm exposures**?" |
| confounded by the doc change, by fleet composition, by anything else that moves | the doc change lands on both halves of the contrast and cancels |

Each sender is its own control **and** each arm is the other's control. That is worth the wording
even though the endpoint stays underpowered in one block — an underpowered clean estimate accumulates
across blocks; an underpowered confounded one never becomes evidence no matter how long it runs.

### 5.2 What María's visibility ruling costs the design, and it is worth paying

She rules **disclosed-after**: the rewrite ships and the sender is shown what went out. Silent would
kill the only lever we control on effect size; offer would cost a round trip on 55% of DMs.

**The price, named**: a disclosed treatment is **distinguishable to the population being measured**,
so a movement in submitted length is *"the sender responded to being rewritten"* — not necessarily
*"the sender learned brevity."* Annoyance, gaming, and writing to what they think the tutor wants all
produce the same direction. **That is not an argument for silence** — a silent arm has no teaching
channel at all and would answer a question nobody asked — but the exploratory endpoint should be
reported as *responded to the treatment*, which is what it measures.

Her wording rule closes the leak that would otherwise make this worse: **the tutor carries no
measurement of the sender's message at all.** A claim count shown only when the tutor fires makes the
smallest number a sender ever sees equal to the trigger plus one — which publishes the held threshold
by inference. That rule also protects the endpoint: a sender who can read the trigger off the
feedback writes to the trigger, and the arm stops measuring teaching.
