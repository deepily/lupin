## 1. Two defects in the current taxonomy — these corrupt, they don't just miss

### 🔴 D1. Line **ranges** are truncated, not frozen

`file.py:NNN` is written as `:\d+`, so a range freezes only its first half:

```
input     "job.py:249-256 copies duration"
frozen    ⟦job.py:249⟧-256 copies duration
          └─ protected ─┘└── sent to the rewriter as prose ──┘
```

The tail `-256` is compressible text. A rewriter that drops it produces `job.py:249` — a **valid-looking, wrong** citation that passes every structural check, because the placeholder itself was never touched. Live in the corpus: `job.py:249-256`, `api_client.py:413-419`, `lines 486–703` (en-dash).

**Fix**: `:\d+(?:[-–]\d+)?`, and accept the en-dash. Add both to the mutation suite.

### 🔴 D2. `:PORT` fires on line numbers

`prefix at :27, path at :120` and `:974`, `:1106` are all **line numbers**, typed as `port` by the regex. Harmless while placeholders are opaque, but it means **the type label is not trustworthy** — so do not let a typed placeholder (`⟦S02_PORT00⟧`) imply the type is correct, and do not build any validator check that reasons from the type.

## 3. Precision — two measurements, opposite verdicts

### ✅ The sha regex is safe. Worry retired.

`\b[0-9a-f]{7,40}\b` can in principle match English words made only of a–f letters (`deadbeef`, `defaced`). Measured across all 2,951 bodies: **one** letter-only hit total (`fcffafaf`, itself a real hex string). **False-positive rate is negligible; do not add a digit-required guard** — it would cost recall on all-letter shas for nothing.

### ⚠️ Frozen proportion — the tail is in SHORT messages, not long ones

| Statistic | Frozen % of characters |
|---|---|
| mean | 7.0 |
| median | 5.6 |
| p75 | 10.1 |
| p90 | 15.5 |
| p95 | 19.0 |
| p99 | 27.4 |
| max | 43.2 |

| Bypass threshold | DMs above it |
|---|---|
| >25% | 48 (1.6%) |
| >40% | 4 (0.1%) |
| >50% | 0 |

**All five densest messages are in the `<80` word band.** The plan (§3) reassured itself with the 250+ band at 14.4% — that was the right check on the wrong end. Long messages have plenty of prose to compress; **short dense ones are where freezing eats the budget**, and those are already the band with the lowest target ratio (15%).

⇒ **Recommended bypass rule**: skip rewriting when frozen ≥ 40% **or** the compressible remainder is under ~40 words. Costs ~0.1% of traffic — effectively free — and removes the cases where the rewriter has nothing to work with.

⇒ Note the whole distribution sits **well under** any threshold that would hurt: nothing exceeds 50%. Freezing genuinely does not eat the compression budget. The plan's central bet holds; only its choice of where to look was off.

### ⚠️ Long quoted spans are the real SOFT cost

The review's issue 3 is confirmed by eye, not yet by number. `"Retained for signature parity (unused on the bounded path)"` and `"does a real-invocation test drive the shared call site with both new parameters."` are full sentences frozen whole by the `dquoted` rule. **Recommend capping SOFT quoted spans at ~8 words**; above that, freeze the literals *inside* the quote and let the prose compress.

# Addendum — placeholder token cost (expert review issue 5)

**Measured 2026-08-07 with `tiktoken`, offline, zero API spend.** Harness: `bench_placeholders.py`.

## 🔴 The plan's placeholder costs more than the literals it replaces

`⟦` (U+27E6) is absent from BPE vocabularies, so it fragments into byte-level tokens. Measured **in context** (delta of a sentence with the placeholder vs. the same sentence with the slot empty — this catches merges across the boundary that measuring the token alone misses):

| Format | cl100k in-context | o200k in-context |
|---|---|---|
| `⟦L00⟧` — **the plan's choice** | 5.6 | **7.6** |
| `⟦S02_PATH00⟧` — the review's suggestion | 7.6 | **9.6** |
| `[[L00]]` | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| `<L00>` | 2.6 | 3.0 |

Against the literals actually being replaced: `judge.py:572` = 4 tokens · `d256e25a` = 5 · `:7999` = 3 · `max_tokens=8192` = 5.

⇒ **On o200k, freezing `d256e25a` (5 tokens) as `⟦L00⟧` (7.6) is a net loss before compression starts.** The plan's own design table requires *"the placeholder must not cost more than the literal it replaces."* As specified, it violates that rule — and gets **worse** on newer tokenizers, the opposite of the way to bet.

## Corpus-wide

All 2,956 bodies, non-overlapping spans, o200k:

| Format | Literal tokens | Placeholder tokens | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `⟦L00⟧` | 63,699 | 92,624 | **+28,925 (+45%)** |
| `[[L00]]` | 63,699 | 46,312 | −17,387 (−27%) |
| `<L00>` | 63,699 | 34,734 | −28,965 (−45%) |

The freeze step as specified **adds ~29k tokens** to rewriter input across the corpus before a single word is compressed.

## Recommendation — and its limit

**Switch to `[[L00]]`.** It is the balance point: −27% instead of +45%, while avoiding both formats the plan rejected on behavioral grounds (`<>` reads as HTML and gets rewritten; `{}` reads as a template slot to fill).

⚠️ **`<L00>` is cheapest on cost and I am NOT recommending it.** The plan rejected angle brackets for a reason this benchmark cannot test. **Token cost is one axis; model behavior is the other, and I measured only the first.** Whether any format survives contact with a model belongs to the shadow run.

## Two honesty notes

1. **My literal count disagrees with the plan's.** I measure 3.9 non-overlapping literals per DM; §1 reports 8.8 occurrences per DM. The likely cause is that §1 counts per-type occurrences including nested/overlapping matches while this pass resolves to non-overlapping spans. **If 8.8 is the truer count, the overhead above roughly doubles.** Worth reconciling before the economics in §2 are restated.
2. **Two tokenizers, both OpenAI-family.** Flash-class and other vendor tokenizers are untested here. The direction (rare Unicode fragments, ASCII does not) is general; the exact numbers are not.

# Addendum 2 — the 3.9-vs-8.8 discrepancy, resolved

Measured both ways over 2,957 bodies:

| Counting method | Result |
|---|---|
| Sum of per-type occurrences, overlaps double-counted (**the plan's method**) | 21,636 = **7.3/DM** |
| Non-overlapping resolved spans | 11,588 = **3.9/DM** |

⇒ **§1's "8.8 literals per DM" is the overlap-inflated per-type sum, not a count of things needing a placeholder.** A sha inside a backticked span inside a quote counts three times there and once here. My 7.3 lands near their 8.8 using their method, so the methods reconcile; the residual gap is my `CONST` regex being far stricter than theirs (305 vs their 8,330).

**Consequence for the placeholder economics — I was wrong to hedge.** One placeholder is emitted per *resolved span*, so 3.9/DM is the correct basis and the +28,925-token overhead in Addendum 1 **stands as measured**. It does not double.

**The real sensitivity is elsewhere: bare integers.** 8,380 occurrences — the single largest class in the corpus, present in 58% of DMs per §1 — and **not extracted by the current tier at all**. Folding them in would raise the span count substantially and scale the placeholder overhead with it.

⇒ That makes the format choice **more** decisive, not less: the more spans we freeze, the more a +45% placeholder format costs. Choosing `[[L00]]` over `⟦L00⟧` should precede any decision to widen extraction.

**One recall gap on my side, stated plainly**: my `CONST` regex requires an underscore, so it finds 305 where the plan finds 8,330. Their number is likely closer to right. This does not affect the placeholder measurement (which prices spans, whatever produces them) but it does mean §2 of this document undercounts the CONST class.

# Addendum 3 — bare integers cannot be frozen at any price

The open question from Addendum 2, answered.

## They are load-bearing

7,099 bare integers in the corpus. I read a stride sample of 34 in context. **Almost none are incidental** — this traffic is engineering status reporting, so its integers are the payload:

> `2710 passed / 1 skipped` · `3 failed / 21669 passed / 25 skipped, 621s` · `199 stmts / 0 miss / 58 branch` · `census now 31/31` · `91 in / 91 out` · `budget 20 / content budget 17` · `timeout seconds = 600` · `pinned to a constant 180s` · `417 cosa entries` · `merge #20` · `the 14 (oldest 142.9h)`

Even the enumerators — `(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)` — are structural pointers whose renumbering silently breaks a reference. By size: 56% single-digit, 25% two-digit, 14% three-digit, 5% four-plus.

⇒ On the merits, this class belongs in HARD.

## But the placeholder always costs more than the number

A bare integer is **1.06 tokens**. Measured on o200k across all 7,099:

| Format | Placeholder tokens | Delta vs the literals |
|---|---|---|
| `⟦L00⟧` | 56,792 | **+49,266 (+655%)** |
| `[[L00]]` | 28,396 | +20,870 (+277%) |
| `<L00>` | 21,297 | +13,771 (+183%) |

**There is no format that makes this trade work.** The cheapest placeholder anyone would accept still costs roughly triple the thing it protects. This is not a tuning problem — a placeholder cannot be shorter than one token, and the literal already is one.

## ⇒ Verify, don't freeze

The freeze protocol is the wrong instrument for cheap literals. But the *goal* — knowing the number survived — does not require substitution at all:

> **Extract the multiset of bare integers from the input, and assert the same multiset appears in the output. No placeholder, no substitution, zero added tokens.**

This catches omission and mutation, which are the failure modes that matter here. It does **not** catch relocation (`3 failed / 21669 passed` → `21669 failed / 3 passed` passes a multiset check) — so pair it with the clause-confinement rule the expert review already recommends, and count a message as high-risk when its integers are dense.

⇒ **Two protection tiers, not one**: *freeze* for literals expensive enough to pay for a placeholder, *verify-in-place* for literals that are not. The plan currently assumes one mechanism covers everything, and for the corpus's single largest immutable class it cannot.

# Addendum 4 — the economic gate on extraction width

Written after Mr Radio's first build measured **89,936 placeholder tokens against 95,735 literal tokens — 5,799 ahead**, with `⟦L00⟧` at 179,508. Moving bare integers to verify-in-place is what flipped the sign.

⚠️ **5,799 on 95,735 is a 6% margin.** Extraction width is now an **economic** lever, not only a safety one. Every class added to the freeze tier spends against it.

## The rule

> **A class earns a placeholder only if its mean literal token cost exceeds the placeholder's cost.** Below that line, freezing loses tokens outright and buys nothing the verify-in-place check doesn't already give.

Measured across the corpus, o200k, against `[[L00]]` = 3 tokens:

| Verdict | Class | Spans | Mean tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Net loss** | `:PORT` | 1,772 | **2.49** |
| 🔴 | numbers-with-units | 568 | **2.28** |
| 🔴 | percentages | 285 | **2.25** |
| ⚠️ Break-even | CLI flags | 204 | 3.00 |
| ⚠️ | comma-integers | 332 | 3.04 |
| ⚠️ | §-pointers | 330 | 3.10 |
| ⚠️ | ratios | 538 | 3.22 |
| ⚠️ | snake_case identifiers | 2,663 | 3.26 |
| ✅ Pays | UUID | 7 | 24.00 |
| ✅ | email | 16 | 9.44 |
| ✅ | `file:line` with ranges | 119 | 7.85 |
| ✅ | quoted spans | 3,087 | 7.13 |
| ✅ | backticked spans | 2,666 | 6.99 |
| ✅ | `file:line` | 396 | 5.98 |
| ✅ | paths | 230 | 5.58 |
| ✅ | shas / session ids | 2,262 | 4.81 |

⇒ **Move `:PORT`, numbers-with-units and percentages to verify-in-place** with the bare integers — ~2,600 spans that currently cost more than they protect.

⇒ The break-even band earns nothing either way. Freeze them only if a specific corruption case justifies the complexity.

## On the bypass rate

Mr Radio measured 6.4% of messages bypassed and a **100% frozen** maximum, and asked whether his gate is too aggressive. **It isn't — the taxonomy is.**

A wide rebuild here (my set plus all 16 catalogued classes) gives 6.07 spans/DM against his 7.59, but:

| | This rebuild | His build |
|---|---|---|
| median frozen | 9.7% | — |
| p99 | 32.5% | — |
| **max** | **50.0%** | **100%** |
| ≥40% frozen | 9 msgs (0.3%) | 6.4% |

**A body frozen at 100% means the spans cover every character.** That is a greedy pattern, not dense text. First suspects: uncapped quoted spans, and the new spelled-out-numbers class. Fix the patterns and the bypass rate should fall to well under 1% on its own — at which point the gate needs no adjustment at all.

## Correction — CONST, and I was wrong

Addendum 2 offered to retract my stricter `CONST` regex in favour of the plan's broader one. **Mr Radio refused it and was right.** Bare all-caps in this corpus is overwhelmingly English: `NOT` 810 · `HEAD` 473 · `KISS` 449 · `AND` 231 · `PASS` 181. The underscore requirement stays; snake_case and dotted identifiers get their own class.

# Addendum 5 — glyphs are ceremony, not immutables

After retiering, the build stands at **4.83 spans/DM, max frozen ratio 45.7%, margin 20,010 under 77,205 — 26%**, up from 6%. Two pattern bugs drove most of it: §-pointers matching the next word's first letter (`gate the` → `gate t`, 1,307 junk spans — that class was mine and its greedy shape was my error), and the glyph pattern swallowing bare arrows.

## The conflict

**Glyphs were moved to verify-in-place. That fights the plan's own second goal.** §0 asks the rewriter to *"strip hedging, ceremony, and coined vocabulary."* Glyphs **are** the ceremony — so a rule requiring them to survive makes the fail-closed path fire whenever the rewriter does exactly what it was told.

Measured:

| | |
|---|---|
| Messages carrying glyphs | 1,733 / 2,967 — **58%** |
| Occurrences | 1,936 |
| **Leading / decorative** | 1,503 — **78%** |
| Inline | 433 — 22% |

Top: 🦉 574 · 🫡 487 · 🕊️ 141 · 💍 131 · 🙏🏼 89 — persona marks and salutes.

**The sender persona already travels in the envelope's `from` field.** A leading 🦉 is redundant decoration, not an immutable — freezing or verifying it protects information the message carries twice.

## The split

⇒ **A leading glyph run is declared removable boilerplate.** The expert review's issue 1 explicitly provides for this: *"every actionable proposition, unless specific boilerplate classes are declared removable."* This is that class.

⇒ **Inline glyphs are verified** — ✅ ⚠️ ✓ used mid-sentence are content markers and carry meaning.

⇒ Without the split, a high fail-closed rate will be read as a defect in the protocol when it is in fact the rewriter obeying its instructions. **That misdiagnosis is more expensive than the tokens**, because it points debugging at the validator instead of at the contract.

## Two corrections to earlier addenda

1. **Addendum 4 called the 100%-frozen body a greedy pattern. Half right.** That message was `🫡👑` — two glyphs, genuinely all-literal. §-pointers were the greedy class and never appeared at the maximum because they spread thin across many messages instead of dominating one.
2. **The bypass-rate gap was apples-to-oranges.** 0.2% of messages hit the frozen-ratio rule, matching the 0.3% measured here; the other 167 hit the 40-word compressible floor, median 31 words. **The gate stands as built** — the disagreement was never about the gate.
