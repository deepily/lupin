## 1. Why this is back on the table

The three-arm design was shelved on 2026-08-04 for a reason that no longer applies:

> *"With only 28 randomized hours, a three-arm experiment would provide just 9–10
> hour-blocks per arm — substantially less than the original plan's assumed
> approximately 20."*

**Mon–Sat at 14 hours/day = 84 slots = 28 per arm.** That clears the bar the design was
rejected against, with margin over the ~20 it originally assumed.

**But block count was only ONE of three objections.** The other two are design errors and
they are not fixed by adding days. Reviving the plan verbatim would rebuild both. This
document exists to fix them before anything is built.

## 2. The two errors that adding days does NOT fix

Quoted from the superseded doc's own banner:

> 1. *"The fixed `(hour + day) % 3` rotation makes rejecting **always** precede graded,
>    so rejection's carryover lands systematically in the comparison arm."*
> 2. *"Five dose thresholds across ~20 rejecting hours gives four observations each —
>    which contradicts this document's own §4 argument that the hour, not the message,
>    is the unit of randomization."*

### Fix for (1) — carryover confound

A modular rotation is deterministic, so every arm has a **fixed predecessor**. If being
refused in hour *N* makes a session write shorter in hour *N+1*, that spillover lands on
whichever arm always follows rejecting — and gets scored as that arm's effect.

**Proposed**: randomize assignment under a balance constraint, not a formula:
- each arm gets 28 slots;
- each arm appears at each clock hour (09–22) **twice**, so no arm owns a time of day;
- across all adjacent pairs, each arm follows each other arm **as evenly as the counts
  allow** — so carryover is spread, not concentrated;
- no arm runs more than 2 consecutive hours (the constraint the two-arm pilot already uses).

Rejection-sample against those constraints from a recorded seed, exactly as
`generate_schedule.py` already does. This is a generator change, not new machinery.

### Fix for (2) — dose thresholds

Five thresholds across 28 rejecting hours is ~5–6 blocks each. That is a dose-response
curve drawn through six points per dose, and it contradicts the design's own "the hour is
the unit of randomization" argument.

**Proposed**: **one threshold, 150 words** — the same value the two-arm pilot ran, so this
week and next are directly comparable. Dose-response is a separate, later experiment that
needs its own power calculation. Trying to answer two questions at once is what produced
the objection.

## 3. What this week's data changes about the question

This is the part that makes the three-arm run *more* interesting than it was on 08-03, not
less.

The two-arm pilot's read as of Wednesday midpoint (commit `758ad84e`):

| | Tue 08-04 | Wed 08-05 |
|---|---|---|
| Observed first-try reduction | 26.8% | 23.8% |
| Break-even required | 22.4% | **30.8%** |
| Rewrite spend (est-tokens) | 16.1k | **46.4k** |
| Optimistic bound | +3,147 saved | **−10,558 lost** |

**Behaviour moved and the economics still lost.** The rejecting arm produces real restraint
(157.4 → 126.3 words per attempt; 51.7% → 27.0% of messages over 150 words) but the rewrite
loop costs more than the shorter first tries repay.

That makes `graded` — *tell the sender it was too long, deliver it anyway* — the arm most
worth measuring. **It has no rewrite loop, so it has no rewrite spend.** If graded captures
even part of rejecting's restraint, it wins outright on cost, because its cost is ~zero.

So the Monday question is sharper than "do the three arms differ":

> **Does telling a session it was verbose buy restraint without paying for a rewrite?**

- graded ≈ rejecting on restraint → **ship graded**, the refusal loop is pure overhead.
- graded ≈ blind, rejecting below both → restraint requires the refusal, and then the
  cost verdict from this week decides whether it is worth buying.
- all three alike → the dose is too weak to matter at fleet volume; stop working this.

Every one of those three outcomes is actionable. That is the test of a design worth running.

## 4. Fresh tag, not pooled

**Run it as `three-arm-v1`, a separate experiment, not pooled with `two-arm-v1`.**

- different arm set and a different question;
- this week's corpus carries the mid-flight extension caveat (see
  `2026.08.06-dm-pilot-schedule-extension.md` §4) and pooling would spread that caveat onto
  clean data;
- the labelling scheme in the superseded doc §5 already keeps the vocabularies disjoint, so
  the classifier stays a lookup with no timestamp arithmetic.

Carry forward intact from the superseded doc: §1 baseline findings, §5 labelling, §6
**log-the-attempt-not-the-send** (survivorship bias — the corpus writer must sit *before*
the length check), §10 limitations.

## 5. ⚠️ The precondition nobody has priced: the fleet has to be talking

The pilot measures DMs between working sessions. Volume tracks **headcount**, not open slots:

| Day | rows | distinct senders |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-02 | 112 | 6 |
| 2026-08-03 | 734 | 11 |
| 2026-08-04 | 962 | 13 |
| 2026-08-05 | 782 | 10 |
| **2026-08-06** | **71** | 8 |

The crew was reaped Wednesday afternoon and volume fell 782 → 71 the next day. **An armed
schedule over a quiet fleet collects nothing.**

At ~950 rows/day with a full crew, a Mon–Sat run yields roughly 5–6k rows — comfortable for
28 blocks/arm. At Thursday's rate it yields a few hundred, and the week produces another
underpowered result.

**So the three-arm week is not really a scheduling decision; it is a staffing decision.**
Whatever the crew is working on Mon–Sat has to be real work — synthetic DM filler would
measure the generator, not the fleet, and would poison the corpus the same way a side-door
submission poisons a scheduled test run.

## 6. Two questions for Rick

**Q1 — Is `graded` still the right third arm?**
My recommendation: **yes, and it is now the most interesting one**, for the §3 reason — it
is the only arm that could deliver restraint at zero rewrite cost, which is exactly where
this week's result says the problem is.

**Q2 — What does "burn all available tokens" mean operationally?**
My recommendation: **a fixed 84-slot schedule with the budget as a stop-EARLY condition, not
a stop-late one.** Declare the end in advance; if budget runs out first, the run ends early
and reports what it got. The alternative — run until the budget is gone — makes the stopping
rule depend on spend, which is one more data-dependent stopping rule of exactly the kind
this week already has to caveat.
