# Critical Review: DM Verbosity Three-Arm Pilot Experiment

## Overall assessment

This is a strong pilot skeleton, particularly the attempt-level logging, explicit experiment labels, blind instrumentation, and safety exemption. However, the current plan overstates what the data could establish. With a few focused changes, it would be defensible as a directional pilot—not yet as evidence of dose response, causal carryover, or a “half-life” of learning.

## Changes to make now

### 1. Start all three treatments simultaneously

The schedule currently begins after implementation items 1–3, before rejection and client handling are implemented. That would label some hours `rejecting` even though messages were not actually rejected.

Introduce a single `go_live_at` and do not include rows before that point in the experiment. Earlier rows can be labeled `shadow` or `instrumentation-only`. Deploy the rejection branch and client error handling together.

### 2. Replace the fixed G → B → R rotation with a randomized, balanced schedule

During almost every day:

- graded is followed by blind;
- blind is followed by rejecting;
- rejecting is followed by graded.

Consequently, lingering effects of rejection will systematically appear in graded hours. That directly confounds the comparison the experiment is intended to make.

Generate the schedule in advance using randomized three-hour permutations, constrained so that:

- each three-hour block contains every arm;
- each arm occupies each clock position approximately equally;
- predecessor transitions are balanced;
- the schedule and seed are saved and versioned.

This can remain stateless at runtime. “Nothing stored” is an engineering convenience, not an experimental advantage; an auditable schedule is preferable.

### 3. Use one threshold—150 words—for the entire pilot

Five thresholds would leave only roughly four rejecting-hour observations per threshold. That cannot support a credible dose-response curve.

The 150-word threshold looks reasonable for establishing that treatment was actually administered without overwhelming operations. Keep it fixed and undisclosed. Inspect bunching just below 150, but defer the ladder until next week.

### 4. Change the primary outcome

The intervention affects approximately the longest 15% of messages, so the fleet median could remain almost unchanged even if the intervention works well.

Recommended specification:

- **Primary contrast:** rejecting versus blind.
- **Primary outcome:** hour-level mean excess words per eligible attempt, `max(words − 60, 0)`.
- **Secondary contrast:** graded versus blind.
- **Secondary outcomes:** percentage at or above 150, mean log word count, attempted messages per delivered message, and time to the next successful delivery.

Keep median words as a descriptive robustness measure, not the sole primary outcome.

Analyze the entire assigned hour as intention-to-treat. Selecting only messages after a refusal would condition on treatment uptake and writer behavior.

### 5. Drop causal language from the exposed/unexposed analyses

The proposed difference-in-differences does not solve the selection problem. “Exposed” sessions are selected because they produced an extreme message; subsequent shortening may reflect regression to the mean, workload changes, or retry behavior. The proposed A and C measurements are also not clean pretreatment observations.

For this pilot:

- compare randomized arms for the main result;
- treat refusal-aligned event studies as descriptive;
- do not report a causal “half-life”;
- treat re-spin comparisons as anecdotes, not a natural experiment;
- use the contemporaneous blind arm—not the historical baseline—as the main counterfactual.

The claim that within-hour analyses retain message-level power should also be removed. Messages remain correlated within session, and retries are especially dependent.

### 6. Separate gate outcome from actual delivery outcome

A row written before delivery cannot truthfully say `accepted`. It only knows that it passed the length gate.

Recommended fields include:

- `scheduled_arm`
- `effective_arm`
- `schedule_version`
- `assignment_hour_utc`
- `eligible_for_rejection`
- `exemption_reason`
- `length_gate`: `passed | rejected | exempt`
- `delivery_outcome`: `delivered | failed | not_attempted`
- `threshold`
- `word_count_version`
- `override_reason`

This prevents downstream delivery failures from being counted as accepted messages and makes overrides auditable.

### 7. Predeclare operational stopping rules

Keep the coordination exemption, but log every exemption. Also monitor:

- rejection rate;
- repeated rejection loops;
- attempts per successful delivery;
- delivery delay;
- critical-message failures.

Define in advance when the experiment will be pinned to blind or graded. Otherwise, a manual shutdown becomes an undocumented, outcome-dependent intervention.

## What the pilot can defensibly report

A suitable conclusion would look like:

> During randomized policy hours, attempted verbosity was X lower under rejection than under blind handling, with Y rejecting hours and Z active sessions. The result is directional and includes carryover and incomplete treatment uptake.

Avoid claiming that:

- a particular threshold is optimal;
- refusal caused exposed sessions to learn;
- learning lasted a specific number of messages;
- no carryover was found;
- context clearing can only bias results toward zero.

The final bias claim is too strong: context clearing may correlate with refusals, workload, or session behavior, so its effect is not guaranteed to be simple attenuation.

## Improvements for next week

First decide whether the target is a **fleet policy effect** or an **individual learning mechanism**. The current plan tries to answer both.

### Fleet policy effect

Run a longer balanced crossover with:

- one- or two-hour randomized blocks;
- transition-balanced sequences;
- a full week of observation;
- a fixed 150-word threshold for the replication;
- current and previous arm included in the analysis;
- session and time-block dependence accounted for;
- randomization-based inference or a hierarchical model rather than ordinary message-level tests.

### Individual learning mechanism

A stronger design would randomize eligible over-threshold events between:

- warning plus delivery;
- the same warning plus rejection.

Then measure the next one, three, and five messages. Matching the wording isolates the incremental effect of refusing delivery rather than conflating enforcement with feedback. This is a sequential randomized experiment and should still account for repeated observations within sessions.

### Additional instrumentation

Add:

- explicit rejection-to-retry linkage;
- context-clear markers;
- message sequence numbers;
- persona or seat and recipient type;
- task or workload category where available;
- latency and retry cost;
- empirical exposure estimates by session rather than Poisson approximations based on the average seat.

Only after the 150-word replication should dose response be added. Use two or three randomized thresholds, not five, and ensure each threshold receives enough rejecting blocks.

## Bottom line

For the immediate pilot, fix deployment timing, randomize the schedule, freeze the threshold, replace the median primary outcome, and demote the subgroup and carryover claims. With those changes, the pilot can provide useful and honest directional evidence that informs a stronger experiment next week.
