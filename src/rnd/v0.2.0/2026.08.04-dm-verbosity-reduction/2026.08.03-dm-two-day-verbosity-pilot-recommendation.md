# Two-Day DM Verbosity Pilot Recommendation

## Experiment window

The experiment has two 14-hour workdays, for a total of **28 randomized hour-blocks**:

- Tuesday, August 4, 2026: 09:00–23:00 America/New_York
- Wednesday, August 5, 2026: 09:00–23:00 America/New_York

The end time should be treated as an exact, predeclared boundary rather than “approximately 11.” If 23:00 cannot be guaranteed, choose an earlier time that can be maintained on both days.

Only these active intervals should appear in the serialized schedule. Offline overnight hours should not advance the experimental rotation.

## Recommended pilot: two arms

With only 28 randomized hours, a three-arm experiment would provide just 9–10 hour-blocks per arm—substantially less than the original plan’s assumed approximately 20.

For this pilot, use:

- **Blind**
- **Rejecting at 150 words**

Defer graded versus blind until the larger experiment next week. Rejection versus blind is the more consequential and operationally informative comparison.

This produces 14 hours per arm and permits a clean matched-clock design.

## Randomization mechanism

Generate Tuesday as a constrained random sequence containing seven blind and seven rejecting assignments. Limit consecutive runs of the same treatment to no more than two hours.

Wednesday receives the opposite treatment at every clock hour:

```python
# Tuesday: constrained random order containing 7 of each arm.
tuesday = randomize_with_max_run(
    ["blind"] * 7 + ["rejecting"] * 7,
    max_run=2,
)

# Wednesday: opposite treatment at every corresponding clock hour.
wednesday = [
    "rejecting" if arm == "blind" else "blind"
    for arm in tuesday
]
```

The Tuesday order should be generated from a recorded random seed rather than chosen manually.

This design provides:

- exactly 14 hours per arm;
- exactly seven hours per arm on each day;
- blind and rejecting both observed at every clock hour;
- balanced transition directions across the two days;
- no treatment runs longer than two hours.

An illustrative schedule fragment is:

| Time | Tuesday | Wednesday |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | blind | rejecting |
| 10:00 | rejecting | blind |
| 11:00 | rejecting | blind |
| 12:00 | blind | rejecting |

## Schedule integration

Generate the complete schedule before deployment and serialize the 28 explicit hourly slots to an immutable JSON file. The runtime service should load the file once during application startup.

A read-only experiment-policy object should resolve the assignment once at the beginning of each send request. The same assignment should control instrumentation, rejection or delivery, internal logging, and the caller-visible response.

The server that actually decides whether a DM is delivered should be authoritative. MCP clients or other adapters should forward the server’s response rather than independently calculating the active arm.

Outside the listed active intervals, `assignment_at()` should return `None`, meaning the experiment is inactive and ordinary nonexperimental behavior applies.

Every attempt should record at least:

- `schedule_id`
- `slot_id`
- `scheduled_arm`
- `effective_arm`
- `assigned_at_utc`
- `reject_threshold`
- `eligible_for_rejection`
- `length_gate`
- `delivery_outcome`
- `override_reason`, if applicable

Resolve the assignment using a single request-arrival timestamp. Do not reread the clock later in the operation, because a send may cross an hour boundary.

## Analysis

Treat each clock hour as a matched pair:

- Tuesday 09:00 versus Wednesday 09:00;
- Tuesday 10:00 versus Wednesday 10:00;
- and so forth.

The recommended primary outcome is hour-level mean excess words per eligible attempt:

```text
mean(max(words − 60, 0))
```

Report:

- rejecting-minus-blind difference across the 14 matched clock hours;
- an uncertainty or randomization interval;
- proportion of attempts at or above 150 words;
- attempted messages per successful delivery;
- delivery delay;
- repeated-rejection loops.

The effective experimental evidence is **14 matched hour pairs**, not the thousands of individual messages. Individual messages remain correlated within sessions and hours.

## If three arms are non-negotiable

A constrained three-arm schedule can allocate 10, 9, and 9 hours, with every clock hour receiving different arms across the two days. However:

- no clock hour can observe all three conditions;
- each arm receives only 9–10 randomized hours;
- carryover and dose-response analyses will be especially weak;
- the results should be presented strictly as feasibility and directional evidence.

## Bottom line

Under this two-day time constraint, the two-arm matched design is materially more defensible and more likely to produce a useful directional signal. Run blind versus rejecting now, hold the rejection threshold at 150 words, and restore the graded arm in next week’s larger experiment.
