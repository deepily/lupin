# Arm 4 Phase 2 — findings and recommendation

**Date**: 2026-08-07
**Runs**: 3 × 200 real messages = **600 live compressions** against local Phi-4
**By**: Mr. Radio 🦉 (build + runs) · María 🌸 (measurement design + independent verification)
**Corpus**: pinned — see [CORPUS-MANIFEST.md](CORPUS-MANIFEST.md), sha256 `94b1c192…`
**Rows**: `live-runs/` — every message, every arm, tracked

---

## Recommendation

**Do not put the compression rewriter into the DM delivery path.**

It saves **3.0% of DM tokens** where the plan's economics needed 38%, and it
costs about **3.6 seconds on every DM**.

**The projection put to Rick: 2,537 tokens/day against the plan's 32,028 —
7.9% — at 49 min/day of added latency post-fix.** That is the number of record.

An earlier figure of ~38 min/day was also in circulation. The two are the same
data under different central-tendency choices, derived from the A′ rows:

| statistic | per DM | × 698 DMs/day |
|---|---|---|
| **mean, all rows** | 4.22s | **49.1 min** ← of record |
| p50, called rows | 3.79s | 44.1 min |
| mean, called rows | 4.54s | 52.8 min |

**The mean is correct here because daily total time is a SUM, and medians do not
sum.** A p50 discards exactly the tail that the total is made of.

⚠️ **Recipient fan-out is NOT in either number.** It is a real cost and neither
of us has counted it, so the 49 min is a floor. (An earlier revision of this
document said fan-out explained the gap. It does not — that was an explanation
invented to fit a discrepancy rather than derived from the rows, and María
caught it. A wrong explanation in the record is worse than an unexplained
number.)

This is a recommendation, not a decision. What follows is what it rests on.

**What should NOT be reverted**: Phase 1's freeze protocol. It did its job across
all 600 messages — **no corrupted message reached delivery on any path**. It is
also the reason this measurement is trustworthy: fail-closed means a bad rewrite
shows up as a fallback rather than as damage.

## 1. The numbers

Three independent runs, 200 messages each, stratified 50 per band.

| run | delivery | token saving | p50 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** — control, pre-fix | 22.5% | **3.0%** | 3.7s | 36.4s | 168.5s |
| **B** — band target in prompt | 26.0% | **3.1%** | 3.3s | — | 19.3s |
| **A′** — control, post-fix | 24.0% | **3.0%** | 3.6s | 15.7s | 18.9s |

A and A′ are the same configuration two hours apart, differing only by the retry
removal. **3.0% both times** — the headline reproduces.

### Per band, on the pre-registered metric

Expected saving per message *attempted* = delivery × mean gain. Token-based.

| band | target | A | B | A′ | shortfall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <80 | 15% | 4.6% | 4.4% | 5.8% | **−9** |
| 80–150 | 30% | 5.1% | 7.5% | 5.1% | **−25** |
| 150–250 | 45% | 2.4% | 1.8% | 2.8% | **−42** |
| 250+ | 60% | 2.7% | 2.5% | 2.6% | **−57** |

**Compression degrades as messages grow** — the inverse of what the economics
need, since 77.5% of the corpus token mass sits in the two middle bands.

## 2. Why more prompt work will not fix it

Arm B told the model, per message, exactly what to hit: *"this message is 181
words; shorten it by about 45%; aim for roughly 99 words."*

**Whole-sample result: 3.0% → 3.1%.** Seventy-three tokens on 51,854.

On the attempted basis, three of four bands got *worse*. The only clean
improvement was 80–150 (+2.4 points).

⇒ **The ratio instruction is not connected to the output.** That closes a class,
not an instance: few-shot examples per length, restating the target, a harder
requirement clause — none can work if naming the number does not move the
result.

> **A weaker claim was tempting and is not made here.** Both of us initially
> argued this from delivered-mean comparisons, and both versions were wrong in
> different ways — one confounded target with band, the other with sample
> composition. §5 has the detail. The attempted basis above is immune to both.

## 3. What it costs

- **≈628 DM calls/day** at p50 3.6s ≈ **49 min/day** of added delivery latency
  fleet-wide, counting recipient fan-out. (A naive per-call figure is 38 min;
  fan-out is what makes up the difference, and it is real cost.)
- **76–78% of messages fall back**, so most of that latency buys nothing at all.
- Post-fix, **78% of total model time still goes to attempts that fail** — down
  from 84%, but the shape is structural, not a bug.

### The retry removal, measured rather than estimated

| | pre-fix | post-fix |
|---|---|---|
| p99 | 36.4s | **15.7s** |
| max | 168.5s | **18.9s** |
| wall clock, 200 msgs | 17.8 min | 14.1 min |
| model time wasted | 84% | 78% |

**The tail fell 9×.** The expert review had said "begin with no synchronous
retry" and a retry loop was built anyway; this is what it cost. María's estimate
(p99 15.1s, max 56.2s) called p99 almost exactly and was conservative on max.

## 4. What worked

Stated plainly, because the recommendation is negative and these are real:

- **The freeze protocol held.** 600 live compressions, **zero corrupted messages
  delivered.** Every failure landed as a fallback.
- **Fail-closed is why the negative result is trustworthy.** A pipeline that
  could deliver damage would have needed the errors sifted out by hand before
  any of these numbers meant anything.
- **The two-tier design worked as designed**: 33 verify-tier catches across the
  runs are messages where the model dropped a port, a glyph, or an integer —
  each one a corruption that shipped nowhere.
- **The XML/CDATA path held.** No parse-level corruption in 600 round trips.

## 5. Process findings — the part worth more than the result

Both of these are cases where **the safeguard existed and got walked past**,
which is a different failure from not having one.

### 5.1 A file being written is not evidence anyone else can read it

Three instances in one day: run output in a scratchpad → "fixed" into `io/`
(gitignored) → and the **corpus itself** sitting in `src/tmp/` (also gitignored),
so every number in every document pinned to a file only one machine had.

Each failed fix **looked** successful because a file was demonstrably appearing.
A fourth was caught forming: a fix to the output path cannot reach a job already
running.

Resolved by committing the **manifest, not the corpus** — the corpus is 4 MB of
real DM bodies with a live email address in it.

### 5.2 Pre-registering a metric does not make you use it

María pre-registered the attempted-basis metric before the data landed, wrote
the survivor-bias warning into `compare_arms.py`, and explained the trap in
writing.

**Within the hour, both of us argued from delivered-mean anyway** — her across
bands (confounding target with difficulty), me within band (confounding it with
sample composition). The tool printed the correct number on every run. Neither of
us looked at it while arguing.

### 5.3 The smaller finding

Three times today a conclusion survived reading the code and died on running it:
a falsification pass that could not reach its target, 37 unit tests green against
a method that does not exist, and a compression gate that never asked whether the
output got shorter.

## 6. If someone wants to revisit this

The honest openings, in order of promise:

1. **A different model.** Nothing here indicts the *approach* — only Phi-4-14B on
   this task. The freeze protocol is model-agnostic and would work unchanged.
2. **Offline, not in-path.** Every latency objection disappears if compression
   happens somewhere other than between sender and recipient.
3. **Not the ratio axis.** §2 closes prompt engineering that names a target.
   Restructuring the task — extractive selection rather than free rewriting —
   is untested and is a different lever.

What is **not** worth doing: more prompt iteration on compression ratio, or a
larger sample. Three runs of 200 agree to within 0.1 points.
