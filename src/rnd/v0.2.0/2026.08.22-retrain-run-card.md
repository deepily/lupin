## The run — three commands, in this order

```bash
./run-agentic-intent-training.sh validate
./run-agentic-intent-training.sh test  --llm <model> --quantize-bits <bits>
./run-agentic-intent-training.sh full  --llm <model> --quantize-bits <bits>
```

- **`validate`** parses all three `.jsonl` files, checks the five required fields (`command`, `instruction`, `input`, `output`, `prompt`), and prints the example count per file. Cheap confirmation you are pointed at the right dataset.
- **`test`** runs the whole pipeline on a 1% sample, 5–10 minutes. Ten minutes here beats discovering a broken path at hour three of four.
- **`full`** is the real run, 3–4 hours.

Neither `validate` nor `test` nor `full` regenerates the dataset, so none of them can clobber the good data.

## Rick's two rulings, recorded

1. **Geometry stays with math.** The router was observed sending "What is the area of a circle with a radius of 6?" to the *calculator* — a leak running opposite to the arithmetic one. Roughly 20 sibling geometry lines were never sampled. Relabelling them off one observation an hour before a run was rejected; the finding survives as a question for after the run.
2. **Class imbalance ships as-is, recorded.** `automatic` at 192 against calculator's 552. Pre-existing — the relabel moved utterances only between math and calculator. Changing the sampling strategy an hour before a run would make this result incomparable to previous runs. Note that commit `24f8d88f` on this same corpus reads *"two starved classes fixed"*, so starved classes are a known failure mode here and this is a live risk accepted knowingly.

## A false alarm, and why it was false

Two of us independently reported that moved utterances were missing from the regenerated dataset — first "1 of 44 absent", then "35 of 44 absent". **Both were wrong, and by the same mechanism.**

The check was: grep the corpus utterance text against the generated `.jsonl`. The control that killed it: 30 utterances that were **never moved** scored 9 of 30 present — the same rate as the moved set. So the absence is a property of substring-matching against the generator's escaping and augmentation, not of the data.

Tiffany independently reached the same conclusion from the generator's side: it appends one row per corpus line per augmentation pass, so coverage is **44 of 44 by construction**, and no per-utterance grep is needed or meaningful.

**Do not act on any missing-utterance count for this dataset.** What stands is the label read-back above.
