# Notification length before and after the DM tutor — the tail moved, the middle did not

**Date**: 2026-08-18
**Branch**: `wip-v0.2.0-2026.08.03-present-and-demo`
**Data**: the live `notifications` table (317,509 rows at time of measurement)
**Cutover**: 2026-08-13 14:14 EDT — first DM-tutor firings, ship-fleet-wide sha `b8d10bd3`
**Queries**: `src/rnd/v0.2.0/notif_len.py`, `notif_pop.py`, `notif_tmpl.py`, `notif_ts.py`, `notif_clean.py`, `notif_dd.py` (the last one produced the headline table)

---

## 1. The question

Rick's hypothesis: the DM tutor taught Claude what a shorter DM looks like, and Claude generalized
that lesson to the **spoken notifications** it sends him — a channel the tutor never touches.

The tutor rewrites DMs only. Notifications go out through a different path. If notification length
moved at the tutor's cutover, that is spillover: a behavior change carried by the model rather than
by the code.

This is a natural experiment against production data. Nothing was manipulated for the measurement.

---

## 2. The headline

Equal 8-day windows either side of the cutover, **authored notifications only** (population defined
in §3):

| metric | before | after | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| median chars | 272 | 287 | **+6%** |
| p90 chars | 1009 | 738 | **−27%** |
| mean chars | 380 | 345 | −9% |
| n | 1,914 | 4,923 | |

**The distribution did not shift down. Its right tail was cut.**

Long notifications got about 27% shorter. The typical notification did not shrink — it grew
slightly. The mean fell only because the tail fell; the mean is being dragged, it is not reporting
a change in the middle of the distribution.

Rick's sense is half right, and it is right about the half he would notice. A 1,000-character
notification read aloud is the one that registers as too long; a 272-character one is not what
anybody complains about. The change removed the notifications that were experienced as bloated
while leaving the ordinary ones alone.

### Replicated independently per project

The same shape appears in both projects with enough volume to cut separately:

| project | p90 before → after | p50 before → after |
|---|---|---|
| lupin | 1109 → 802 | 264 → 290 |
| plan | 905 → 554 | 271 → 282 |

Both: tail down hard, median flat-to-slightly-up. The per-project replication is what makes the
tail result stateable at all — see §6 on daily variance.

## 3. The measurement problem was bigger than the effect

This is the reusable part of this document. The effect is a 27% tail move. The errors that had to
be cleared before that number could be read were larger than the effect itself, and each one
produced a confident-looking wrong answer.

**First cut — 416 rows out of 317,509.**
The filter was `direction='ai_to_human' AND sender_persona IS NOT NULL`. It looked principled: a
persona means a real session composed it. It returned 416 rows total, across the whole table.
Reason: `sender_persona` is populated almost only on DMs. Filtering on it selects the one channel
this study is explicitly *not* about, and discards the channel it is about.

**Second cut — a median of exactly 75 chars on both sides.**
Identical to the digit on both sides of the cutover. That is the tell. A metric that does not move
at all, on a table where everything else moves, is usually measuring something that is not being
generated by the process under study.

It was not. The population was dominated by hook-emitted tool pings — machine strings, not composed
speech:

| message | count |
|---|---:|
| `Done: mcp__cosa-voice___dm_send_fn` | 10,038 |
| `Done: mcp__cosa-voice__notify` | 4,315 |
| `Owed status unknown.` | 3,507 |

In total **52,612 of 75,673 rows** in that cut were of this kind. Machine strings are constant
length by construction, they are emitted at a rate that tracks activity rather than authorship, and
they swamp every statistic computed over the mixed population. Any before/after comparison run on
that mixture is measuring how often hooks fired, not how long Claude writes.

**Final population.** A row counts as an authored notification if:

```
direction = 'ai_to_human'
AND sender_id LIKE 'claude.code@%'
AND type IN ('progress','task','alert','custom')
AND message NOT LIKE 'Done: %'
AND length(message) > 15
AND the exact message text occurs <= 2 times table-wide
```

The last clause is the load-bearing one. **A string that recurs is a template, not authorship.**
Everything else on that list is a named exclusion — it removes the machine strings somebody already
thought to name. The recurrence clause removes the ones nobody thought of, including the ones that
had not been written yet when the filter was designed. It is the only part of the filter that
generalizes.

The threshold of 2 rather than 1 is deliberate: a genuinely authored sentence occasionally repeats
once by coincidence, and dropping exact-duplicate pairs would bias against short messages, which
collide more often.

## 4. Rival explanations, ruled out with receipts

Two mechanisms could produce a length drop at roughly this time without any model behavior change.
Both are ruled out by evidence, not by argument.

**The 500-character spoken cap.** `src/conf/lupin-app.ini` line 1073 sets
`cosa voice spoken char cap = 500`. Line 1078 sets
`cosa voice enforce spoken char cap = False`. The cap is configured and not enforced. Direct
confirmation from the data: **808 post-cutover rows exceed 500 characters**. If clipping were
producing the tail cut, those rows could not exist. Nothing is being clipped.

**The turn rider's "≤3 sentences AND ≤60 words" instruction.** This text is a real length pressure
on composed output. But it landed **2026-08-03**, ten days before the cutover. It sits entirely
inside the BEFORE window. A constant that was already in force throughout the before period cannot
produce a step at 08-13. If anything, its presence in the before window makes the tail cut harder
to produce, not easier.

---

## 5. What this document refuses to claim

**Causation is not established.**

The tutor shipped fleet-wide in a single cutover. There is no untreated control group — every
session that could have been measured received the treatment on the same day, at the same sha. That
makes this an **uncontrolled before/after**, and an uncontrolled before/after cannot separate the
treatment from anything else that changed that day.

The spillover story fits the data. So would any other fleet-wide change on 2026-08-13. The data
cannot distinguish between them, and this document does not pretend it can.

A difference-in-differences design would settle it: find a project the tutor never touched, measure
the same windows there, and check whether the tail moved in the treated projects *relative to* the
untreated one. **I looked for such a project and did not find one.** Until one exists, the honest
statement is:

> Notification length changed shape at the tutor cutover, in the direction the spillover hypothesis
> predicts. The tutor is a candidate cause. It is not a demonstrated one.

**Daily variance is large.** Daily p50 ranges from **106 to 431 characters** across the study
window — a spread several times the size of the before/after difference in the median. Daily p50
tracks session count, and session count moved from **2 to 81** over the same period. A composition
shift of that size can move aggregate statistics on its own, with no per-session behavior change at
all.

That is why the equal-window design and the per-project replication are not decoration. The equal
windows keep the two sides from being weighted by different amounts of activity, and the per-project
cut shows the tail move surviving inside each project separately rather than appearing only in a
pooled average across projects whose mix changed. **They are the reason the tail result is
stateable at all** — and they still do not make it causal.

## 6. Reproducing it

The scripts are in `src/rnd/v0.2.0/`:

| script | what it does |
|---|---|
| `notif_pop.py` | population sizing — how many rows survive each filter clause |
| `notif_tmpl.py` | template census — the recurring-message frequency table in §3 |
| `notif_clean.py` | the authored-notification filter in its final form |
| `notif_len.py` | length distribution, pooled |
| `notif_ts.py` | daily time series — the variance and session-count figures in §5 |
| `notif_dd.py` | **the headline table** — equal windows, pooled and per project |

They read the live database read-only through `cosa.rest.db.database.get_database_url()`. Re-running
one to verify a number is fine. Re-designing the analysis is a different piece of work than this
document.
