**The headline rests almost entirely on the single most style-sensitive detector class, and does not
survive removing it.**

| arm | blocked | name | path | number | **name ALONE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| phi_4 | 73 | 50 | 18 | 8 | 47 (**64.4%**) |

**Sensitivity check — drop name-only blocks, re-run the same test:**

```
b (flash_lite only) = 15   c (phi_4 only) = 10   b + c = 25   p = 0.4244
```

**p moves from 2.7e-07 to 0.42.** On the unambiguous classes — numbers, hex ids, paths — **the arms
are statistically indistinguishable.** The whole established direction comes from capitalised-token
novelty: the class the guard's own docstring flags as style-driven, and the one that cannot tell an
invented name from a paraphrase that happens to capitalise.

**This does not invalidate the result.** The guard is what ships, so a rewrite blocked on a name still
never reaches its recipient and the operational number stands. What it forbids is the reading a reader
will otherwise take — that Flash-Lite *invents more facts*. On the classes where invention is
unambiguous, we measured nothing.

⇒ **The headline must carry the split**: *"flagged 1.8× more often by the production guard, 76% of it
on capitalised-token novelty; excluding that class the arms are indistinguishable (p = 0.42)."*
