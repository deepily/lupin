## THE SURVIVING ORPHANS — defect five, and it is a new shape

Sixteen processes were resident from `test_the_kill_LEAVES_NO_SURVIVORS`: three hog processes
(2.1 GB each) plus thirteen chain processes — three complete chains at depths 4, 6 and 6. The
test asserts no survivors and **passes**.

**Every chain ROOT had PPID 1281 = `systemd --user`. They had been REPARENTED**, and their PGID
still pointed at a long-dead pytest group.

⇒ `_jstest_descendants` finds processes **by parentage**, and an orphan has no parent to be found
by. This is NOT the tree-reach bug that `0dc4cb05` closed, and deepening the fixture past depth 6
would not have caught it: **the link was already cut before enumeration began.** Depth was never
the variable.

**The fix, in the order that matters:**

```bash
setsid "$@" &          # 1. the TARGET must OWN a process group…
target_pgid=$!         #    …before killing a group is a safe thing to do
kill -9 -"$target_pgid" # 2. THEN kill that group — never the ambient one
```

🔴 **Half of this is not a smaller fix, it is a self-kill.** Measured: today the backgrounded
child inherits the **watchdog shell's own PGID**, so `kill -9 -PGID` without the `setsid` first
takes down the watchdog and its caller.

⚠️ **And say what a group kill still cannot do**: a child can call `setsid()` itself and leave the
group exactly as it can leave the tree. Group membership is **better evidence than parentage, not
proof**. Belt and braces is group-kill ∪ descendant-walk.

⚠️ **The test needs a negative too.** "No survivors" passes just as well when *everything* died,
including the watchdog that was supposed to do the killing. It must also assert that the watchdog
and its caller are **still alive** afterwards.

All sixteen were killed by **explicit named PID, deepest-first** — never a pattern (row
`cd332d2b`). `ps` confirms none remain.
