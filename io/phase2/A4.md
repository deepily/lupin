**Note on the count (B7).** Legacy's total and the mux's pending differ by exactly the active item. The mux already computes the total for its section chip (`TtsChromeRenderer.ts:232-234`), so the body header is reading the wrong one of two values it already has.

**Note on B1 and the exact predicate (read before building it).** María's ruling is "hide while empty, reveal on arrival". Legacy's runtime predicate is one term wider — `showSection = hasActiveOrQueued || toolbarWantsVisible` (`js:22521`) — so an *empty* queue whose toolbar button is `active` still shows the section with its `🔇 Nothing in the queue` panel. The cold-load invisibility comes from that predicate never being evaluated, not from the predicate itself. Build the predicate, and the ruling follows from it on a cold load. This is also the other half of **A0 B4b**: a toolbar toggle of this section re-runs `updateTTSQueueSection()` (`js:15354-15357`) precisely so the empty panel appears and disappears with the toggle — that row is now closed.

**A4 build items**: (1) match the visibility rule — visible iff an item exists or the toolbar button is active, evaluated on load, on every queue mutation and on a toolbar toggle (María's ruling; closes A0 B4b); (2) reload persistence for the queue and the focus-mode fields, with the paused-state reset and the stale-focus auto-exit; (3) the body header must count active + pending, and the section-bar title must follow the Paused / focus states; (4) separate Pause and Play buttons, and a manual pause that blocks queue advance; (5) the stale-focus auto-exit (rides 2); (6) defer an action-required activation while TTS is playing (shared with A3 R3). Cosmetic, not owed but worth a build decision: the toolbar title ("TTS Queue" vs "TTS Audio") and the clear-button label.
